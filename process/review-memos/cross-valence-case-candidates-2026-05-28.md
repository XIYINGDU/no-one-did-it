## CANDIDATE 1 — The al-Aulaqi OLC memo (Obama) — RECOMMENDED, A-grade

### (1) One-paragraph factual summary

In 2010, on the public record, the Obama administration placed Anwar al-Aulaqi, a US citizen and (per the government) an operational leader of al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula, on a list approved for lethal targeting (the targeting decision is documented; the specific approval chain is reconstructed from the released memo, official statements, and reporting — Stephen to confirm which links are primary-sourced). The legal basis was a 41-page memorandum dated July 16, 2010, authored by David Barron, then Acting Assistant Attorney General heading the DOJ Office of Legal Counsel (OLC). The Barron memo concluded that the 2001 Authorization for Use of Military Force (AUMF) supplied statutory authority to use lethal force against al-Aulaqi notwithstanding his citizenship; that the federal foreign-murder statute (18 U.S.C. § 1119) incorporated a "public-authority justification" that rendered the killing lawful conduct of war rather than murder; and that, under a *Mathews v. Eldridge* balancing analysis, the Fifth Amendment's Due Process Clause posed no bar where capture was infeasible and the target posed a "continued and imminent threat." Before the strike, al-Aulaqi's father sued to enjoin the targeting (*Al-Aulaqi v. Obama*, D.D.C.); the suit was dismissed December 7, 2010 on standing and political-question grounds without reaching the merits. Al-Aulaqi was killed in a US drone strike in Yemen on September 30, 2011; his 16-year-old US-citizen son Abdulrahman and US citizen Samir Khan were killed in subsequent strikes. A later damages suit (*Al-Aulaqi v. Panetta*) was also dismissed. The Barron memo itself was withheld for years; the Second Circuit ordered a redacted version released in 2014 (*New York Times Co. v. DOJ*), and the OLC posted it.

### (2) Laundering mechanism

This is the cleanest available **system/object alibi via legal opinion** — structurally identical to the Bybee/Yoo OLC torture memos the book already uses in ch-2 (slot 5) and ch-12 (beat 7 failure-column). The move: a discretionary executive decision to kill a citizen without trial is converted, by an OLC opinion, into a *legal conclusion* — "the law authorizes this." The opinion becomes the named cause ("it was lawful; OLC said so"), and the named decision-makers who chose to kill recede behind the legal instrument. Who was publicly blamed: arguably no one — the killing was defended as lawful, and the memo's reasoning was the defense. Who actually controlled: the President, the National Security Council principals, the CIA/JSOC chain, and the OLC author who supplied the authorization. The diagnostic move the book teaches — *the citation is the first step in the analysis, not the last; find the invoker* — applies precisely. The opinion did not write itself; named officials requested it, and named officials acted on it.

Note the pattern-fit subtlety: this is *not* pretext (ch-6) in the strict sense — the government did not recruit a clean category to hide a different motive; it built a bespoke legal architecture for the actual motive. It is the **system/object alibi** in the specific OLC-opinion-as-shield form, which the book treats as its own recurring sub-pattern (the "opinion-shop" — ch-12 beat 3 rule 1). That makes it a near-perfect structural twin to Bybee/Yoo, which is exactly why it balances so well: the book already argues this pattern about a Republican administration; the al-Aulaqi memo lets the book argue the *identical* pattern about a Democratic one.

### (3) Taxonomy classification

Per `.claude/skills/taxonomy-classification/SKILL.md` blocks:
- Block 3 (system/object alibi): **YES.** A legal opinion (a procedure/instrument) is reified as the cause ("it was lawful conduct of war"); the OLC opinion has plausible mechanistic involvement (it genuinely authorized the act); the human actors who requested and acted on it faced no individual legal accountability (the suits were dismissed on justiciability grounds, never reaching the merits). The opinion's "authority" is treated as the answer to the accountability question.
- Block 4 (cost-bearing goat): **PARTIAL / SECONDARY.** Abdulrahman al-Aulaqi (16-year-old US citizen, killed in a separate October 2011 strike, never alleged to be a combatant) and Samir Khan absorbed lethal cost without being the named responsibility-holders. The administration never accountably explained Abdulrahman's death (an official later attributed it to being "in the wrong place at the wrong time" — unverified as policy and should not be quoted as an admission without Stephen locking the source).
- **Primary category: system/object alibi (legal-opinion-as-shield).** Secondary: cost-bearing goat (Abdulrahman al-Aulaqi / Samir Khan). Hybrid declared explicitly per rule 01.

### (4) Responsibility chain

- **Control:** President Obama; NSC principals; CIA/JSOC operational chain; OLC (Barron) for the authorizing instrument. The chain of authorization is partly documented and partly inferred (rule 07): the OLC memo, the AUMF framework, and named officials' public defenses document the instrument and the policy; the precise per-actor authorization sequence between NSC principals and the operational chain is reconstructed from reporting and the litigation record, not fully on the public primary record. - **Benefit:** the executive's claim of unreviewable lethal-targeting authority over citizens, established as precedent; the counterterrorism-results narrative.

- **Preventability:** entirely preventable; the decision was discretionary. Capture-infeasibility was asserted by the same officials who chose the strike.
- **Record-control:** strong executive record-control — the memo was classified and withheld for years; release came only by court order (Second Circuit, 2014). This also gives the case a *record-control* dimension (ch-12 relevance) on top of its primary system/object-alibi classification.
- **Cost:** Anwar al-Aulaqi (killed without trial; the government's evidence never tested adversarially); Abdulrahman al-Aulaqi (16, US citizen, no combatant allegation); Samir Khan; and — at the systemic level — every US citizen now subject to a precedent that lethal targeting can be authorized by executive legal opinion without judicial review.

## CANDIDATE 2 — Fast and Furious executive-privilege assertion (Obama/Holder) — RECOMMENDED WITH CAVEAT, A-/B+

### (1) One-paragraph factual summary

Operation Fast and Furious (2009–2011) was an ATF "gunwalking" operation in which agents allowed roughly 2,000 firearms to be trafficked toward Mexican cartels in hopes of building cases against higher-level traffickers; the operation lost track of most of the weapons, two of which were found at the scene of the December 2010 killing of US Border Patrol agent Brian Terry. As the House Oversight Committee investigated, DOJ initially sent Congress a February 4, 2011 letter denying gunwalking, then withdrew it ten months later as inaccurate. On June 20, 2012, at Attorney General Eric Holder's request, President Obama asserted executive privilege over a category of documents Congress had subpoenaed — specifically, internal DOJ deliberative materials about how the Department crafted and then corrected its responses to Congress and the media (i.e., post-operation documents, not the operational records). On June 28, 2012, the House voted 255–67 to hold Holder in criminal (and separately civil) contempt — the first sitting Attorney General so held. The DOJ Inspector General's 471-page report (September 19, 2012) found the operation was marked by "a series of misguided strategies, tactics, errors in judgment and management failures" and faulted fourteen officials, but did not find that Holder personally knew of gunwalking before early 2011. In *Committee on Oversight and Government Reform v. Lynch* (D.D.C.), Judge Amy Berman Jackson ruled on January 19, 2016 that the deliberative-process privilege did not shield the withheld documents under the case's specific circumstances — in part because DOJ had already disclosed the substance to its own IG — and ordered production; later rulings (2018) refined the privilege's scope, and the matter settled in 2019.

### (2) Laundering mechanism

Two distinct laundering layers, and the book should use only the *cleaner* one:

- **Layer A — the operation itself: partial scapegoat / management-failure.** The IG faulted line and mid-level ATF/DOJ officials; the operation was a genuine botch with real culpability at the execution level. This layer is *not* a clean laundering story — it is an accountability process that largely worked (the IG named names; officials resigned/were reassigned). Using this layer would risk the same over-claim the book warns against.
- **Layer B — the executive-privilege assertion: record-control.** This is the book-relevant layer. The privilege was asserted over documents about *how DOJ responded to oversight* — the record of the institution managing its own accountability. A court ultimately found the assertion did not hold under the circumstances and ordered production. This is a textbook **record-control** move: the actor who controls the record controls future responsibility, via privilege assertion. Who was publicly blamed: the contempt vote targeted Holder; the privilege framing recast the dispute as congressional overreach into protected deliberation. Who controlled: the executive, asserting privilege over its own self-investigation records. The diagnostic (ch-12) applies: *make the record's contestation visible; the privilege assertion is the laundering surface, the court order is the intercept.*

### (3) Taxonomy classification

- Block 2 (partial scapegoat): **YES** for Layer A (operational officials genuinely culpable; some accountability climbed but the operation's design responsibility was diffuse). This layer is real but is NOT the book's pattern-of-interest and risks over-claim.
- Block 3 (system/object alibi): **YES** for Layer B — *executive privilege* (a legal/procedural instrument) is reified as the reason documents cannot be produced; the named officials who decided to assert it recede behind "the privilege."
- **Recommended primary category for book use: system/object alibi (privilege-as-shield) / record-control sub-pattern.** Declare that the operational layer (partial scapegoat) is a separate, largely-functioning accountability story the book does not lean on. This honest two-layer split is itself a model of the taxonomy discipline.

### (4) Responsibility chain (Layer B only — the book-relevant layer)

- **Control:** President Obama (asserted the privilege) and AG Holder (requested it); DOJ leadership controlling the document corpus.
- **Benefit:** delay and limitation of congressional access to records of how DOJ handled the false February 2011 letter and its correction.
- **Knowledge:** documented — the privilege assertion was a deliberate, named decision (the June 20, 2012 letter from the Deputy AG is on record).
- **Preventability:** the assertion was discretionary; DOJ could have produced or negotiated.
- **Record-control:** this IS the case. Privilege as the record-control instrument; pierced by a court order on a specific-circumstances theory (prior disclosure to the IG waived/outweighed the privilege).
- **Cost:** the oversight function (delayed years); the Terry family (the underlying accountability for the operation that produced the weapons at the scene); and, at the systemic level, the contested precedent on deliberative-process privilege over oversight-response records.

## CANDIDATE 3 — IRS tax-exempt screening (Obama) — RECOMMEND **AGAINST** as a factual anchor (honesty finding)

### (1) One-paragraph factual summary

In May 2013, the Treasury Inspector General for Tax Administration (TIGTA) reported that the IRS unit processing tax-exempt (501(c)(4)) applications had used inappropriate criteria — "Be On the Lookout" (BOLO) terms including "Tea Party," "Patriots," and "9/12" — to flag applications for extra scrutiny, causing long delays. IRS official Lois Lerner publicly disclosed and apologized for the practice; she later asserted the Fifth Amendment before Congress. The story was widely framed as the Obama IRS targeting conservative groups. However, the documentary record was substantially complicated over the following years: the same 2013 TIGTA data showed some progressive-named groups were also flagged (at a lower rate), and a **second TIGTA report in September/October 2017** found that from 2004 to 2013 the IRS had used BOLO criteria capturing *both* conservative and liberal/progressive keywords (e.g., "progressive," "occupy," "Israel," "ACORN successors"). The 2017 report — requested by a bipartisan group of senators — significantly undercut the clean "one-side targeting" narrative, while still showing conservative groups bore the brunt of the delays. No criminal charges resulted; DOJ declined prosecution (2015); the IRS later settled lawsuits with affected conservative groups (2017–2018) and apologized.

### (2) Why I recommend against it — the honesty finding the editor needs

This is the candidate where the book's own thesis must be applied to the book. The CRITICAL HONESTY REQUIREMENT in the task is load-bearing here:

The IRS case is **a contested-record case, not a clean laundering case.** The original 2013 framing ("the IRS targeted conservatives") hardened into accepted fact in much public memory — and was then substantially complicated by the 2017 IG report showing bilateral keyword screening. **That trajectory — a weak/partial evidentiary picture hardening into a confident one-directional fact — is the exact failure mode the book exists to diagnose** (rule 02: "The book accuses other institutions of letting weak evidence harden into fact. The same charge applies to the crew."). To use the IRS case as a Democratic-administration laundering anchor, the book would have to adopt the 2013 framing and quietly under-weight the 2017 correction — i.e., commit responsibility-laundering's evidentiary cousin in the act of accusing others of it. That is disqualifying.

There is a *legitimate* laundering reading available (a system/object alibi: "the BOLO process did it" / "low-level Cincinnati employees did it," diffusing responsibility away from any named decision-maker, with Lerner as a partial-scapegoat candidate). But establishing *that* reading to A/B grade would require resolving the still-contested question of who set the criteria and why — and the record does not cleanly resolve it. Per the taxonomy skill's conflict rule: when the control/knowledge chain evidence is missing or contested, cap at C and do not advance to chapter use.

### (3)–(4) Taxonomy / chain (provisional, capped)

If forced: Block 3 (system/object alibi) candidate — "the BOLO process" / "the screening criteria" as the named cause, diffusing responsibility; Lerner as a possible partial scapegoat. But the control chain (who chose the criteria, with what intent) is **contested and unresolved on the public record**, so the classification cannot be confirmed. Cap: **C.**
