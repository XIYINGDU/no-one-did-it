## ch-3 — Cicero, *De Officiis* I.23 (Walter Miller, Loeb 1913) — **VERIFIED**

**Exact text (epigraph span):**
> "he who does not prevent or oppose wrong, if he can, is just as guilty of wrong as if he deserted his parents or his friends or his country."

Full sentence in §23: "For he who, under the influence of anger or some other passion, wrongfully assaults another seems, as it were, to be laying violent hands upon a comrade; but he who does not prevent or oppose wrong, if he can, is just as guilty of wrong as if he deserted his parents or his friends or his country."

**Locked citation:** Cicero, *De Officiis* I.23, trans. Walter Miller (Loeb Classical Library, 1913). Locus confirmed: Book I, section [23], subsection "Injustice: active and passive." Public domain (1913 Loeb; Gutenberg #47001). Verb "practised"/spelling as in Miller. Byte-exact confirmed against Gutenberg #47001.

## ch-7 — Islamic *isnad* voice, Alfred Guillaume, *The Traditions of Islam* (1924), Ch. IV — **VERIFIED**

**Exact text (aptest line, byte-confirmed twice):**
> "The *isnad* is a matter of religion; and were it not for the *isnad* any one could say what he pleased."

**Locked citation:** Alfred Guillaume, *The Traditions of Islam: An Introduction to the Study of the Hadith Literature* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1924), ch. IV ("Criticism of Hadith by Muslims"), p. 84. Public domain in the US (95-year term elapsed). Italics on *isnad* in source; "any one" is two words.

**Attribution nuance (load-bearing):** Guillaume renders this as the "solemn words" found within Imam Muslim's collection; he does **not** attribute it to a named individual (not to Ibn al-Mubarak). Attribute the epigraph to **Guillaume's translation** (the line as he gives it), NOT to a classical authority by name — naming Ibn al-Mubarak would exceed what this source supports. If the crew wants the classical attribution, that needs a separate sourced pass; until then, attribute to Guillaume.

Alt on the same page if a tighter line is wanted: "The text of a tradition is rarely taken into account, and that criticism is confined to the *isnad*" (p. 86). Both VERIFIED.
