## Verdict: CLEAR-WITH-FIXES

The preface is, on the whole, unusually disciplined for a sales front-matter — the genre most tempted to over-promise. The three opening cases are accurately compressed against their A-graded case files; the central promise (an eight-question instrument and four shapes, walked through real cases) is a payoff the book genuinely delivers (ch-13 ships a six-page field guide carrying exactly those artefacts); and the closing return to the three openers honors the A2B "return to the opening object" contract. No veto.

But three fixes are required and one is recommended before front-matter lock. The two REQUIRED rule-05 fixes are the same class of self-claim overclaim I flagged in the ch-8 al-Aulaqi synthesis ("proves" / "the proof"): the preface reaches for asserted-causation and asserted-intent verbs ("deliberate," "on purpose," "built ... on purpose, for a reason") that the book's own evidence discipline elsewhere hedges. The third REQUIRED fix is a non-partisan-balance calibration: the preface's "from one American administration to the next across decades" is now *defensible* because al-Aulaqi (Obama) is in the roster — but it is defensible only narrowly, and the current phrasing implies a more even cross-party distribution than the roster actually carries. None rises to veto; the claims are correctable in place without weakening any evidence.

## Finding 1 (REQUIRED — rule 05 / V8) — the closing "built ... on purpose, for a reason" asserts intent the cases do not uniformly carry

Line 19 (the close):

> "*No one did it* is not a fact about the world. It is a thing that was built — by someone, on purpose, for a reason."

This is the preface's hammer line, and it is the single sentence most at risk of failing the book's own diagnostic. "Built ... on purpose, for a reason" asserts deliberate design — documented intent — as a universal property of *every* case the book covers. The book's own roster does not support that universal:

- **Therac-25** (ch-2 opening case): the case file is explicit that the alibi *migrated downward as challenges arrived* — operator, then machine, then bug. That is a sequence of institutional reflex and containment, not necessarily a single actor who "built" the alibi "on purpose." The case file's load-bearing finding is *structural* (no hardware interlocks, no independent code review, framing that climbs down the stack), not a documented intent to launder.
- **Boeing** (the plane-in-the-sea opener): the DPA admits Boeing *misled the FAA* — a documented deception at the certification-deception layer — but the case file is careful that "the institutional decisions that approved MCAS's single-sensor architecture ... were never personally adjudicated." Intent is documented for the AEG deception, not for the alibi-construction as a whole.

The book's central diagnostic move (over-rule 2; ch-2's "the alibi can climb the institutional stack") is precisely that responsibility laundering *does not require* a single designing intent — it often runs on procedure, reflex, and downward-migrating blame with *no one* having "built" it on purpose. That is the more unsettling and more original claim. The closing line, by asserting universal deliberate construction, actually *undercuts* the book's most distinctive thesis and reaches past rule 05's bar on "deliberately" / "on purpose" without per-case citation.

**Why this is V8 and not a quibble:** the book diagnoses others for letting a confident causal story harden past the evidence. The preface's closing line lets "built on purpose" harden into a universal where the evidence is case-by-case. A hostile reader who reaches ch-2 and finds Therac-25 framed as downward-migrating containment — not as a deliberately built alibi — will read the preface's promise as oversold on page one.

**Fix (Wayne):** keep the rhetorical snap; drop the universal-intent claim. The honest and *stronger* version names that the move is often deliberate AND often structural — that "no one did it" can be engineered or can be the emergent product of an apparatus built to make the question unanswerable (which line 11 already says well: "the apparatus is built to make a single question hard to answer"). Suggested register:

> "*No one did it* is not a fact about the world. It is a thing that gets built — sometimes by a decision, always by an arrangement — and what is built can be taken apart."

or, if the single-builder cadence is wanted, attribute it to the cases that earn it rather than to all: "...it is a thing that was built — sometimes deliberately, always by an arrangement that could have been otherwise." The point that survives at full strength: it is *not* nature, it is *not* inevitable, and it is reversible. That is the claim the book delivers. "On purpose, for a reason" as a universal is the claim it cannot.

## Finding 2 (REQUIRED — rule 05 / V8) — "old, deliberate, and increasingly refined" front-loads the same universal-intent overclaim

Line 7:

> "What looks like an accident, or complexity, or an unfortunate system failure is very often a move — old, deliberate, and increasingly refined."

Two mitigations are already present and good: "very often" (not "always") and "a move" (which the book does establish as a recurring pattern). The problem is the adjective "deliberate" sitting in an unhedged triad. Paired with line 19's "on purpose," it sets up an intent-frame the roster only partly honors. This is the same verb-class as the ch-8 "proves" finding: a certainty register the evidence grade cannot uniformly cover.

Note the asymmetry that makes this a real finding rather than pedantry: the al-Aulaqi case (now load-bearing for the non-partisan claim — see Finding 3) is the *cleanest* case of a *deliberate* instrument (the OLC memo was authored, reviewed, pre-authorized). But Therac-25 and the system/object-alibi cases are the *least* deliberate. "Deliberate" as a blanket adjective over-describes exactly the cases the book uses to teach that laundering needs no single deliberate author.

**Fix (Wayne):** soften the adjective to track the genuine range. "...a move — old, sometimes deliberate, increasingly refined." Or replace "deliberate" with a structure word the whole roster carries: "...a move — old, recurring, and increasingly refined." "Recurring" is the book's actual, fully-earned claim (it is literally the thesis sentence: "the recurring method"); "deliberate" is the one it cannot make uniformly. This is a one-word fix that removes the V8 surface without losing any heat.

## Finding 3 (REQUIRED — non-partisan balance / V1) — "from one American administration to the next across decades" is now defensible but the phrasing overstates the evenness of the roster

Line 15:

> "The move belongs to no party and no flag. It runs from one American administration to the next across decades ... It is not a property of the left or the right."

This is the highest-stakes claim in the dispatch, and the honest answer is: **the claim is now true, but only just, and the current phrasing implies more balance than the roster delivers.** The facts:

- The roster's *contemporary US administration anchors* are Trump I / Trump II (family separation, census citizenship, J6 pardons/DOJ scrubbing, Alien Enemies Act/Abrego Garcia, DOGE records, Ukraine aid hold). The cross-valence memo (2026-05-28) documented this capture explicitly: "all administration cases are Trump I / Trump II."
- The *one* Democratic-administration case in the book is the al-Aulaqi OLC memo (Obama 2010), added 2026-05-28 to ch-8 beat 4 as the cross-party echo. It is A-grade and genuine — not manufactured balance (per over-rule 1; per the cross-valence memo's honest grading; per my 2026-05-28 al-Aulaqi clearance).
- The historical echoes ch-8 leans on for "administration-agnostic" inoculation (Watergate/Nixon, Iran-Contra/Reagan-Bush 41) are cross-*era* but, as the cross-valence memo flagged, both *Republican*.

So the roster is: many Republican-administration cases, one Democratic-administration case, plus cross-era (but same-party) historical echoes. That genuinely supports "no party and no flag" as a claim about the *structural pattern* — the al-Aulaqi case proves the pattern is administration-agnostic on the merits, which is exactly why it is the right fix and not a stunt. But "it runs from one American administration to the next across decades" reads, in a sales preface, as a claim of roughly even back-and-forth cross-party recurrence. The roster does not show even back-and-forth. It shows one well-chosen Democratic instance against a Republican-weighted contemporary set.

**Why this is V1 and not a request to manufacture symmetry:** the cure for the cold reader's partisan-capture finding must itself be honest (my standing rule — see memory [[feedback-cross-valence-honesty]]). The fix is NOT to inflate the Democratic count or soften the Republican cases — that would be the false-equivalence the project style rules forbid. The fix is to make the preface's *claim* match the roster's *actual shape*: the **pattern** is party-neutral (true, and al-Aulaqi demonstrates it); the **book does not claim the cases are evenly distributed across parties**, because the move follows power-under-procedure, and whichever party holds the relevant levers in a given case is incidental to the structure.

**Fix (Wayne):** narrow the claim from implied even distribution to the genuine structural claim. The existing line 15 *already does most of this work* in its second half — "It is not a property of the left or the right. It is a property of power operating under procedure." That is the honest claim. The vulnerable clause is the first one. Suggested:

> "The move belongs to no party and no flag. It has run under administrations of both parties and across decades; it runs through a hospital radiation machine, a defense ministry, a frontier artificial-intelligence lab, a postal service, a census. It is not a property of the left or the right. It is a property of power operating under procedure, and procedure is everywhere power is."

"Under administrations of both parties" is exactly true (Obama + Trump, at minimum) and claims no more than the roster carries. "From one administration to the next across decades" should go, because it implies a serial cross-party chain the book does not document. This keeps the inoculation honest: the pattern is party-neutral; the book is not pretending its case selection is symmetric.

## Finding 4 (RECOMMENDED — rule 02 / C-grade dating) — "Three thousand years ago" overstates the datable record

Line 9:

> "Three thousand years ago a goat was driven into the wilderness carrying the community's sins..."

The ancient-scapegoat case file anchors the rite in Leviticus 16, and modern scholarship (Milgrom, cited in the case file) places the Priestly composition of Leviticus 16 in roughly the 6th–5th century BCE — about 2,500 years ago, not 3,000. The case file does note a *wider* ancient Near Eastern practice (Babylonian *kuppuru*; the practice "is one survival of a wider practice") that could reach further back, so "three thousand years" is not fabricated — but it is at the loose end of the datable record and the case file does not firmly anchor a 3,000-year date.

This is RECOMMENDED, not REQUIRED, because (a) it is a rhetorical round number in a preface, not a load-bearing chapter claim resting on C-grade evidence (which rule 02 would bar), and (b) ch-1/ch-2 carry the precise textual citations. But the book's authority *is* its grading discipline, and a sales preface that opens its historical claim with a slightly-too-round number is a small, free target for a hostile reviewer.

**Fix (Wayne, optional):** soften to "Some twenty-five centuries ago" (tracks the Leviticus 16 dating the case file supports) or keep the resonance but hedge the precision: "Thousands of years ago a goat was driven into the wilderness..." The second keeps the cadence and is unimpeachable.
