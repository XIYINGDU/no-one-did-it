## Finding 1 (REQUIRED — V1/V8) — the synthesis claims a shared shape al-Aulaqi does not instantiate

This is the central red-team finding. The chapter runs on ONE structural spine, stated in beats 2, 7, 8 and restated in the synthesis at line 76:

> "the accountability mechanism is named as the prior laundering, and dismantling the mechanism is named as the correction."

Watergate fits it (the Cox firing recast the prosecution as the persecution). Iran-Contra fits it (the Independent Counsel recast as a partisan apparatus; the pardons as the correction). The second-term proclamations fit it (prosecutions / records statutes / due-process review named as the prior abuse). **al-Aulaqi does not fit it.** No accountability mechanism was named as the prior abuse in the al-Aulaqi case. Nobody recast the pre-strike lawsuit, or a records statute, or a court, as the villain. The government won dismissal on standing and political-question, then special-factors — it *avoided* review; it did not *invert* a mechanism into the wrongdoer.

The al-Aulaqi paragraph's own text (line 74) knows this — it makes a *different* structural claim: "an opinion authored inside the branch that would act converted a contestable executive decision into a closed legal conclusion, controlled by the branch that benefited from it." That is the **opinion-as-instrument** shape — the same shape as the Bybee/Yoo torture memos (ch-2 slot 5; ch-12 brief's "OLC opinion-shop pattern"), which line 74 itself names as the structural twin. It is NOT the **mechanism-named-as-abuse** shape the synthesis on line 76 asserts all three echoes share.

So line 76 forces al-Aulaqi into a shape it doesn't occupy, in order to make the cross-party roster read as a clean three-of-a-kind. That is the chapter smoothing a structural seam to improve the story — the exact move the book diagnoses (V8: no internal laundering; V1: earned, not manufactured). A hostile reader who knows the al-Aulaqi facts will see that the synthesis sentence does not survive its own diagnostic.

**Why this is a V1/V8 finding and not just a quibble:** the cold reader's problem was partisan capture — and the cure for it must itself be honest, or the cure reproduces the disease at one remove. Claiming a false structural identity to complete the roster is partisan-symmetry-by-assertion: it makes the cross-party point look earned by fiat rather than by fit.

**Fix (Wayne):** do not force al-Aulaqi into the mechanism-as-abuse shape. The chapter has TWO related laundering shapes co-present and should say so:
- The mechanism-as-abuse shape (Watergate, Iran-Contra, second-term) — the chapter's spine.
- The opinion-as-instrument shape (Bybee/Yoo under Bush; Barron under Obama) — the cross-party pair that line 74 actually demonstrates.

The cross-administration inoculation is *stronger* this way, not weaker: it shows the diagnostic catches BOTH shapes across BOTH parties. Concretely, revise line 76 so the "they share one structural shape" sentence either (a) narrows to the three mechanism-as-abuse cases (Watergate, Iran-Contra, second-term) and frames al-Aulaqi as the cross-party instance of the *adjacent* opinion-as-instrument shape the chapter also tracks; or (b) raises the shared description to the genuine common abstraction — "a contestable executive decision is converted into a settled conclusion, by the branch that controls and benefits, insulated from external review" — which all four cases DO share, with the named sub-mechanism differing (reframing the mechanism vs. closing the question by opinion). Option (b) is the cleaner single-sentence fix and keeps the synthesis intact. Either way, the false identity claim on line 76 must go.

## Finding 2 (REQUIRED — rule 05) — "proves" / "the proof" overstate, and lean on Finding 1

Two rhetorical-register overclaims, both rule-05 flagged verbs in the book's own voice:
- Line 74: "the next echo **proves** it from the other side."
- Line 76: "The roster is **the proof**, not the embarrassment."

These are not defamation surface (they are the book's self-claim, not a claim about a named person), so they are out of Nancy's lane and in mine. But they are exactly the certainty-verbs rule 05 says to soften to "supports / indicates / shows," and they are doing *extra* work precisely because Finding 1's structural fit is overstated. A roster of three (or four) cases *supports* an administration-agnostic claim; it does not *prove* it (a sampling-from-the-record claim cannot be proof, and the chapter elsewhere — line 198 — correctly hedges to "the deepest documentary record," not "all"). Once Finding 1 is fixed, the honest verb is "supports" / "demonstrates across the aisle," not "proves."

**Fix (Wayne):** line 74 "the next echo proves it" → "the next echo shows it"/"the next echo carries it across the aisle." Line 76 "the roster is the proof, not the embarrassment" → "the roster carries the point, not the embarrassment" or "the roster is the demonstration, not the embarrassment." Keep the rhetorical snap; drop the epistemics the evidence can't cover.

Item 4 of the dispatch (and Alan's stated risk) is under-carrying the steelman. But it slightly understates how contested the case is, and it leaves the steelman's strongest residue (the AUMF-reaches-Yemen premise was "widely accepted," per Alan Q2a) standing unaddressed. Per over-rule 4, the strongest counterargument should be visibly met, not partly stepped around.

Marking RECOMMENDED not REQUIRED because the chapter does not *win by under-carrying* — it wins on detachment-from-review, which is A-grade structure independent of the legality split.
