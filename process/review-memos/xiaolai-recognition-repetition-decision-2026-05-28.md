### DISPOSITION 1 — Class B (recognition delivered, not earned)

**PARTIAL — selective REVISE, with named limits. Not a full six-chapter rewrite, and not a KEEP.**

The book has two distinct things its reports lump under "delivered recognition," and the diagnostic the book teaches separates them:

1. **A reference instrument being installed.** Ch-03's eight questions (lines 43-52) are the book's master tool, carried for ten subsequent chapters. You cannot induct a reader into an eight-item checklist without spending the whole chapter, and a checklist the reader must carry is legitimately *handed over*, not *discovered*. The chapter already primes the want (lines 21-23, "the gap is what concerns us... Above all, a question and the eight smaller questions that compose it") before printing the list. That is the correct order: build the felt need, then hand the tool. This is the book's legitimate didactic register, and A2B rule 10 is not violated — the recognition the chapter must *earn* is "the sequence is the laundering" (line 37) and "the gap names the laundering" (line 51), not the existence of the checklist. The reader earned those; the takeaway axis came back CLEAN.

2. **A genuine recognition beat that the prose pre-empts.** This is the defect, and it is real. Ch-11 line 140 is the clearest instance: the italic free-standing aphorism *"The diagnostic was always a design rule wearing forensic clothes"* states the click and then the chapter labels it a recognition — and re-states it at line 188. Ch-08 line 71-73 ("The reversal sits here," after the point is made three times in plain prose immediately above) is the same move. Ch-04 lines 131/175/179 ("This is the recognition") announces the second recognition before the matrix that would let the reader earn it. Ch-02 line 97 ("This is our most important asymmetry") tells a conclusion buried under procedural bookkeeping. These fail V4 directly: the V4 test tolerates zero or one explicit "this is the recognition / what this shows" sentence; these chapters run two-to-four.

**What concretely changes (selective, by chapter):**
- **Cut or demote the free-standing labeling sentence**, not the insight. Ch-11 line 140: cut the italic aphorism *or* fold it into the matrix prose at line 138 where the reader is already doing the work, so the prose *points* without *concluding*. Same for ch-08 line 71 ("The reversal sits here") and ch-04 line 175 ("This is the recognition"). The rule: a recognition beat may have a short reversal sentence (rule 04 explicitly wants one) but may **not** have a sentence that *names the recognition as a recognition*. Point, do not announce-then-label.
- **Move the announcement after the evidence, never before it.** Ch-04's worst sub-failure is that line 131 pre-tells the multi-channel recognition *before* the seven-channel matrix that delivers it. Reorder so the matrix lands first and the reader arrives a half-step ahead of any summary. (Bonnie, structural — the matrix already exists; this is sequencing.)
- **Strip the internal build-stage labels that leaked into recognition prose.** Ch-04 line 177's "the second move from S1.5 to S2" is not a recognition style question — it is leaked metadata that reads as a coordinate from a system the reader is locked out of. Cut. (This overlaps Wayne's clear prose work; route with Class A-residual.)

**What does NOT change:** Ch-03's eight-question installation. The instrument is handed over by design; the chapter earns the recognitions that matter (sequence, gap). Leave the list where it is and as it is.

Reasoning: The escalation framed this as a single voice decision spanning six chapters. Value 4 says the single frame compresses out the load-bearing distinction between *installing a tool* (legitimate handoff) and *staging a recognition* (must be earned). The fix is not "un-tell six chapters" — that would over-correct the installation chapters and risk regressing V5 (capacity change) on the book's central instrument. The fix is to cut the small number of sentences that *announce-and-label* a recognition, and to reorder the one chapter (ch-04) that puts the announcement before its own evidence. That is a selective prose-and-sequencing pass on roughly four chapters (02, 04, 08, 11), not a register change.

The dissent or counter that would have changed the call: *"The aphorism IS the book's teaching voice; a diagnostic manual is entitled to state its conclusions, and cold readers conflate 'I was told' with 'I disagree.'"* — Named in the triage and in several reports' open questions. It would have moved me to KEEP **if** the labeled-recognition sentences were carrying real evidential or pedagogical load. They are not: in every flagged instance the chapter has *already made the point in plain prose immediately above the label* (ch-08 three times before line 73; ch-11 the matrix at 138 before the aphorism at 140). The label is therefore pure redundancy that also trips V4 — it costs the earned click and adds nothing. A standalone short reversal sentence survives (rule 04 wants it); a sentence that says "this is the recognition" does not.

### DISPOSITION 2 — Class C (repetition fatigue on the eight-question diagnostic)

**PARTIAL — compress after first full installation; keep the full walk once. Not KEEP-as-is, not aggressive cut.**

The mechanism the repetition encodes (Value 2): a reader who skips a chapter still leaves with the portable instrument. That mechanism is real for a diagnostic manual and it still binds — so a blanket "cut the repetition" is best-practice retrieval that would damage the book's working-manual function. But the mechanism does **not** require the *full eight-question walk in near-complete form four-plus times*. It requires the tool be installed once in full, then *referenced* — not re-walked cell by cell — thereafter. The reports are consistent and specific: ch-03 itself re-prints the full list a third time at lines 164-171 ("in the form a reader can carry away") *after* it has already run twice in the same chapter; ch-06 runs a six-questions-by-three-cases grid (eighteen dense cells) that is an explicit put-down point; ch-08's eight-question walk reads as a "proper-noun inventory" that lost the reader before the chapter's own turn.

**What concretely changes:**
- **Keep the full numbered walk exactly once, where it is introduced — ch-03, lines 43-52,** plus its first full demonstration (VW). That is the installation. Do not touch it.
- **Within ch-03, cut the third reprint of the full list (lines 162-171).** The reader has watched it run twice in the same chapter; the carry-away reprint is redundant *inside* the installation chapter. (A back-of-book reference card, if wanted, is the right home for a clean reprint — that is a separate Bonnie call, surfaced below.)
- **After ch-03, abbreviate the walk to a referenced subset, not a full re-walk.** In later chapters (06, 08, and any other that re-walks all eight), run the diagnostic by naming only the two-to-three questions that *do new work in that case* and citing the rest by reference ("the control, benefit, and knowledge questions run as in chapter 3; here the record-control question does the new work"). The eighteen-cell grid in ch-06 is the priority target: collapse it to the cells that differentiate the three cases. (Bonnie, structural.)
- **Keep re-walks where the chapter's whole point is the re-walk surviving a new domain** — e.g., ch-03's Pinto run exists to prove forty-year portability; that is load-bearing, not fatigue. Do not abbreviate a walk whose existence is the argument.

Reasoning: Value 2 protects the installation (mechanism still binds); Value 4 protects against the elegant over-cut that would strip the book's manual function. The honest middle is: install once in full, reference thereafter, and only ever re-walk in full when the re-walk *is* the chapter's claim. This is a per-chapter compression (rule 08 would class it `structural-polish` for ch-06 and the affected later chapters), not a wholesale removal.

The dissent or counter that would have changed the call: *"The whole point of a diagnostic manual is that the tool is portable; a reader who reads only chapter 8 must still get the full instrument, so every chapter must re-walk it in full."* — The strongest pro-repetition case. It would have moved me to KEEP **if** the book were a reference work read by chapter-dipping. It is trade nonfiction read front-to-back (the reports are sequential cold-reads; the V6 "diagnostic memory" target assumes linear installation). For a linear reader, the fourth full walk does not install — it fatigues, and the reports show it actively *cost* retention (ch-06 H2: "I lost track of what was new"). The portability concern is real but is correctly served by a back-of-book reference card, not by re-walking inside the narrative. That redirect is what makes this PARTIAL rather than KEEP.
