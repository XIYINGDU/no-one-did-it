### Cross-chapter coherence check

**Q1: Is the template doing the same WORK in every chapter?**

No, and that is the correct read. The template's four blocks (Recognize / Diagnose / Act / Avoid) name four distinct functions of a beat-10. Each function appears in every chapter's beat-10, but the FORM the function takes depends on the chapter's case-type:

- **Diagnostic chapters (2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 10):** template fits cleanly because the chapter is itself installing a diagnostic instrument. The four blocks correspond to install-validate-deploy-self-protect.
- **Anchor-deepening chapters (5, 8):** template strains because the chapter is extending a prior diagnostic into a specific case-type. Ch-5 dissolves the blocks into prose; ch-8 splits the Avoid block across four role-seams.
- **Rule-chapters (11, 12):** template inverts because the chapter argues from rule to case, not case to rule. Ch-11 uses four roles directly; ch-12 uses four roles + fifth note.
- **Power-self-as-goat chapter (9):** template doesn't fit at all. The chapter's diagnostic is "is the speaker using 'abuse' to describe the prosecution or what the prosecution revealed?" — that is a three-test frame, not a marker frame.
- **Field-guide chapter (13):** template compresses to one-line role moves because the chapter IS the field guide.
- **Recognition-only chapter (1):** template is deliberately not present because the chapter's job is to install recognition without method.

The template is doing the same FUNCTION in every chapter; it is not doing the same FORM. That is the right discipline.

**Q2: Does the "three markers" assertion hold corpus-wide?**

Partially.

- **Chapters where "three markers" is asserted and earned:** 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 10 (per-layer), 12.
- **Chapters where "three markers" is replaced with something else honest:** 1 (three signs — speed/scale/absence), 5 (four properties of partial-scapegoats + second marker), 8 (three markers diagnose ONE layer of the five-layer stack), 9 (three tests, not markers), 11 (one diagnostic question), 13 (four markers — see below).
- **Chapter where the marker count drifts without flag:** 13 lists four markers at L277 ("a procedure cited without a named person; a name without a named function; a record held by one party to the dispute; or a destruction announcement without an inventory") — this is a deliberate compression of the corpus's recurring patterns into a portable list; the field-guide form requires this, and the brief commits to it. **Honest.**

Wayne's "embraced the threshold" note in ch-2 brief at L172 commits to the three-markers assertion as a heuristic, tested across the book. The corpus shows the heuristic operating where it can (taxonomy diagnostic chapters) and adapting honestly where it cannot (rule chapters, power-self chapter, field guide). The honest adaptation is the right move; pretending three markers always work would have failed V1 (earned not manufactured).

**Q3: Are any beat-10s functioning as LIVE recognition vs as TAKEAWAY APPENDIX?**

This is the value question — does the beat-10 land as the chapter's recognition beat, or as a tacked-on "what to do at home" appendix?

- **Live recognition** (the beat-10 lands as the chapter's diagnostic landing, not as a separate audience-translation): chs 1, 3, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12.
- **Strong but slightly appendix-feeling** (the beat-10 is functionally useful but tonally separates from the analytical body): chs 2 and 4. In each, the four-block template's labelled headers create a slight register shift — the chapter body operates in analytical prose, then beat-10 opens with **bolded directive headers**. This is the cost of the template's clarity. The chapter is still functionally complete; the register-shift is the cost of giving the reader a portable structure. **Acceptable** — the cost is paid for the benefit.
- **Compressed to artefact** (beat-10 is by design a compressed instrument, not an analytical landing): ch-13. This is correct for ch-13 specifically; would be wrong elsewhere.
- **Distributed across the chapter close** (beat-10 reads as continuous with the chapter's argumentative landing): ch-5. The chapter dissolves the template into prose and gains continuity at the cost of structural clarity. Defensible for a "where the partial-scapegoat pattern shows up" chapter; the looseness fits the chapter's argument.

No beat-10 in the corpus functions as a true takeaway-appendix that severs from the chapter's argument. The closest is ch-2 itself, the template install, which has to perform the template's structure in front of the reader for the first time. Subsequent chapters inherit the form and earn it.
