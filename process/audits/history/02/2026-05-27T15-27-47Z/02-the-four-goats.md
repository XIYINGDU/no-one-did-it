# Chapter 2 — The Four Goats

## Accusation scene — a treatment room in East Texas

At the East Texas Cancer Center in Tyler, on the afternoon of March 21, 1986, a thirty-three-year-old oil-field worker named Voyne Ray Cox was lying face down on the treatment couch of a Therac-25 medical linear accelerator. He had been there before. A tumor had been cut from his shoulder; the radiation was the follow-up. The machine was newer than its predecessor at the clinic. It was operated by a technician seated in an adjoining room, on the other side of a shielding wall, at a console that talked to the accelerator through software written for a PDP-11 computer.

The console displayed "Malfunction 54." The technician, who could not see Cox through the wall, cleared the error and pressed proceed. Cox felt what he later described, in the words preserved by Nancy Leveson's investigation seven years afterward, as the sensation of being hit "between the shoulders with a baseball bat." [CITE: leveson-turner-therac-25-ieee-1993] He got up, walked out, and reported what had happened. Within months he was dying. He died in September.

The manufacturer of the machine was Atomic Energy of Canada Limited, then a Canadian Crown corporation. AECL's first response to the East Texas accident, and to four serious overdoses that had preceded it at Marietta, Hamilton, Yakima, and again at Tyler three weeks later, followed a consistent template. The machine, AECL's field engineers told the hospitals, could not have delivered the dose the patients described. It was operating as designed. The error must lie elsewhere — with the operator, with the patient, with a coincidence of unrelated illness.

Six patients across three countries received massive overdoses from Therac-25 accelerators between June 1985 and January 1987. [CITE: leveson-turner-therac-25-ieee-1993] At least two of them — Cox and Glen Dodd at Yakima — died of injuries directly attributable to the accidents; Verdon Kidd at Marietta died after his accident, but whether the overdose was the proximate cause remains contested in the medical record. [CITE: leveson-turner-therac-25-ieee-1993] None of them was named publicly as the cause of what happened to them. None of them needed to be. The named cause was the machine. The machine, the manufacturer said, could not.

This is the pattern we now install. Not the Therac-25 specifically — that case appears here as the first of nine. The pattern has four shapes, and these are the pages that name them. It is what made it possible, again and again across the following four decades, for a corporation, a court system, a military doctrine apparatus, and a state to point at the wrong actor when a harm needed someone to be the cause of it. The four shapes are not interchangeable. We call them the four goats. Recognizing which shape is in front of us is the first move of every diagnostic that follows.

## Official story — the three-stage shift

The most useful thing about the Therac-25 record is that it does not show one alibi. It shows three, in order, each replacing the one before it as the prior one collapsed.

The first stage was the operator. After the second East Texas accident in April 1986, AECL's internal field-service correspondence focused on the technician at the console: an experienced operator who had pressed an editing key to correct a treatment-mode entry, and who became, in AECL's framing, the proximate cause of what had happened to the patient on the couch. The hospital physicist Fritz Hager and the AECL field engineer spent days in the treatment room trying to reproduce the keystroke sequence. They did reproduce it. The reproduction did not stay inside the operator-error frame; it pushed the case into the second stage.

The second stage was the machine. Once the same symptoms had appeared at multiple sites under experienced operators, the framing shifted to passive constructions: the system delivered an erroneous dose, the machine produced an unexpected output, the device entered an unintended state. AECL filed a Medical Device Report with the United States Food and Drug Administration on April 15, 1986, after the second Tyler accident. [CITE: fda-therac-25-recall-and-cap-1986-1987] The FDA declared the device defective on May 2 of that year and ultimately classified the recall as Class I — its highest severity tier, reserved for products with a reasonable probability of causing serious injury or death. AECL responded with what it characterized as a fix: disabling the Up-Arrow editing key on the operator console. The fix did not work. The Yakima accident of January 1987 happened under the supposedly fixed software.

The third stage was the unnamed software bug. Forced to acknowledge that the keystroke sequence had reproduced a software state, AECL described the issue as a programming defect — an isolated error in code, the kind of artifact that turns up in any complex system. Not as the predictable outcome of design choices: a single programmer writing safety-critical real-time code without independent verification; the elimination of hardware interlocks that had backstopped the same software faults in earlier Therac models; the absence of any formal hazard analysis on the deployed system. The bug framing kept the named cause at the level of the artifact, never climbing to the people who had designed the artifact's safety architecture.

Three layers of named cause: the operator at the console, the machine on the floor, the bug in the code. None of the three names a Boeing executive, an AECL vice-president, or a regulator. None of the three is wrong, exactly. The operator did press the key; the machine did deliver the dose; there was a bug. What the three layers achieve, taken together, is a containment. Each named cause is true at its level and insufficient at any higher one. The named cause migrates downward as challenges arrive. The people who could have prevented the harm — by retaining hardware interlocks, by commissioning independent code review, by disclosing the pattern across sites after the second accident in July 1985 — never appear in the same sentence as the harm they did not prevent.

This is the first kind of laundering we have learned to name, and it does not run only on radiation therapy machines.

## Hidden architecture — the alibi climbs the institutional stack

Take the same pattern, scale it from a Canadian medical-device division to one of the world's two civil-aerospace manufacturers, and watch what happens to the named cause as the institutional stack rises.

On October 29, 2018, Lion Air Flight 610, a Boeing 737 MAX 8, crashed into the Java Sea thirteen minutes after takeoff from Jakarta. All 189 people aboard were killed. [CITE: knkt-lion-air-610-final-report-2019] On March 10, 2019, Ethiopian Airlines Flight 302, also a 737 MAX 8, crashed near Bishoftu six minutes after takeoff from Addis Ababa. All 157 people aboard were killed. [CITE: ecaa-ethiopian-302-final-report-2022] The total across the two crashes was 346 people. The aircraft was grounded globally by March 13, 2019, with the FAA grounding last among major regulators — twelve days after it had publicly affirmed the type's airworthiness. [CITE: faa-emergency-order-grounding-737-max-2019-03-13]

The Indonesian KNKT final report of October 2019 and the Ethiopian ECAA final report of December 2022 documented the same mechanical sequence. [CITE: knkt-lion-air-610-final-report-2019; ecaa-ethiopian-302-final-report-2022] A flight-control software system called the Maneuvering Characteristics Augmentation System — MCAS — had repeatedly commanded nose-down trim of the horizontal stabilizer, based on erroneous data from a single angle-of-attack sensor, until the crews lost the ability to recover. The named cause moved through the same three-stage shift that AECL's correspondence had run thirty-two years earlier.

Stage one: the pilots. In the weeks after Lion Air, Boeing's external framing identified the runaway-stabilizer memory item as sufficient to recover from any MCAS-induced trim runaway. Boeing's then-CEO Dennis Muilenburg, in a November 2018 conference call as reported by the *Seattle Times*, told investors that the company provided "all the information that's needed to safely fly our aircraft." [CITE: seattle-times-gates-baker-737-max-2018-2019 — pending-stephen-lock] The implication was clear. The Lion Air crew had not used what was given to them. Stage one held for four months. Then ET 302's crew, having received the post-Lion-Air guidance, executed the procedure as written and were unable to recover. The pilot-error framing collapsed within weeks.

Stage two: MCAS. The named cause shifted to the software architecture itself, in the same passive language AECL had used: MCAS had taken inputs from a single sensor, had commanded repeated trim inputs, had failed to disengage. The agency was placed on the artifact. The named individuals who had chosen the single-sensor input — and who, in 2016, had quietly removed an earlier low-speed authority limitation that would have made the failure mode less consequential — were not personally named in Boeing's external account.

Stage three: the certification process. The third and most load-bearing alibi was procedural. The Federal Aviation Administration's Organization Designation Authorization framework, under FAA Order 8110.4C, delegated portions of certification authority to Boeing employees acting as Authorized Representatives of the FAA. In other words, Boeing's own engineers signed for the FAA on Boeing's own aircraft. The Pulitzer-winning *Seattle Times* series by Dominic Gates and Mike Baker, beginning on March 17, 2019, documented that FAA technical staff had been overruled on MCAS reviews, and that the System Safety Analysis for MCAS had understated its control authority by roughly a factor of four. [CITE: seattle-times-gates-baker-737-max-2018-2019] The House Committee on Transportation and Infrastructure final report of September 2020 ran 245 pages and documented five "broken processes" across Boeing and the FAA: production pressure, faulty design assumptions, a culture of concealment, conflicts of interest within the ODA process, and grossly insufficient FAA oversight. [CITE: house-ti-final-report-737-max-2020-09] The named cause was the certification process. The certification process is not a person.

The alibi can climb the institutional stack. That is the sentence to keep. The Therac-25 alibi sat at the machine layer — one device, one manufacturer, one regulator, one bug. The Boeing alibi climbs from the pilot through the software to the certification framework, and from the certification framework, as the next beat will show, into the federal-prosecution layer. The same move — naming an artifact or a process where a controlling decision belongs — happens at every floor of the building. The taller the building, the more the move can absorb. The category of laundering this teaches is what we call the **system/object alibi**: a tool, a piece of software, a corporation, a procedure, a "process" is named as the cause when the named decisions were made by identifiable people. Once we learn to recognize this category at the machine floor — Cox at Tyler, the bug in the PDP-11 code — we have done most of the work of recognizing it at the procedural floor, where it does most of its modern damage.

## Older echo — Sacco and Vanzetti

The system/object alibi is the least intuitive of the four categories, which is why we have installed it first. The next category is the oldest and the easiest to feel. It is the category in which a person is named the cause of a harm they did not produce, in order to close the public record on the harm.

On April 15, 1920, the paymaster Frederick Parmenter and the guard Alessandro Berardelli were shot to death during the robbery of the Slater and Morrill shoe factory payroll in South Braintree, Massachusetts. Three weeks later, on May 5, 1920, two Italian immigrants — Nicola Sacco, a shoemaker, and Bartolomeo Vanzetti, a fish-peddler — were arrested on a streetcar in Brockton. Both were followers of the deported anarchist Luigi Galleani. Both were armed when arrested. Both, by the prosecution's later account at trial, gave the police that night false statements about their politics and associations, which the prosecution presented as "consciousness of guilt."

They were tried at Dedham, Massachusetts, from May 31 to July 14, 1921, before Judge Webster Thayer, on charges of first-degree murder. A jury convicted them. [CITE: commonwealth-v-sacco-vanzetti-trial-record-1921] Seven motions for a new trial, all heard by Thayer himself, ran from 1921 through 1924. After the condemned killer Celestino Madeiros confessed in 1925 that the Morelli gang of Providence had done the South Braintree job, a final motion was denied by Thayer in October 1926. On April 9, 1927, Thayer sentenced them to death. Governor Alvan T. Fuller appointed an advisory committee, chaired by the Harvard president A. Lawrence Lowell. The Lowell Committee report of July 27, 1927, endorsed the verdict. [CITE: lowell-committee-report-1927-07-27] Both men were electrocuted at Charlestown State Prison shortly after midnight on August 23, 1927.

Three things are documented in the record of that trial, all of them by sources the rest of Massachusetts's own establishment eventually accepted. First, the trial was conducted in the middle of the First Red Scare, in a state whose political class was hunting Italian anarchists in the months after Luigi Galleani's deportation and the bombing of Attorney General A. Mitchell Palmer's home. [CITE: avrich-1991-sacco-vanzetti-anarchist-background] Second, Judge Thayer's extrajudicial statements about the defendants — reproduced in affidavits including one from the writer Robert Benchley, who recorded that Thayer had referred to them at the Worcester Country Club as "those anarchist bastards" — established a record of bias the appellate process never let touch the verdict, because the appellate process in Massachusetts at the time vested every post-trial motion in the original trial judge. Felix Frankfurter, then a Harvard Law School professor, set out the record of that bias in *The Atlantic Monthly* in March 1927. [CITE: frankfurter-1927-atlantic-sacco-vanzetti] Third, the documentary case for the actual robbers — the Morelli gang, named in the Madeiros confession and corroborated through the chain of evidence the historian Paul Avrich reconstructed in 1991, with the ballistics record re-examined by William Young and David Kaiser in their 1985 study *Postmortem: New Evidence in the Case of Sacco and Vanzetti* — was available to the state by 1925 and acted on by no one. [CITE: avrich-1991-sacco-vanzetti-anarchist-background; young-kaiser-1985-postmortem-sacco-vanzetti]

The state itself eventually said as much. On July 19, 1977 — fifty years to the day, almost, after the trial's last verdict — Governor Michael S. Dukakis issued a proclamation declaring that the trial of Sacco and Vanzetti was "permeated by prejudice against foreigners and hostility toward unorthodox political views" and that "any disgrace should be forever removed from their names." [CITE: dukakis-proclamation-sacco-vanzetti-1977-07-19] The proclamation stopped short of vacating the conviction, which would have required judicial action that has never come. It is one of the rare instances we will encounter in which the record controller — the sovereign of the jurisdiction that produced the laundering — turned on itself, in writing, on the record, fifty years late.

What Sacco and Vanzetti install is what we call the **pure scapegoat**. It is the move in which the actor publicly named as the cause of a harm did not, in any meaningful evidentiary sense, produce the harm — and the naming protects, or covers, or substitutes for, the actor who did. It is the oldest of the four moves and the one we are least likely to contest. We install it here, in 1920s Massachusetts, because the documentary record is unusually full and because the state's own retraction is unusually explicit. The category is most useful when we can carry it forward without having to relitigate the case. Sacco and Vanzetti is offered for the category, not for a verdict on Sacco.

## Record hardens — Abu Ghraib

The third category is harder. In it, the publicly named actor is not innocent. The publicly named actor really did the documented act. The laundering happens above the named actor — at the level of the chain of command, the doctrine, the policy — and the legitimate prosecution of the lower-rank actor is treated as if it had completed the accounting for everyone above.

In late April 2004, Rebecca Leung's segment on CBS's *60 Minutes II* on April 28 and Seymour M. Hersh's "Torture at Abu Ghraib" in *The New Yorker* — posted online on April 30 and dated May 10 — published photographs taken inside Tier 1A of the Abu Ghraib prison in Iraq. [CITE: cbs-60-minutes-ii-hersh-new-yorker-abu-ghraib-2004] The photographs showed US Army military police posing with stripped, hooded, and restrained detainees. They included the hooded detainee on a box with wires attached to his hands; the human pyramid; dog-threat scenes; forced-nudity tableaux. They circulated in the Arab press within hours. They became the defining images of the United States' occupation of Iraq.

On May 7, 2004, Secretary of Defense Donald Rumsfeld told the Senate Armed Services Committee that the abuse was the work of a small number of soldiers acting outside their orders. President George W. Bush echoed the framing publicly. The phrase that lasted was "a few bad apples." [CITE: rumsfeld-sasc-testimony-abu-ghraib-2004-05-07] Eleven soldiers of the 372nd Military Police Company were court-martialed. Specialist Charles Graner was sentenced to ten years on January 15, 2005. Private First Class Lynndie England was sentenced to three years on September 26, 2005. Staff Sergeant Ivan Frederick was convicted on a plea on October 21, 2004 and sentenced to eight years. [CITE: abu-ghraib-courts-martial-and-karpinski-demotion-2004-2005] The convictions of the night-shift soldiers were real. They had taken the photographs. They had done the documented acts.

Above them, the chain of approvals that produced the conditions of Tier 1A was visible in documents that the executive branch held back from public view until 2009. The Office of Legal Counsel issued a memorandum on August 1, 2002. Its authors were Jay Bybee and John Yoo. It redefined the legal definition of torture as a matter of US domestic interpretation. [CITE: bybee-yoo-olc-torture-memos-2002-opr-2009] Secretary Rumsfeld approved an expanded list of interrogation techniques for use at the Guantanamo Bay detention facility on December 2, 2002. He rescinded the approval on January 15, 2003. He reissued a reduced list on April 16, 2003. Major General Geoffrey Miller, the Guantanamo commander, visited Iraq from August 31 to September 9, 2003. According to the AR 15-6 inquiry by Major General Antonio Taguba and the report of the Independent Panel chaired by James Schlesinger, Miller recommended "Gitmo-izing" Abu Ghraib's interrogation operations. Lieutenant General Ricardo Sanchez, the CJTF-7 commander, signed an interrogation and counter-resistance policy on September 14, 2003. The subsequent Fay/Jones AR 15-6 investigation found that the policy had created the doctrinal ambiguity that migrated into military police behavior. The Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence, then headed by Stephen Cambone, was identified in the Senate Armed Services Committee Inquiry as having pressed for expanded interrogation authorities. The 205th Military Intelligence Brigade at Abu Ghraib exercised effective operational control over Tier 1A. Colonel Thomas Pappas commanded the brigade; Lieutenant Colonel Steven Jordan ran the joint interrogation cell. [CITE: sasc-detainee-treatment-report-2008-11-20; taguba-schlesinger-fay-jones-reports-2004]

The Taguba Report, completed on March 9, 2004 and leaked in early May, found "systemic and illegal abuse" and named command failures above the night shift. [CITE: taguba-schlesinger-fay-jones-reports-2004] The Schlesinger Panel, reporting on August 24, 2004, concluded that there had been "fundamental failures throughout all levels of command" and traced the migration of techniques from Guantanamo. [CITE: taguba-schlesinger-fay-jones-reports-2004] The Senate Armed Services Committee Inquiry, chaired by Senator Carl Levin and Ranking Member John McCain and dated November 20, 2008, declassified on April 21, 2009, concluded — at Conclusion 19 — that senior officials' decisions had "conveyed the message that physical pressures and degradation were appropriate treatment for detainees." [CITE: sasc-detainee-treatment-report-2008-11-20] The International Committee of the Red Cross's February 2004 confidential report, leaked to the *Wall Street Journal* in May, had documented systematic mistreatment to the Coalition authorities before the photographs surfaced. [CITE: icrc-iraq-detainees-report-2004-02] The Office of Professional Responsibility report on the Bybee-Yoo memoranda, released February 19, 2010, found professional misconduct; that finding was overridden in the Margolis memorandum of the same period without rescinding the underlying factual record. [CITE: bybee-yoo-olc-torture-memos-2002-opr-2009]

No senior actor in that chain was criminally prosecuted. Brigadier General Janis Karpinski, the 800th Military Police Brigade commander, was demoted to colonel and relieved in May 2005 — a partial climb that stopped well below the doctrine layer. [CITE: karpinski-reduction-in-grade-order-2005-05] The bottom of the chain visibly bore the convictions; the top of the chain produced doctrine, expressed regret, retired or was promoted, and continued.

What Abu Ghraib installs is what we call the **partial scapegoat**. It is the move in which the legitimate prosecution of the lower-rank actor is treated as if it had completed accountability for the chain above. The convicted soldiers' guilt is not in dispute. What is in dispute is whether their court-martial sentences exhausted the responsibility for the conditions under which the photographs were taken. The documentary record, harden as it did across five years of classification, says they did not.

The classification itself is the second-order laundering. By the time the Senate Armed Services Committee Inquiry was declassified, the public attention had moved on. The record-hardening across the five-year delay let the partial scapegoats look complete enough for long enough that the upstream actors aged out of the prosecutorial window. The record was sealed not to protect the soldiers it documented but to delay the public's reading of the chain it documented.

## Goat resists — the Russell counter-reading

An honest treatment of the pure-scapegoat category cannot leave Sacco and Vanzetti without saying that there is a serious within-case counter-reading, and that the strongest version of the install must face it before asserting the category.

In *Tragedy in Dedham* (1962) and in his revised conclusion in *Sacco and Vanzetti: The Case Resolved* (1986), the historian Francis Russell argued, after working with ballistics evidence including the 1961 re-tests conducted by a Boston Globe-Selectron consortium, that Sacco — though not Vanzetti — fired the fatal shot at South Braintree. [CITE: russell-1962-1986-sacco-vanzetti-counter-reading] Subsequent ballistics scholarship has contested Russell's conclusion, and the 1961 re-tests themselves rest on a chain of custody that has been challenged in academic review. The point, however, is not whether Russell was right. The point is that the strongest reading of Sacco and Vanzetti that would reclassify the case is a reading in which our category would split: pure scapegoat for Vanzetti, partial scapegoat for Sacco.

We do not adopt that split here, for two reasons. The first is that the question the case asks the taxonomy is not "did Sacco shoot someone, in some incident, ever?" The question is whether the South Braintree conviction was supported by evidence that would survive a non-prejudicial trial. The Frankfurter essay of March 1927, the Avrich chain of evidence published in 1991, the Young and Kaiser ballistics documentation published in 1985, the Lowell Committee record, the Bullard editorial in the *Boston Herald* of October 26, 1926, and the Dukakis proclamation of 1977 together establish that on the specific charge for which they were executed the evidence was insufficient and the process was prejudicial. [CITE: frankfurter-1927-atlantic-sacco-vanzetti; avrich-1991-sacco-vanzetti-anarchist-background; young-kaiser-1985-postmortem-sacco-vanzetti; lowell-committee-report-1927-07-27; bullard-1926-boston-herald-we-submit; dukakis-proclamation-sacco-vanzetti-1977-07-19] That is the condition our pure-scapegoat category names.

The second reason is structural and worth saying out loud. We are installing four categories at once; we cannot also re-prove every case within them. The honest treatment of the Russell counter-reading is to name it here, in the slot for resistance to our classification, and to hold the partial-scapegoat reading of Sacco as the strongest alternative the category will permit. The category installs cleanly; the case stays within it; we are not asked to take on faith something the record contests. We weaken the unconditional reading of the category, not the case, and let the case carry the taxonomy rather than asking the taxonomy to carry the case. Later chapters that depend on pure-scapegoat classification will name the cases for which the documentary record supports the unconditional reading and the cases, like this one, where we are asked to carry a partially-contested install.

That is, in compact form, what we will do when other anchor cases are brought under taxonomy pressure. The taxonomy is a diagnostic, not a verdict. It names the move that produced the visible blame; it does not pre-decide what other evidence, weighed against other questions, will produce.

## Alibi weakens — machine forensics and procedural cycling

The system/object alibi can be made to give way at the machine layer. It cannot, on the available record, be made to give way at the procedural layer in the same fashion. This is our most important asymmetry, and the cases that produced it are both already in view.

At the machine layer, the Therac-25 alibi gave way to engineering forensics paired with an institutional fact-finder. In April 1986, the East Texas physicist Fritz Hager reproduced the keystroke sequence with the AECL field engineer in the treatment room. In 1993, Nancy Leveson and Clark Turner published "An Investigation of the Therac-25 Accidents" in *IEEE Computer*, identifying the race condition between the Treat task and the keyboard-handler task on shared state variables, and re-publishing the full reconstruction in *Safeware* in 1995. [CITE: leveson-turner-therac-25-ieee-1993] The FDA's Class I recall and the Corrective Action Plan of February 1987 forced AECL into a comprehensive hardware-and-software redesign — including, this time, the reinstatement of the independent hardware interlocks that the Therac-25 had removed from its predecessor models. [CITE: fda-therac-25-recall-and-cap-1986-1987] The artifact, opened to independent reverse-engineering and to institutional fact-finding, surrendered its alibi. The engineering forensics survived the institutional effort to keep them inside AECL's "could not" framing. The alibi collapsed.

At the procedural layer, the same kind of collapse did not happen — or, more accurately, the alibi re-formed each time it was forced to collapse. On January 7, 2021, Boeing entered into a deferred prosecution agreement with the United States Department of Justice. The case was filed in the Northern District of Texas, before Judge Reed O'Connor. The DPA's Statement of Facts, paragraphs 1 through 63, set out the conduct. Two of Boeing's 737 Chief Technical Pilots — Mark Forkner and Patrik Gustavsson — were named in the Statement of Facts as having allegedly deceived the FAA's Aircraft Evaluation Group about the operational scope of MCAS. Boeing itself admitted, in the same Statement of Facts, to a conspiracy to defraud the United States. The corporation paid two and a half billion dollars. The total broke into three parts: $243.6 million as a criminal monetary penalty; $1.77 billion in compensation to airline customers; and $500 million into a beneficiaries' fund for crash victims. [CITE: boeing-737-max-dpa-2021]

Forkner was tried, on four counts of wire fraud. He was acquitted by a jury in March 2022. [CITE: us-v-forkner-2022-acquittal] Gustavsson was not personally charged. Muilenburg was not personally charged. The one named individual to face trial was acquitted; the corporation had admitted the conduct; the named decisions inside Boeing engineering and management that had produced the single-sensor architecture, the 2016 expansion of MCAS authority, and the omission of MCAS from the pilot training package never reached individual federal prosecution to verdict.

On May 14, 2024, the Department of Justice notified the court that Boeing had breached the 2021 DPA. The Department cited the company's failure to "design, implement, and enforce a compliance and ethics program to prevent and detect violations of the U.S. fraud laws throughout its operations." [CITE: doj-boeing-dpa-breach-letter-2024-05-14] The breach finding followed the January 2024 Alaska Airlines Flight 1282 door-plug blowout. That event lay outside the DPA's covered conduct, but the Department cited it as evidence of continued failure. Boeing then agreed, in July 2024, to a plea agreement on the same fraud conspiracy charge. In December 2024, Judge O'Connor rejected the plea agreement on grounds related to the independent compliance monitor selection. [CITE: oconnor-order-rejecting-boeing-plea-2024-12 — pending-stephen-lock] After the January 2025 change of administration, the Department of Justice replaced the plea with a non-prosecution agreement. The agreement was announced on May 23, 2025. Under it, Boeing committed to more than $1.1 billion in additional payments. The total criminal monetary penalty was raised to $487.2 million; the $243.6 million paid by Boeing in 2021 was credited against that figure. A new victim-family fund received $444.5 million from Boeing. Boeing committed an additional sum of roughly $445 million to compliance, safety, and quality investments. [CITE: doj-boeing-npa-resolution-2025-05-23] Victim families brought an appeal under the Crime Victims' Rights Act. On March 31, 2026, the Fifth Circuit ruled on that appeal in *Ryan v. United States*. The court held that the 2024 breach had mooted the DPA challenge, and that the CVRA did not entitle victims to an unlimited right to appeal the Rule 48(a) dismissal of the non-prosecution agreement. [CITE: ryan-v-united-states-5th-cir-2026-03-31 — pending-stephen-lock] The procedural shell, having cracked on its own terms in 2024, re-formed in 2025 and was sustained on appeal in 2026.

The asymmetry is the lesson. Machine-layer alibis collapse to engineering forensics plus a willing fact-finder; the forensics, once produced, are the record. Procedural-layer alibis cycle. A deferred prosecution agreement becomes a breached agreement becomes a rejected plea becomes a non-prosecution agreement becomes a Fifth Circuit ruling, and at every stage the procedural form is the named bearer of responsibility while the named individuals inside the corporation are progressively absorbed into it. The alibi can climb the institutional stack; it can also recurse inside any one floor of the stack, replacing itself with a near-identical version under a new procedural label. The pattern this seeds — at the corporate-form layer, at the federal-prosecution layer, and, in later chapters, at the algorithmic-deployment layer — is one of the shapes we will test against new material.

## Anti-laundering rule — cost-bearing language and the doctrine boundary

The fourth category does not need a person to be named as the cause. Its laundering is done by language, and its cost is borne by an actor who is not accused of anything.

On May 30, 2022, three months and six days into the full-scale Russian invasion of Ukraine, President Vladimir Vladimirovich Putin signed Decree No. 330. The decree simplified the path to Russian citizenship for orphans and unaccompanied minors from the Donetsk and Luhansk people's republics and from Ukraine. On July 11, 2022, the President signed Decree No. 585. That decree expanded the simplification to all Ukrainian citizens. [CITE: russian-decrees-330-585-ukrainian-children-2022] The two decrees were the legal scaffolding for a practice that the Russian government, in its own state media, described in the language of care: "evacuation," "humanitarian corridor," "guardianship," "rescue from a war zone," "citizenship for orphans." Maria Alekseyevna Lvova-Belova, the Russian Presidential Commissioner for Children's Rights, has spoken publicly about the practice on Russian state media. In 2022, she stated on RIA Novosti and TASS that she had taken into her family a Ukrainian boy from Mariupol, identified by Russian state media as "Filipp." Russian state media presented the placement as an act of personal care. [CITE: putin-lvova-belova-public-defence-of-transfers-2022-2023]

That state-media framing is the alibi. We report that Lvova-Belova said these things; we do not adopt her framing as our own. The framing presents the transfer of Ukrainian children into the territory of an occupying state, the change of their legal status and citizenship, and their placement with Russian foster families as the kind of care that an absent parent would want for a child in a war zone. International humanitarian law disagrees, and the disagreement is our anti-laundering rule.

Article 78 of Additional Protocol I to the Geneva Conventions of 1949 forbids the evacuation of children of a party to a conflict to a foreign country, except temporarily, except where compelling reasons of health or of safety require it, and except with the written consent of the parents or legal guardians where they can be found. The article also requires that the child's identification be preserved, that education be in the child's own language and culture, and that the child be returned when conditions allow. Article 78(1) expressly excludes occupied territory from the safety-grounds permission, foreclosing the carve-out the Russian framing requires. [CITE: api-1977-art-78-child-evacuation] Article 49 of the Fourth Geneva Convention prohibits the deportation or transfer of protected persons from occupied territory to the territory of the occupying power. [CITE: gciv-1949-art-49-deportation-prohibition] Article II(e) of the 1948 Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide prohibits "forcibly transferring children of the group to another group" as one of five enumerated acts. [CITE: genocide-convention-1948-art-ii-e] Article 7 of the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child gives the child the right to know and be cared for by his or her parents. Article 8 protects the child's identity, including nationality and family relations. [CITE: un-crc-1989-arts-7-8]

On March 17, 2023, the International Criminal Court's Pre-Trial Chamber II issued arrest warrants against Vladimir Vladimirovich Putin and Maria Alekseyevna Lvova-Belova. The Chamber found reasonable grounds to believe that each bears individual criminal responsibility for two charges: the war crime of unlawful deportation of population (children), and the war crime of unlawful transfer of population (children). The charges were brought under Articles 8(2)(a)(vii) and 8(2)(b)(viii) of the Rome Statute. [CITE: icc-arrest-warrants-putin-lvova-belova-2023-03-17] The case is at the warrant stage as of May 2026. No trial has occurred. Russia does not recognize the International Criminal Court's jurisdiction. Both Putin and Lvova-Belova have publicly denied the allegations. The Ukrainian government maintains a "Children of War" registry. As of May 25, 2026, that registry recorded more than 20,000 children identified as deported or forcibly transferred. The Yale School of Public Health's Humanitarian Research Lab, working with the State Department–funded Conflict Observatory, has documented a network of receiving facilities in the Russian Federation. Their report sequence began on February 14, 2023, and has continued through 2026. [CITE: ukraine-children-of-war-registry-2026-05-25; yale-hrl-conflict-observatory-ukrainian-children-2023-02-14] The Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights has also documented forced-transfer cases. Its reporting runs through periodic releases from its Human Rights Monitoring Mission in Ukraine. [CITE: ohchr-hrmmu-ukraine-periodic-reports-2022-2026] Returns brokered by Qatar, by the Holy See, by Save Ukraine, and by Ukrainian non-governmental organizations have brought back several hundred children. The great majority of registered cases remain unreturned. Yale HRL and OHCHR, in those reports, describe the program as operationally directed at the highest levels of the Russian state. The characterization is theirs. [CITE: yale-hrl-conflict-observatory-ukrainian-children-2023-02-14; ohchr-hrmmu-ukraine-periodic-reports-2022-2026]

This is what we call the **cost-bearing goat**. The harm-bearer — the child — is not publicly accused of anything. There is no scapegoat in the conventional sense. The responsibility is laundered into euphemism and bureaucracy: the decree, the citizenship registry, the foster placement, the on-camera statement of "personal care." The category names the move; the international-humanitarian-law instruments are the bright lines the move is trying to blur.

What our four cases — across radiation therapy, civil aviation, military detention, and the forced transfer of children — share is something narrower than a unified theory of blame. The chain of responsibility stops, in the cases we have examined, at the procedural-stage boundary that the available doctrine cannot cross. At Abu Ghraib, the United States military justice system reached the night-shift soldiers under the Uniform Code of Military Justice. The doctrinal frame that would have reached the chain of command — Article 87 of Additional Protocol I, command-responsibility doctrine, the Rome Statute's exclusion of the superior-orders defense for the gravest war crimes — was not the frame used inside the United States legal apparatus, in part because the United States has not ratified the Rome Statute and in part because the Uniform Code of Military Justice's command-liability doctrine has, in the *Calley* line, set a high bar for prosecution that the available legal advice from the Office of Legal Counsel arguably reinforced. [CITE: api-1977-art-87-commander-duties; rome-statute-arts-6-8-28-33; us-v-calley-1973-cmaa] The chain stopped at the boundary of the available doctrine. At the International Criminal Court, the prosecutor's choice at the warrant stage was Article 8 of the Rome Statute — the war-crimes articles on unlawful deportation and transfer — not Article 6(e), the genocide article on forcibly transferring children of the group. The chain stopped at the article the evidence at the warrant stage supports, not at the gravest applicable category. At the warrant stage, the Article 8 charge clears the *dolus specialis* threshold that Article 6(e) would require. [CITE: rome-statute-arts-6-8-28-33; icc-arrest-warrants-putin-lvova-belova-2023-03-17] In Australia, as the next beat will show, the *Bringing Them Home* finding that the removal policy met the criteria of Article II(e) of the Genocide Convention was rejected by the Howard Government and was not adopted by the Rudd National Apology. [CITE: bringing-them-home-1997] The chain stopped at the legal-category recognition the political system could absorb.

The chapter's anti-laundering rule, then — earned from the cases it has examined and not yet asserted as universal — is that in each of the four cases this chapter has examined, the chain of responsibility stops at the procedural-stage boundary the available doctrine cannot cross. Where doctrine reaches, the chain climbs. Where doctrine does not reach, the chain stops, and the cost-bearer absorbs the rest. The work of an anti-laundering rule, properly understood, is the work of extending doctrine to the chain that control, benefit, knowledge, and preventability already point at.

## The escape — four interceptions and four costs

There are documented cases in which the same kind of laundering, in the same kind of institutional setting, has been intercepted. The chapter ends with four of them, paired one-for-one with the four anchor cases above. The point of the pairing is not optimism. The point is to name, with precision, the institutional preconditions that produced the interception and the cost that was paid for it — and to name, with equal precision, where the lesson does not generalize back to the anchor case.

**The pure scapegoat, intercepted.** On October 15, 1894, Captain Alfred Dreyfus, the only Jewish officer on the French General Staff, was arrested on charges of espionage based on a torn document — the *bordereau* — recovered from a wastepaper basket at the German Embassy in Paris. He was court-martialed and convicted in December, publicly degraded at the École Militaire on January 5, 1895, and sent to Devil's Island. [CITE: dreyfus-conseil-de-guerre-conviction-1894] In March 1896, Lieutenant-Colonel Georges Picquart, newly appointed head of the Statistical Section, compared the bordereau's handwriting to that on a *petit bleu* intercepted from another officer, Major Charles Esterhazy, and concluded that Esterhazy, not Dreyfus, was the spy. Picquart was reassigned to Tunisia and later imprisoned. Émile Zola, on January 13, 1898, published "J'accuse...!" on the front page of *L'Aurore*, naming the General Staff officers responsible for the laundering. [CITE: zola-jaccuse-laurore-1898-01-13] Zola was convicted of libel and fled to England; he died in 1902 of carbon-monoxide poisoning under circumstances historians still contest. On July 12, 1906, the Cour de Cassation, sitting as the *Chambres réunies*, quashed the 1899 Rennes retrial conviction without remand and exonerated Dreyfus. [CITE: cour-de-cassation-dreyfus-exoneration-1906-07-12] The lesson is documentary preservation: the Statistical Section's archive of intercepted documents survived the institution's effort to bury the case it had built. The cost of escape was twelve years of Dreyfus's life, Devil's Island, Picquart's imprisonment, Zola's exile, and the formation of *Action Française* in 1899. The limit of the lesson is the Cour de Cassation: Massachusetts had no equivalent court willing to vacate the Sacco-Vanzetti conviction, and the Dukakis proclamation of 1977 explicitly declined to require it.

**The partial scapegoat, intercepted.** Between 1966 and 1970, the New York Police Department patrolman Frank Serpico documented payoffs in the Seventh, Ninth, and Eighty-First precincts and attempted to report through the Chief Inspector's office, the Mayor's Department of Investigation, and Mayor John V. Lindsay's office; each report produced reassignment rather than investigation. Sergeant David Durk corroborated the reports and eventually identified a path out of the department to the *New York Times* reporter David Burnham, whose front-page story of April 25, 1970 — "Graft Paid to Police Here Said to Run Into Millions" — forced the political response. [CITE: burnham-nyt-1970-04-25-graft-paid-to-police] On May 21, 1970, Mayor Lindsay signed Executive Order No. 11 establishing the Knapp Commission under Whitman Knapp. The Commission's public hearings of October and December 1971 and its Final Report of December 27, 1972 documented the pad system as departmental, distinguished "meat eaters" from "grass eaters," and rejected the bad-apples framing in operative language. [CITE: lindsay-executive-order-11-knapp-commission-1970-05-21] The Office of the Special Prosecutor for Anti-Corruption was established in 1972. On February 3, 1971, during a narcotics entry at 778 Driggs Avenue in Williamsburg, Serpico was shot in the face; the conduct of the backing officers, Patrolmen Gary Roteman and Arthur Cesare, was reviewed and not charged; Serpico has publicly disputed the official finding. The Mollen Commission of 1992 through 1994 documented the recurrence of similar patterns inside the same department a generation later. [CITE: mollen-commission-report-1994-07-07] The Office of the Special Prosecutor was defunded by Governor Mario Cuomo in 1990. [CITE: cuomo-eo-144-special-prosecutor-termination-1990] The cost of escape was Serpico's face, Durk's career, and the closure of the oversight architecture that Knapp had built. The limit of the lesson is structural: the partial-scapegoat laundering at Abu Ghraib had no Lindsay-equivalent appointer, because the appointing authority was the principal of the chain Knapp's mechanism would have investigated. The Schlesinger Panel, the Fay/Jones Report, and the SASC Inquiry were all instituted within the misconduct authority's own chain. None had the structural distance the Knapp interception required.

**The system/object alibi, intercepted.** Between 1999 and 2015, the United Kingdom Post Office, acting as a private prosecutor, brought criminal cases against more than 700 sub-postmasters and sub-postmistresses on the basis of accounting shortfalls displayed by the Horizon IT system supplied by Fujitsu. Sub-postmasters were convicted of theft, false accounting, and fraud on the basis of computer outputs whose internal behavior only Fujitsu could examine. In May 2009, the *Computer Weekly* reporter Rebecca Thomson assembled the dispersed accounts into a recognizable pattern; the Justice for Subpostmasters Alliance, founded by Alan Bates the same month, coordinated the dispersed community across the next decade. [CITE: thomson-2009-computer-weekly-horizon] The High Court of England and Wales granted a Group Litigation Order in March 2017. On December 16, 2019, Mr Justice Peter Fraser handed down the *Horizon Issues* judgment, finding the Horizon system "not remotely robust" on the basis of the Known Error Log and Fujitsu engineering records that the litigation's disclosure phase had compelled. [CITE: bates-v-post-office-no-6-horizon-issues-2019] The claimants' technical expert, Jason Coyne, had reverse-engineered the error logs. On April 23, 2021, the Court of Appeal in *Hamilton & Others v Post Office Ltd* quashed thirty-nine convictions, describing the prosecutions as an "affront to the conscience of the Court." [CITE: hamilton-v-post-office-2021-court-of-appeal; hamilton-v-post-office-affront-to-conscience-2021] The Post Office (Horizon System) Offences Act 2024 received Royal Assent on May 24, 2024. [CITE: post-office-horizon-system-offences-act-2024] At least four sub-postmasters' suicides have been documented as connected to the prosecutions; the precise count remains part of the Post Office Horizon IT Inquiry's continuing reconstruction. The cost of escape was twenty-five years; hundreds of sub-postmasters dead before exoneration; Bates's unpaid decade; the litigation funder Therium's substantial share of the 2019 settlement. The limit of the lesson — and the chapter must say this explicitly — is that the ITV drama *Mr Bates vs The Post Office*, broadcast January 1 to 4, 2024, was the amplifier, not the load-bearing interception. The load-bearing interception was Fraser J's December 2019 judgment, made possible by class action and independent reverse-engineering. Conflating the drama with the legal finding produces a misleading lesson: that storytelling pierces system/object alibi. It does not. Engineering forensics paired with a court of disclosure does.

**The cost-bearing goat, intercepted.** Between approximately the 1910s and the 1970s, under each Australian state and territory's so-called "protection" legislation, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children were forcibly removed from their families and placed in state and church-run institutions; the *Bringing Them Home* report of May 26, 1997 found that "between one in three and one in ten" Indigenous children had been removed between 1910 and 1970. [CITE: bringing-them-home-1997] The institutional sequence by which Australia eventually arrived at recognition began outside the state. Coral Edwards and Peter Read founded Link-Up in New South Wales in 1980; Read's 1981 monograph for the NSW Ministry of Aboriginal Affairs introduced the term "Stolen Generations" into the formal record. [CITE: read-1981-stolen-generations-monograph] The Secretariat of National Aboriginal and Islander Child Care was established in 1981. The Human Rights and Equal Opportunity Commission, under the Keating Government, established the National Inquiry on August 11, 1995; *Bringing Them Home* was tabled in Parliament under the Howard Government on May 26, 1997 and compiled testimony from 535 Indigenous people. On February 13, 2008, Prime Minister Kevin Rudd delivered the National Apology in the House of Representatives. [CITE: rudd-national-apology-2008-02-13] The Commonwealth Territories Stolen Generations Redress Scheme opened on March 1, 2022. [CITE: cw-territories-stolen-generations-redress-2022-03-01] The cost of escape was approximately a century from earliest practice to operational reparation; many eligible survivors died before assessment; descendant claims are largely excluded; the *Bringing Them Home* finding that the policy met the criteria of Article II(e) of the Genocide Convention was not adopted by the Howard Government and was not adopted by the Rudd apology; many of the Link-Up founders did not see the apology. The limit of the lesson — and the chapter is required to say this — is that the political conditions that produced the Australian sequence are not available in the Russian case. There is no Russian HREOC. There is no Russian-led civil-society infrastructure inside the Russian Federation capable of assembling the testimony from the cost-bearing community. There is no foreseeable head-of-government recognition. The Ukrainian children case is at an earlier stage of a sequence whose later stages took a century to reach in the most institutionally favorable conditions on record. The Stolen Generations is offered here for diagnosis of the time scale and of the institutional preconditions, not as a prescription for an achievable equivalent. The cost-bearing goat resolves, when it resolves at all, on a generational rather than political timescale, and only when the responsible authority's own records are eventually opened.

Four interceptions. Four costs of escape. Four limits where the lesson does not transfer. The chapter does not claim that laundering is reversible. The chapter claims that laundering is sometimes reversible; that the reversal takes generations more often than years; that the cost of escape falls disproportionately on either the cost-bearing community or the intercepting actor; and that absent continuing institutional architecture the laundering recurs. The pattern Knapp documented at the NYPD recurred under Mollen within twenty years. The Office of the Special Prosecutor was closed in 1990. The Horizon Inquiry's final report has not yet been delivered. The institutional architecture that produced each interception is, in each case, more fragile than the laundering it intercepted.

## What this might mean for us

**Recognize it.** You are inside one of the four shapes whenever the person publicly blamed for a harm does not match the person who controlled the decision that produced it. The pure scapegoat is someone blamed who did not do the thing. The partial scapegoat is someone blamed who did the thing but is not the highest controller of it. The system/object alibi names a tool, a piece of software, a corporation, or a procedure — not a person — as the cause. The cost-bearing goat is someone bearing the harm under language that describes the harm as care. You will meet these shapes in your workplace's "the system flagged you" denial letters; in the news framing of a corporate harm where the named cause is "the algorithm" or "the process"; in political language that moves children, workers, or vulnerable people under euphemism; in the court-martial of the patrol officer with no parallel proceeding for the lieutenant.

**Diagnose it.** Three markers, taught here and used across the rest of the book. First: the publicly named cause is mechanically plausible, but the named human decision-makers behind it are missing from the same sentence. Second: the institutional response is procedural — "we revised our process," "we entered into a DPA," "we issued a service bulletin" — rather than personnel-level — "X is no longer authorized to approve Y." Third: the cost-bearer's name appears in the public account, but the controller's name does not. These markers together raise the question the book has been asking from its first page: where, in the visible account, has control gone missing? Where you see all three, you are looking at the conditions under which a laundering pattern — as this book defines one — typically forms. Where you see two of the three, you are likely looking at a partial form, and the next chapter's eight-question diagnostic refines the test. The book offers these markers as a working heuristic, not as a theorem; the chapters that follow apply them and test them against new material.

**Act on it.** Three roles, three actions; pick the role you are in. As the cost-bearer or the visible blamed party, when a procedural authority is cited against you, ask in writing which named person made the decision that the procedure produced. The reply is the seam. If no name comes back, the procedure is the alibi. As the bystander — the colleague, the juror, the consumer of journalism, the citizen reading a recall notice — when the named cause is a thing ("the model," "the system," "the process"), ask who deployed the thing, who profited from its deployment, and who had the authority to stop it. Those three names are the responsibility chain. The thing is not the cause. As the institutional actor with some power — the manager, the regulator, the journalist, the editor, the engineer with a sign-off — refuse to sign or publish a procedural disposition that names no individual where individuals had control. The signature is the laundering instrument. Withhold it, or sign with a written note that records who else was in the chain.

**Avoid becoming it.** You are being positioned as the goat when you are asked to sign for, take responsibility for, or "own" an outcome whose controlling decisions were made above you and whose record you cannot inspect. The specific warning signs are these: you are named in a public communication about an event whose internal cause analysis you have not been shown; you are asked to be the spokesperson for a procedural disposition you did not negotiate; your role is described to outsiders in language that elides the chain of approvals that led to your role. When any of these meets a public-facing surface, you are the seam where blame is about to land. The Tyler operator at the Therac-25 console did nothing wrong and was nevertheless the first named cause. The Lion Air crew received a runaway-stabilizer procedure that could not save them and was nevertheless the first named cause. The bottom of the chain at Abu Ghraib did the documented acts and bore the convictions without changing the conditions under which the acts were done. The cost-bearing child in a Russian foster placement under a citizenship decree is not accused of anything, and is nevertheless absorbing the cost of a decision made above her. Blame answers the question the institution wants to close. Responsibility answers the questions of control, benefit, knowledge, and preventability. The next chapter is the eight questions that pull them apart.
