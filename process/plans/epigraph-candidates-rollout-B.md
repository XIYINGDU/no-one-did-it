## Chapter 6 — The Pretext
**Diagnostic:** the cited authority (statute/procedure) named as the agent; the stated reason covers the real purpose; the named invoker written out of the sentence.

### 6A (RECOMMENDED)
> "The tyrant will always find a pretext for his tyranny."
- **Aesop / Greek fable tradition / moral as rendered by George Fyler Townsend, 1867.** "The Wolf and the Lamb."
- **Source:** *Aesop's Fables*, Townsend translation (public domain; Project Gutenberg #21), closing moral of "The Wolf and the Lamb."
- **Confidence:** HIGH (byte-confirmed against Gutenberg Townsend text this pass).
- **Why apt:** This *is* the pretext, named. The wolf invents a charge to license a predetermined act; the lamb's refutations are irrelevant because the reason was never the cause. Exactly ch-6's gap between cited cause and documented purpose, in one line, public-domain, audio-survivable.

### 6B (ALT — Chinese, cultural breadth)
> "If names be not correct, language is not in accordance with the truth of things."
- **Confucius / Chinese / *Analects* 13.3 / James Legge translation, 1893.**
- **Source:** *Analects*, Book 13, ch. 3, Legge (public domain; ctext.org).
- **Confidence:** HIGH.
- **Why apt:** Pretext is a names-not-correct failure — the cited authority is a false name for the real purpose. Slightly more oblique than 6A.

**Pick: 6A** — sharpest, names the move directly. 6B if a non-Western pick is wanted here (but 6B is better reserved for ch-13).

## Chapter 7 — The Record Is the Battlefield
**Diagnostic:** the institution holds the only evidence; record-control laundering = structural inaccessibility of what would falsify the official story.

### 7A (RECOMMENDED)
> "Great part of the information obtained in War is contradictory, a still greater part is false, and by far the greatest part is of a doubtful character."
- **Carl von Clausewitz / Prussian / *On War* Bk 1, ch. 6 / J.J. Graham translation, 1873.**
- **Confidence:** HIGH (byte-confirmed this pass).
- **Why apt — MODERATE only:** speaks to evidentiary unreliability, not to *who controls* the record. Offered but NOT preferred; ch-7's move is control, not fog.

### 7B (RECOMMENDED — fits the control move)
> "It would be better to be without the Book of History than to give entire credit to it."
- **Mencius / Chinese / *Mengzi* 7B.3 / James Legge translation, 1895.**
- **Confidence:** HIGH (pilot byte-confirmed; ctext.org).
- **Why apt:** a surviving record is not a true record — read critically. **FLAG: this is the pilot's ch-12 pick.** Do not double-use. Listed to document the overlap.

**Pick: NONE solidly apt-and-unused.** Ch-7's exact move — the accuser holds the only evidence — has no clean public-domain scholar's line I could byte-confirm this pass that isn't already committed to ch-12. **Recommend ch-7 go without** rather than force 7A (only moderate) or steal ch-12's Mencius. Flagged for a targeted second search if the crew wants one (candidate frame to chase: a jurist on *nemo judex in causa sua* — no one a judge in their own cause).

## Chapter 8 — When Power Calls Itself the Goat
**Diagnostic:** power inverts the accusation; the powerful claim victimhood/persecution; the accuser recast as the persecuted.

### 8A (RECOMMENDED)
> "The strong do what they can and the weak suffer what they must."
- **Thucydides / Greek / *History of the Peloponnesian War* Bk 5 (Melian Dialogue) / Richard Crawley translation, 1874.**
- **Source:** Crawley, Bk 5.89 (public domain; Wikisource/Gutenberg).
- **Confidence:** MED — the line is famous and Crawley is public-domain, but Stephen must lift Crawley's *exact* English (translations vary at this line) and confirm 5.89.
- **Why apt:** names raw control behind a moralized surface; the powerful party narrates the encounter as necessity. Strong on power-inversion, somewhat less on the *self-as-victim* turn.

### 8B (RECOMMENDED — closer to the inversion)
> "The tyrant will always find a pretext for his tyranny."
- **Aesop / Townsend 1867 (as 6A).**
- **Confidence:** HIGH.
- **Why apt:** the wolf manufactures a *grievance* — recasts itself as the wronged party against the lamb. That is ch-8's exact inversion (power claiming injury). **FLAG: same line as 6A.** Cannot use in both chapters; if the crew prefers it here, ch-6 takes 6B.

**Pick: 8A (Thucydides/Melian)** for ch-8, leaving the Aesop pretext line to ch-6 — keeps each line single-use and gives ch-8 the power-narrating-necessity register. Stephen to lock Crawley's exact 5.89 wording.

## Chapter 10 — War Is the Perfect Laundry
**Diagnostic:** war as the perfect laundry; the fog/proxy/chain diffuses the decision; "the intelligence was wrong" redescribes a chosen public case as a property of an institution.

### 10A (RECOMMENDED — non-Western, honors the directive)
> "All warfare is based on deception."
- **Sun Tzu / Chinese / *The Art of War* I.18 (Laying Plans) / Lionel Giles translation, 1910.**
- **Confidence:** HIGH (byte-confirmed this pass; Gutenberg Giles).
- **Why apt:** war's foundational text states deception as the medium — the condition under which a decision can be laundered into "the fog." Public-domain, audio-survivable, and the non-Western weight the rollout asked for.

### 10B (ALT — fog-of-responsibility frame)
> "Great part of the information obtained in War is contradictory, a still greater part is false..."
- **Clausewitz / *On War* Bk 1 ch. 6 / Graham 1873 (as 7A).**
- **Confidence:** HIGH.
- **Why apt:** the fog as the alibi-generator — "the intelligence was wrong." Western; offered as alt since ch-10's case is already Western-heavy.

**Pick: 10A (Sun Tzu)** — non-Western, public-domain, exactly on war-as-the-medium-of-the-launder; 10B if the crew prefers the explicit fog-of-information frame.

## Chapter 11 — Make Responsibility Follow Control
**Diagnostic:** the design rule/remedy — make responsibility FOLLOW CONTROL; name the responsible person before the design ships.

### 11A (RECOMMENDED — Chinese Legalist, honors the directive)
> "if the ruler does not insist upon reserving to himself the authority to dispense ... rewards and show his awesome power in punishments, but instead allows his ministers to hand these out, then the people of the state will all fear the ministers and treat the ruler with disrespect."
- **Han Feizi / Chinese Legalist / "The Two Handles" / Burton Watson translation (1964).**
- **Confidence:** MED (wording located via Watson this pass; Stephen to byte-confirm against the Columbia UP edition). **PERMISSIONS FLAG: Watson translation is modern/in-copyright (Columbia UP); clearance required, or substitute a public-domain translation.**
- **Why apt:** the precise ancient inverse of ch-11 — when the authority to reward/punish (control) diffuses away from the named holder, accountability collapses; keep responsibility fixed to the one who holds control. Maps directly onto the SMCR/§302/Park "named officer" design rule.
- **Public-domain fallback:** the same doctrine is in the *Mahabharata*, Shanti Parva (Rajadharma), on *danda* — "kings should stand with the rod of chastisement uplifted in their hands" (Ganguli, 1883–96, public domain). MED; Stephen to locate exact section. Less precise (about punishment authority generally, not responsibility-follows-control), but free and Indian — broadens the set.

**Pick: 11A (Han Feizi)** for aptness; **if permissions are unwanted, switch to the Ganguli/Mahabharata danda line** (public-domain, MED). Either honors the non-Western weighting.

## Chapter 13 — A Reader's Field Guide
**Diagnostic:** the diagnostic handed to the reader; noticing; "we are the escape." Epigraph must be EMPOWERMENT register — noticing / responsibility / the citizen's instrument. NOT grim, NOT near suffering.

### 13A (RECOMMENDED — Chinese, naming-as-instrument)
> "If names be not correct, language is not in accordance with the truth of things. If language be not in accordance with the truth of things, affairs cannot be carried on to success."
- **Confucius / Chinese / *Analects* 13.3 / Legge 1893.**
- **Confidence:** HIGH (byte-confirmed this pass).
- **Why apt:** the field guide's whole instrument is *correcting the name* — putting the invoker back in the sentence, naming the chain. Forward-looking, empowering, instrument-register, not grim. The reader's job stated by Confucius: get the names right and affairs can proceed. Pairs with the book's recurring "name the thing" move.

### 13B (ALT — Western, watch/instrument register)
> "All that is necessary for the triumph of evil is that good men do nothing."
- **Commonly attributed to Edmund Burke.**
- **Confidence:** LOW — **DROP. Misattributed; no verifiable Burke source exists.** Listed only to flag it as a trap to avoid (it is a quote-aggregator staple).

**Pick: 13A (Confucius 13.3)** — empowerment/noticing register, byte-confirmed, public-domain, and exactly the field guide's correct-the-name instrument. No grim alternative needed.
