## ch-1 — the-altar-moves — Manu 8.317 (Sanskrit + Bühler)

**Original (Sanskrit, Devanagari):**
> अन्नादे भ्रूणहा मार्ष्टि पत्यौ भार्याऽपचारिणी ।
> गुरौ शिष्यश्च याज्यश्च स्तेनो राजनि किल्बिषम् ॥ ३१७ ॥

**English (locked, Bühler):**
> The killer of a learned Brāhmaṇa throws his guilt on him who eats his food, an adulterous wife on her (negligent) husband, a (sinning) pupil or sacrificer on (their negligent) teacher (or priest), a thief on the king (who pardons him).

**Attribution:** The Laws of Manu 8.317, trans. Georg Bühler, *Sacred Books of the East*, vol. 25 (1886).

*Notes: Bühler's parenthetical insertions are part of his translation — retain verbatim. Diacritic "Brāhmaṇa" per SBE typography (plain-ASCII "Brahmana" is a permitted house-style normalization, Wayne's call). IAST transliteration if needed: annāde bhrūṇahā mārṣṭi patyau bhāryā'pacāriṇī | gurau śiṣyaśca yājyaśca steno rājani kilbiṣam || 317 ||*

## ch-2 — the-four-goats — Plato, *Phaedrus* 265e (Greek + Jowett) — REPLACES old Confucius/Nyāya pick

**Original (Greek, Burnet 1903):**
> τὸ πάλιν κατ' εἴδη δύνασθαι διατέμνειν κατ' ἄρθρα ᾗ πέφυκεν, καὶ μὴ ἐπιχειρεῖν καταγνύναι μέρος μηδέν, κακοῦ μαγείρου τρόπῳ χρώμενον.

**English (locked, Jowett):**
> The second principle is that of division into species according to the natural formation, where the joint is, not breaking any part as a bad carver might.

**Attribution:** Plato, *Phaedrus* 265e, trans. Benjamin Jowett.

*Notes: Greek lifted from Perseus (Burnet 1903 text), independently corroborated. Jowett English byte-lifted from the Jowett primary text (classics.mit.edu), independently corroborated. Clean cut at the period — no ellipsis. Do NOT substitute the Fowler rendering ("dividing things again by classes, where the natural joints are…after the manner of a bad carver") — that is in-copyright and a different translation; Jowett wording + Jowett attribution are locked together. Greek apostrophe rendered as straight keraia (κατ') to match prior Greek epigraphs in the set; the Unicode-keraia variant (κατ᾽) is textually identical — house-style normalization only.*

## ch-3 — (cicero chapter) — Cicero, *De Officiis* I.23 (Latin + Miller)

**Original (Latin):**
> Qui autem non defendit nec obsistit, si potest, iniuriae, tam est in vitio, quam si parentes aut amicos aut patriam deserat.

**English (locked, Miller):**
> he who does not prevent or oppose wrong, if he can, is just as guilty of wrong as if he deserted his parents or his friends or his country.

**Attribution:** Cicero, *De Officiis* I.23, trans. Walter Miller (Loeb Classical Library, 1913).

*Notes: Latin from The Latin Library §23; final verb deserat (subjunctive) confirmed. Matches Miller English.*

## ch-4 — (machiavelli chapter) — Machiavelli, *Il Principe* VII (Italian + Marriott) — WIKISOURCE orthography

**Original (Italian, Wikisource canonical orthography):**
> E perchè cognosceva le rigorosità passate avergli generato qualche odio, per purgare gli animi di quelli popoli, e guadagnarseli in tutto, volse mostrare che se crudeltà alcuna era seguita, non era nata da lui, ma dall'acerba natura del ministro.

*Notes: Wikisource orthography is authoritative and locked — perchè (grave, not perché) · seguita (no grave on i) · dall'acerba (elided) · comma after popoli; volse/popoli confirmed (not volle/populi).
