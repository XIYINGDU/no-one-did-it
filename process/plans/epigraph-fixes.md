## Chapter 3 — preventability (the one who could have stopped it and didn't bears the guilt)

**Replaces:** the prior Manu 8.316 pick (Manu is already at ch-1; xiaolai: vary ch-3 to a DIFFERENT tradition).

### 3-FIX (RECOMMENDED) — Cicero, *De Officiis* I.23
> "he who does not prevent or oppose wrong, if he can, is just as guilty of wrong as if he deserted his parents or his friends or his country."
- **Author / culture / dates:** Marcus Tullius Cicero (Roman; 106–43 BCE), *De Officiis* (On Duties) Book I, §23.
- **Source / translation:** Walter Miller translation, Loeb Classical Library, 1913 — **public domain**. Byte-located this pass against Project Gutenberg ebook #47001 (Miller) and cross-checkable on Perseus (Cic. Off. 1.23, Latin + Miller English).
- **Confidence:** HIGH (wording surfaced byte-exact against the Miller text this pass; Stephen to lock §23 exactly).
- **Why apt:** This is the preventability doctrine stated outright — Cicero distinguishes the man who *commits* a wrong from the man who *could prevent it and does not*, and rules the second **just as guilty**. That is ch-3's spine: responsibility attaches to whoever held the option to stop the harm and let it run. A Roman/Latin source, public-domain, no Manu, audio-survivable.
