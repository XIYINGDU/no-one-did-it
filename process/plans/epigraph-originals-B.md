## ch-3 — Cicero, *De Officiis* I.23 (Latin)

**Locked English:** "he who does not prevent or oppose wrong, if he can, is as guilty of wrong as if he deserted his parents or his friends or his country."

**Original (Latin), copied from The Latin Library, *De Officiis* Liber I, §23:**
> "Qui autem non defendit nec obsistit, si potest, iniuriae, tam est in vitio, quam si parentes aut amicos aut patriam deserat."

- **Repository / locus:** The Latin Library — `https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/cicero/off1.shtml`, *De Officiis* Book I, §23. Cross-checkable on Perseus (Cic. Off. 1.23, Latin).
- **Confidence:** HIGH — the Latin clause maps one-to-one onto the locked Miller English ("does not defend nor oppose injustice, if he can, is as much at fault as if he deserted parents or friends or country"). The locked English uses "prevent or oppose wrong"; the Latin is "non defendit nec obsistit … iniuriae" (does not defend nor oppose injustice). Same proposition; the Miller translation phrases it as the English shows.
- **Note for Stephen:** confirm the §23 verb is "deserat" (Latin Library reads "deserat"); Miller's English renders it "deserted." Pin whether the locked English derives from Miller (Loeb 1913, PD) or another PD rendering, and confirm accent/punctuation against Perseus.

## ch-4 — Machiavelli, *Il Principe*, Ch. VII (Italian)

**Locked English:** "he desired to show that, if any cruelty had been practised, it had not originated with him, but in the natural sternness of the minister." (Marriott)

**Original (Italian), copied from Wikisource *Il Principe/Capitolo VII*:**
> "E perché cognosceva le rigorosità passate avergli generato qualche odio, per purgare gli animi di quelli popoli e guadagnarseli in tutto, volse mostrare che, se crudeltà alcuna era seguìta, non era nata da lui, ma dalla acerba natura del ministro."

- **Repository / locus:** Italian Wikisource — `https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Il_Principe/Capitolo_VII`, Chapter VII, the Cesare Borgia / Remirro (Ramiro) de Orco passage at Cesena.
- **Confidence:** HIGH on the passage and its match to the locked English. The clause "se crudeltà alcuna era seguìta, non era nata da lui, ma dalla acerba natura del ministro" = "if any cruelty had occurred, it had not originated with him, but from the harsh nature of the minister." Exact diagnostic match.
- **Note for Stephen:** spelling variants exist across Italian editions ("volse mostrare" vs "volle mostrare"; "dalla acerba" vs "dall'acerba"; "popoli" vs "populi"). The Wikisource reading above is one PD edition. Pin one canonical Italian edition (Wikisource vs a critical edition) and lock its exact orthography; the locked English (Marriott 1908, PD) is unaffected by the Italian orthographic choice.

## ch-6 — Aesop, "The Wolf and the Lamb" (Greek) — **FLAG: moral-line mismatch**

**Locked English (moral as the chapter uses it):** the "a tyrant will always find a pretext" sense.

**What the Greek original actually carries.** The Greek prose Aesop (Chambry tradition) DOES have a closing moral (epimythium), copied from Greek Wikisource (Βικιθήκη), *Αἰσώπου Μῦθοι: Λύκος καὶ ἀρήν*:
> "Ὁ λόγος δηλοῖ ὅτι οἷα ἡ πρόθεσίς ἐστιν ἀδικεῖν, παρ' αὐτοῖς οὐδὲ δικαία ἀπολογία ἰσχύει."

That reads: *"The fable shows that against those whose disposition is to do wrong, not even a just defense prevails."*

- **Repository / locus:** Greek Wikisource — `https://el.wikisource.org/wiki/Αισώπου_Μύθοι/Λύκος_και_αρήν` (Chambry tradition; narrative opens "Λύκος θεασάμενος ἄρνα ἀπό τινος ποταμοῦ πίνοντα…").
- **FLAG — do not pair as a match:** The Greek moral that DOES exist says *the unjust-minded will not be stopped by a just defense* — close in spirit but **NOT** the "the tyrant will always find a pretext" line. The English "tyrant…pretext" phrasing is an editorial/translator moral (common in English collections, e.g. Townsend), not a byte-equivalent of the Chambry Greek epimythium. **There is no sourceable Greek original that carries the exact "tyrant will always find a pretext" moral.**
- **Recommendation:** ch-6 keeps English-only, per the directive's own instruction. Do NOT substitute the fable's Greek BODY for the Greek MORAL to manufacture a pairing — the locked English is a *moral* line, and pairing it with the narrative body would misrepresent what the Greek says. If the crew wants a Greek original at all, the only honest option is to (a) re-locate the locked English to the *actual* Chambry epimythium above ("not even a just defense prevails") — a chapter-content decision, not mine — or (b) keep English-only. Confidence on the Greek epimythium itself: HIGH; confidence that it matches the locked "pretext" moral: it does NOT.

## ch-7 — the *isnad* maxim (Arabic)

**Locked English (in-book attribution stays to Guillaume's translation):** "The isnad is a matter of religion; and were it not for the isnad any one could say what he pleased."

**Original (Arabic), copied from Sunnah.com, Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, Introduction (Muqaddimah):**
> "الإِسْنَادُ مِنَ الدِّينِ وَلَوْلاَ الإِسْنَادُ لَقَالَ مَنْ شَاءَ مَا شَاءَ"

- **Repository / locus:** Sunnah.com — `https://sunnah.com/muslim/introduction/31`. Sahih Muslim, Introduction; Sunnah.com reference: *Introduction 32* (in-book reference: Introduction, Narration 31). Attributed there to ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Mubārak, as the directive states.
- **Confidence:** HIGH — the Arabic maps cleanly onto Guillaume's English. "الإسناد من الدين" = "the isnad is [a matter] of religion"; "ولولا الإسناد لقال من شاء ما شاء" = "and were it not for the isnad, whoever wished would say what he wished." Strong match.
- **Note for Stephen:** the in-book ATTRIBUTION remains to Guillaume's translation per the directive; the Arabic is shown as the underlying maxim. Confirm the exact vocalization/diacritics on Sunnah.com at lock time (the displayed harakat are editorial; the consonantal text "الإسناد من الدين ولولا الإسناد لقال من شاء ما شاء" is the stable core). Right-to-left rendering must be preserved in typesetting.

## ch-8 — Thucydides, *History* 5.89 (Greek)

**Locked English:** the Melian "the strong do what they can and the weak suffer what they must."

**Original (Greek), copied from Perseus, Thuc. 5.89:**
> "δυνατὰ δὲ οἱ προύχοντες πράσσουσι καὶ οἱ ἀσθενεῖς ξυγχωροῦσιν."

- **Repository / locus:** Perseus Digital Library — Thucydides, *History of the Peloponnesian War* 5.89 (`scaife.perseus.org` / `perseus.tufts.edu`, urn:cts:greekLit:tlg0003.tlg001, 5.89). Within the Melian Dialogue.
- **Confidence:** HIGH — this is the canonical Greek of the famous line. Literally "the powerful do what they can [δυνατὰ … πράσσουσι] and the weak yield [ξυγχωροῦσιν]." The locked English "suffer what they must" is the standard (Crawley/Hobbes-lineage) rendering of "ξυγχωροῦσιν."
- **Note for Stephen:** the quoted clause is the back half of the sentence beginning "…δίκαια μὲν ἐν τῷ ἀνθρωπείῳ λόγῳ ἀπὸ τῆς ἴσης ἀνάγκης κρίνεται, δυνατὰ δὲ…". If the epigraph wants the full sentence rather than the clause, lift the whole sentence from Perseus 5.89.1. Confirm "ξυγχωροῦσιν" (Attic ξυν-) vs "συγχωροῦσιν" (some editions normalize to συν-) — Perseus reads the Attic ξ form.

## ch-9 — Aristotle, *Politics* I.4 / Bekker 1253b (Greek)

**Locked English:** "the servant is himself an instrument which takes precedence of all other instruments."

**Original (Greek), copied from Perseus, Aristotle *Politics* 1253b:**
> "ὥσπερ ὄργανον πρὸ ὀργάνων πᾶς ὑπηρέτης."

- **Repository / locus:** Perseus Digital Library — Aristotle, *Politics* Book 1, Bekker 1253b (`perseus.tufts.edu`, urn:cts:greekLit:tlg0086.tlg035, 1.1253b). In the discussion of the slave/servant as an animate instrument.
- **Confidence:** HIGH — "ὥσπερ ὄργανον πρὸ ὀργάνων πᾶς ὑπηρέτης" = "as [every] servant is an instrument prior to [other] instruments," i.e., the locked English "an instrument which takes precedence of all other instruments." The phrase "πρὸ ὀργάνων" ("prior to / ahead of instruments") is exactly "takes precedence of all other instruments." Strong match.
- **Note for Stephen:** confirm the surrounding clause for context (the passage runs "…ὥστε καὶ τῶν κτημάτων… ὥσπερ ὄργανον πρὸ ὀργάνων πᾶς ὑπηρέτης"). The locked English (Jowett-lineage) corresponds to this clause. Pin the exact Greek extent the epigraph will show and confirm accents against Perseus.
