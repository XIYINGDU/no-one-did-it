## The through-line, in my words

Civilizations did not stop sacrificing substitutes; they only stopped naming the substitution. Power keeps control and benefit while routing blame onto a weaker bearer — a junior worker, a contractor, an institution, a machine, a procedure, a record, or a euphemism. Four recognizable shapes recur: the pure scapegoat (innocent substitute), the partial scapegoat (real but low-rank actor blamed so the chain above is spared), the system/object alibi (a thing or process named as the cause), and the cost-bearing goat (a victim absorbing the harm under language that describes the harm as care). Eight questions — blame, control, benefit, knowledge, preventability, record-control, cost, and what responsibility would look like if it followed control — pull the visible name apart from the held responsibility; the gap between them is the laundering. The same diagnostic survives transposition into proxy warfare, administrative pretext, record-controlling institutions, the AI stack (input, deployment, evaluation), and the inversion case where power frames the accountability mechanism itself as the prior abuse. Two anti-laundering rules: design systems so a named person bears the consequence of the control question failing (Park, SOX, SMCR, Inquiries Act §21); keep the record so destruction and classification are themselves visible. A field guide consolidates six artefacts a reader can carry: the eight questions, the four-category taxonomy, the AI three-layer diagnostic, the war five-layer stack, a role-by-domain crosswalk, and a by-chapter index.

That paragraph wrote cleanly. The arc is legible.
