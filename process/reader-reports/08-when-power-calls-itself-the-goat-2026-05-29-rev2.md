- [comprehension] §"The chain still climbs" → §"Calling the mechanism the laundering". The chapter names its instrument "the eight-question diagnostic" and "the eight-question walk," and "The chain still climbs" walks me through exactly eight bolded questions — I counted them, they're clean, eight. Then at "Calling the mechanism the laundering," the text says: "Run the same eight-question walk on the act of reframing. Five of the eight shift their target onto the reframing itself; ask those:" — and then lists five bullets. As a reader I stopped. I was told eight, then told "five of the eight shift," then shown five bullets. I went back and tried to reconcile: are the other three not shifting? Which three? The fifth bullet ("Who bears the cost of the mechanism's removal?") and "Who could have prevented the harm the mechanism was designed to prevent?" don't obviously map one-to-one onto the original eight (the original eight include "Who was publicly blamed?" and "What would responsibility look like if it followed control?" — neither appears here). I could not tell whether the diagnostic has eight questions, five questions, or eight-of-which-five-here. The very next paragraph then says "The questions about who controls the proclamation, who benefits..., who had documented knowledge..., and who bears the cost... are the same questions that climbed before" — that's four questions named, not five, not eight. Three different counts (eight / five / four) across three consecutive paragraphs, all describing the same instrument. The instrument is the chapter's spine, and I lost confidence that I was holding the same tool the chapter kept telling me to "run." This is the exact trip you asked me to watch for, and yes, I tripped hard.

To be precise about my experience: I believe the cross-party claim is probably true, but the chapter argued itself out of its own clean demonstration of it and I could not reassemble the demonstration on my own.
