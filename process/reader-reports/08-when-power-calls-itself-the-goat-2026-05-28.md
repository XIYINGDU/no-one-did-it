## Targeted checks the dispatch flagged

**(a) The "three acts" setup.** The opening promises "three acts" only implicitly at first — the phrase that lands the promise is line 23, "Each of these three acts was publicly defended as the correction of a prior wrong," after all three have been laid out. Walking back: act one (Jan 6 pardons) is established at lines 13–15; act three by ordering (DOGE/records) at line 17; act two-by-date (Alien Enemies Act + the March 15 flights + Abrego Garcia) at lines 19–21. All THREE are established as concrete events with dates and citations BEFORE line 23 relies on them, and before the "Three frames, one move" section reuses them. The DOGE act — which the dispatch worried might arrive unestablished — is in fact set up at line 17 with the case name, the docket number, and the administration's position, then re-explained more fully at line 31. None of the three arrives unestablished. This check passes. (One ordering note logged as SOFT below — the acts are introduced in the order 1, 3, 2 by calendar date, which is mildly disorienting, but each is self-contained so I never lost the thread.)

**(b) Production markers / "pending Stephen lock."** None found. I scanned the prose and all 53 footnotes (^319–^371). No "[CITE:]" brackets, no "pending Stephen lock," no "[EVIDENCE NEEDED]," no slug fragments, no TODO/FIXME, no draft-state language. Footnote ^342 (Tower Commission) reads "access: sealed" — this is a substantive access note, not a production marker, and reads fine. Every footnote referenced in the prose has a matching definition; every definition has an in-text caller. The substitution note in ^320 (NPR substituted for paywalled Reuters) is visible but reads as an honest editorial sourcing note, not a production artifact — see SOFT-3.

**(c) Fairness on charged material.** I read this as a reader, not a partisan, and the chapter earns its fairness. Three structural moves make it land as analysis rather than attack: (1) the steelman section takes the strongest pro-administration legal arguments at full force and explicitly concedes them ("The three steelmen survive. We do not contest them"); (2) the Watergate/Iran-Contra section pre-empts the partisanship charge by showing a Republican (Nixon/Saturday Night Massacre) AND the inverse-direction case where the SAME structural move benefited the other side's framing (the 1992 Bush Iran-Contra pardons), with the explicit statement "The shape does not belong to one party"; (3) the closing self-binds the reader and author to apply the test symmetrically ("If we apply the test in good faith to one administration but refuse to apply it to another, the test has been put down"). The repeated refusal to infer mental state (line 49: "We do not infer mental states here; we cite the documented record") reinforces it. I did not finish feeling the author had loaded the dice.

The single-sentence rule landing ("The accountability mechanism is not the laundering. Calling the mechanism the laundering IS the laundering") hit as a click, not a lecture.

### 4. Orientation — CLEAN (one SOFT)

### 5. Trust — CLEAN

### 6. Takeaway — CLEAN

The three explicit tests at the close (prosecution-as-abuse, statute-as-obstruction, order-as-overreach) give me portable diagnostic equipment. The capacity survives the affect.

### 7. Mechanics — CLEAN

## SOFT findings (managed; do not block)

- **SOFT-1 — Third restatement of the diagnostic feels slightly redundant (lines 179–191).** By the closing section the eight-question walk has been: applied in full (lines 43–57), re-run on the act of reframing as five questions (lines 155–163), and then re-expressed as three domain-specific tests (lines 181–187). The third pass is rhetorically motivated (it makes the symmetry-across-parties commitment concrete) and I did not lose anything — but as a reader I felt the diagnostic had been established two passes earlier and the final enumeration was reinforcing rather than advancing. Managed, not lost.

- **SOFT-2 — The three acts are introduced out of calendar order (1, 3, 2).** The opening presents Jan 6 pardons (Jan 20), then DOGE/records (also Jan 20, but the litigation runs later), then the Alien Enemies Act (March 14). Within "Three frames, one move" the order is repeated. It is logically grouped (each act is self-contained and dated), so I never got lost, but a first-time reader briefly has to hold "wait, which one came when" — mild friction, not disorientation.

- **SOFT-3 — The NPR-for-Reuters substitution note in footnote ^320 is reader-visible and slightly distracting.** The note ("Substituted for Reuters wire copy (reuters.com 401-paywalled) per 2026-05-28 URL-verification pass") reads as honest sourcing transparency, which I respect — but it surfaces internal production process to the reader more than a typical endnote would. It does not undermine trust; it mildly breaks the fourth wall in the apparatus. Wanted it either cleaner or invisible.

- **SOFT-4 — "approximately 137" repeated four times without the count ever firming up.** The figure (lines 21, 43, 55, 173) is consistently hedged as "approximately," which is the honest move for a contested count — but by the fourth repetition I wanted one sentence on why the number is approximate (manifests disputed? sealed? individuation pending?). The chapter implies it (allegations "not individuated to adjudicated finding"), so this is a want-more, not a gap. Managed.
