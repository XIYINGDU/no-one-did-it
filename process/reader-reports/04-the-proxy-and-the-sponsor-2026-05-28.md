## What I experienced, axis by axis

### Comprehension — CLEAN

I followed the chapter without getting lost. The opening (unmarked green uniforms in Simferopol) gave me a concrete image; the chapter then told me, plainly, that this is one of three procedural shells covering the same mechanism, and named the other two (the Buk-TELAR, the Blackwater convoy). When a technical term appeared, it was unpacked at the point of use: "Buk-TELAR — the Transporter Erector Launcher And Radar vehicle, which combines launcher, radar, and command functions in a single tracked vehicle"; "serial-equivalent markings — wear patterns, modifications, and other physical features unique to that vehicle." MEJA, CPA Order 17, *Kastigar*, Article 3 bis — each was given enough gloss for me to keep moving.

The MH17 section is dense with dates and forums (Dutch Safety Board, JIT, Hague District Court, ECtHR, ICAO), but the chapter front-loaded a roadmap ("The chain runs from a serial-equivalent identification... to a court verdict, to a regional human-rights tribunal, to an aviation regulator. MH17 carries the spine") so I knew what the layers were before they arrived. I tracked the distinction between supply and ordering when the chapter insisted on it ("Hold that distinction. We will need it") — and it did come back.

### Engagement — CLEAN

The "Three denials, three vocabularies" framing pulled me in: I was invited to "listen for shape, not vocabulary," and the payoff — "Three vocabularies. Three time zones. One shape" — landed as a small click I made myself. The acoustic reversal ("The first reversal here is acoustic, not analytical. Three governments in three years, each saying not us") is a strong, earned beat.

Engagement dips slightly through the "A resolution, a verdict, an appeal" section, which is the most catalog-like stretch (resolution numbers, application numbers, dates stacked). But the chapter knew it and rescued the stretch with the "what each instrument did" passage ("UN GA Resolution 68/262 did not restore Crimea. It made the invalidity of the seizure a part of international law's documented record...") — a rhythmic, accumulating sequence that re-engaged me. Not a friction worth flagging.

### Recognition — CLEAN (the load-bearing check)

**The seven-channel-interception recognition lands as my own discovery.** This is the axis I was told to watch most closely, and the chapter earns it.

By the time I reached "Seven channels of interception," I had already been shown — case by case — that interception ran through different routes and was undone in different ways: Crimea's collapse was *performed* (Putin narrated the order on TV); MH17's was *extracted* (Dutch Safety Board → JIT → serial match); Blackwater's was extracted over years and then *un-done* by a pardon. So when the chapter said "Our load-bearing observation is that the second architecture is as multi-channel as the first," I had the raw material in hand and felt the matrix click into place rather than being handed a verdict. The matrix table then let me read it "one channel at a time" and *see* the pattern: record-fixing succeeds almost everywhere; the sponsor/executive-layer channel is "never attempted" or "stopped at prosecutorial threshold" in all three; the sentence-served channel collapses (Putin uncharged, defendants beyond reach, pardons). I drew the conclusion — "the interception is real, and so is the re-laundering" — and the chapter then stated it, confirming rather than pre-empting.

The "three questions for the proxy" beat also earns its recognition. The chapter ran the three questions on MH17 as a worked example, then explicitly handed me the work for the other two cases: "Every fact needed is already laid down above. The answers converge in both cases too — but the recognition only sticks if we do the work." I checked Crimea and Nisour Square in my head and the answers did converge. That is the reader doing the work, by design.

### Orientation — CLEAN

I always knew which of the three cases I was in. The chapter signposts case transitions cleanly with section headers ("A Buk from the 53rd Brigade," "Nisour Square, the contractor shell") and recurring anchor images (the unmarked uniform / unmarked Buk / contractor kit triad is restated at the open, mid, and close). The two-track structure (laundering architecture / interception architecture) was named for me late ("We have been alternating between two registers"), which retroactively organized what I had been reading.

**On the cross-case time-jump risk specifically:** I was watching for a forward-reference to MH17 (or another case) dropped into the middle of a *different* case's account that would knock me out of the thread. I did not hit one that broke me. The chapter does interleave timelines, but it does so *deliberately and with signposting*, not as an ambush:

- In the Crimea opening, MH17 and Nisour Square are named up front (line 17, 19) as the parallel cases — so they are established as the chapter's cast before any single case is told in depth. That is framing, not a jolt.
- In the Blackwater section, there are two forward-glances to the other cases' clocks: "The Federation Council... resolution authorizing force in Ukraine on 1 March 2014 would be six years away; the Buk would fire seven years away" (line 75) and "Six years and three months before the Buk fired" (line 63, opening the section). These are *time-anchoring* devices that locate Blackwater relative to the spine case (MH17). The first time I hit "the Buk would fire seven years away" mid-Blackwater-paragraph, I paused for a half-beat to confirm the arithmetic referent — but it oriented me rather than lost me, because MH17's date (17 July 2014) was already fixed in my head from the prior section. **SOFT-adjacent, not HARD:** I managed it; it actually helped me hold the three clocks in parallel. Reported here because it was the closest thing to the flagged risk, and a less-oriented reader might feel the half-beat more sharply.

### Trust — CLEAN

The chapter earned my belief at the moment of each load-bearing claim. Where the evidence was strong (the Hague conviction, the ICAO finding, the FBI's fourteen-of-seventeen conclusion), the prose was definite. Where it was weaker or contested, the chapter hedged inside the sentence and *told me why*: the JIT's presidential-level finding is repeatedly qualified ("a prosecutorial conclusion at the close of investigation. It is not a court verdict... It does not speak to ordering the shootdown"). The "Three counterarguments at full strength" section is the strongest trust-builder: it steelmanned the Russian Crimea position, the MH17 neutrality challenge, and the Blackwater high-threat/lawful-pardon defense before answering them, and conceded what it could not adjudicate ("We do not adjudicate the constitutional question of the February 2014 government change in Kyiv"). I never felt the book was overclaiming; the residual-point admission on the "Western evidentiary ecosystem" (line 111) was disarming and increased trust.

One small note inside this axis: the taxonomy passage (line 57) tells me MH17 is "a hybrid... partial scapegoat... [and] system/object alibi." Because chapter 2 (assumed read) installed those four categories, this read as a legitimate application, not a fudge — and the chapter justified Pulatov's acquittal as the proof of adversarial proceedings, which I found persuasive. Clean.

### Takeaway — CLEAN

I can complete the takeaway test. After reading I can say: *I can now recognize a proxy-deniability arrangement — when a more powerful entity uses a less-visible or differently-categorized intermediary to do what it could not survive doing in its own name — by asking who supplied the means, who controlled the territory, and who would lose access if the proxy were prosecuted; and I can now read a "case closed" claim across multiple channels instead of trusting any single one.* That is a capacity, not just an affect.

The closing "Recognise it / Diagnose it / Act on it / Avoid becoming the proxy" structure translated the diagnostic into non-headline life with specific, non-generic examples (temp-agency labor shields, platform "independent contractor" structuring, the outside investigator whose recommendation the company "must" follow, the civic-foundation message-carrier). The "avoid becoming the proxy" turn — "The first person to put their name on the unmarked vehicle carries the prosecution" — gave the chapter a personal stake that stuck. Beat 10 is specific, not "stay informed."
