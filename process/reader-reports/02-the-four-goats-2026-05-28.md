## Findings (SOFT)

### S1 — Engagement / mechanics: Abu Ghraib command-chain paragraph is a names-and-dates wall (lines 73–75)
The paragraph beginning "Above them, the chain of approvals..." (line 73) is one long block stacking Bybee, Yoo, Rumsfeld, Miller, Sanchez, Cambone, Pappas, Jordan, plus the Taguba / Schlesinger / Fay-Jones / SASC / ICRC / OPR / Margolis reports in line 75. I tracked the *point* — the chain was visible and documented — but I stopped tracking the *individuals*. By the end I retained "lots of senior people, none prosecuted" rather than any specific name above Karpinski. The diagnostic survives (which is why this is SOFT, not HARD), but the density here is noticeably heavier than the equivalent Boeing or Therac sections, and read aloud it would be a slog. I wanted the names thinned to the two or three that actually do the work.

### S2 — Engagement: the Russell counter-case section briefly slowed me (lines 83–93)
After four crisp category installs, the "What Francis Russell argued" section is the first place the prose turns inward and argues *about its own method* ("we are installing four categories at once; we cannot also re-prove every case within them"). I understood why it was there and I respected it — but it is the one stretch where I felt the book talking to itself rather than to me. Lines 91–93 in particular ("We weaken the unconditional reading of the category, not the case, and let the case carry the taxonomy rather than asking the taxonomy to carry the case") I had to read twice to be sure I had the distinction. It resolved, and the honesty earned trust, so SOFT — but it is a small dip in momentum right after a strong run.

### S3 — Recognition: the closing-section subheadings telegraph their own structure (lines 141–147)
"Recognize it." / "Diagnose it." / "Act on it." / "Avoid becoming it." are clean and useful as navigation, but as a sequence of imperative subheads they made the ending feel slightly like a worksheet — I could see the machinery (here comes the recognition step, here comes the action step). It did not break anything; the content under each was specific and good (especially the callback to the four anchor cases in line 147). But the labels themselves are the most "told" the chapter ever feels. SOFT.

### S4 — Orientation: "the first of nine" vs. "four cases" (line 19)
Line 19 says the Therac-25 case "appears here as the first of nine," then in the same paragraph the chapter is about "the four goats" and "the four cases that follow." On first read I had a half-second of "wait, nine or four?" before I understood: nine total cases across the book, four *anchor* cases in this chapter, each teaching one shape. The sentence sorted itself out, but the two numbers landing one beside the other gave me a momentary count-mismatch. SOFT.

### S5 — Mechanics: one date sequence felt internally crowded (line 105)
The DPA-to-NPA paragraph (line 105) is the densest single date-chain in the chapter — Jan 2021, May 2024, July 2024, Dec 2024, Jan 2025, May 23 2025, March 31 2026. I followed it because the *shape* ("agreement becomes breach becomes rejected plea becomes NPA becomes appeal") is doing the work and the dates are scenery. But it is right at the edge of where I'd have started skimming the dates. The very next paragraph (line 107) rescues it by naming the shape explicitly ("A deferred prosecution agreement becomes a breached agreement becomes..."), which is exactly what I needed. SOFT, and arguably self-correcting.

## Clean axes

**Comprehension — clean.** I understood every load-bearing claim. The three-stage migration (operator → machine → bug) in the Therac section is taught once and then I recognized it instantly when it recurred at Boeing (pilots → MCAS → certification). No concept was used before it was graspable.

**Recognition (the Boeing DPA→NPA asymmetry) — clean, and this is the strongest beat.** This is the part the task flagged, and it landed as my own recognition, not a told conclusion. The chapter set it up correctly: it first showed me the Therac alibi *collapsing* (line 99 — forensics plus a willing fact-finder, the alibi surrenders), then showed me the procedural alibi *re-forming* under new labels (line 105). By the time line 107 says "The asymmetry is the lesson," I had already felt it — machine alibis collapse to forensics, procedural alibis cycle. The word "recurse" did real work. I got the click before the chapter named it. That is the recognition beat working.

**Recognition (the four categories) — clean.** The task asked whether the four categories feel introduced in a way that orients me, or whether I was sorting cases with no frame. They oriented me. The key move is that each category is *named at the case that teaches it* and the name is bolded at the moment of install: system/object alibi (line 49), pure scapegoat (line 63), partial scapegoat (line 79), cost-bearing goat (line 121). I never felt I was holding an abstract taxonomy waiting for examples; the example came first and the name crystallized it. The line "we name each one at the case that teaches it, because a shape is easier to hold once we have watched it operate" (line 19) is a promise the chapter keeps.

**Recognition (the four-interception closing) — clean; it carries.** The task asked whether the four-interception section is a put-down point (too many dossiers stacked) or whether it carries. It carries — for one specific reason: each interception is paired *one-for-one* with an anchor case I already met (Dreyfus↔Sacco-Vanzetti, Serpico↔Abu Ghraib, Horizon↔Boeing/Therac, Stolen Generations↔Ukrainian children), and each one closes with "the limit of the lesson" naming where it does NOT transfer back. That structure kept me oriented: I was not meeting four new cases cold, I was watching four escape-hatches for patterns I already understood. The Horizon limit (lines 135 — "the drama was the amplifier, not the load-bearing interception... storytelling does not pierce system/object alibi; engineering forensics paired with a court of disclosure does") was the single sharpest moment in the whole closing run and made me trust the book more. It is dense, yes (see S1-adjacent fatigue), but it earned the density.

**Orientation — clean.** I always knew which of the four shapes I was inside and why. The "alibi climbs the stack" framing (line 49) gave me a spatial model — machine floor, procedural floor, prosecution floor — that I carried through the rest of the chapter. The closing "Recognize it" paragraph (line 141) restates all four shapes in one place, which landed as a welcome consolidation rather than redundant repetition.

**Trust — clean, and high.** The chapter repeatedly told me what it did NOT know or could NOT claim, and that built trust rather than eroding it: Verdon Kidd's cause of death "remains contested in the medical record" (line 17); the Russell counter-reading is faced head-on rather than buried (lines 83–93); "we report that Lvova-Belova said these things; we do not adopt her framing" (line 115); "The characterization is theirs" (line 119); "earned from the cases we have examined and not yet asserted as universal" (line 125); "We offer these markers as a working heuristic, not as a theorem" (line 143). At no point did the prose claim more than it had shown me. The anti-laundering rule (line 125 — "the chain stops at the procedural-stage boundary the available doctrine cannot cross") is stated as a hypothesis earned from four cases, not a law, and I believed it more for that.

**Takeaway — clean.** I can complete the takeaway test. A week from now I can say: there are four shapes of blame-misdirection (innocent blamed; guilty-but-too-low blamed; a thing/process named instead of a person; the harmed party absorbing cost under the language of care), and the diagnostic question is always "where has control gone missing in the visible account?" The three diagnostic markers (line 143) and the role-based actions (line 145 — cost-bearer asks for the name in writing; bystander asks who deployed/profited/could-have-stopped; sign-off holder refuses to sign a nameless disposition) are concrete and portable. I leave with a capacity, not just a feeling.

**Engagement — clean overall.** The opening (Cox on the couch, "between the shoulders with a baseball bat," dead by September) pulled me in immediately and the chapter sustained momentum through the Boeing and Sacco-Vanzetti sections. The two engagement dips (S1 Abu Ghraib density, S2 Russell method-talk) are local and recoverable, not chapter-level.
