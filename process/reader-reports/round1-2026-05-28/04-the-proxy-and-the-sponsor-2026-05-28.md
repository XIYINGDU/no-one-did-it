## Findings (HARD)

### H1 — Comprehension: the timeline tangent at the end of the Blackwater section knocked me out of the chapter (lines 75, 81)

I was following the Blackwater indictment story closely. The convoy fired, the FBI found it unjustified, five men were indicted. Then, mid-paragraph, this:

> "The Federation Council of the Russian Federal Assembly's resolution authorizing force in Ukraine on 1 March 2014 would be six years away; the Buk would fire seven years away." (line 75)

I stopped. I had to reread it twice. I'm in 2008 Baghdad, learning who got indicted, and the prose suddenly jumps me forward to count down the years to two *other* cases I just read about in a *different* section. It felt like the author leaning over my shoulder to remind me the cases are connected — but I already knew that; the chapter told me three times in the opening. The interruption broke my grip on the Blackwater chain right at the moment (the indictment) where I most needed to hold the thread of who was charged. By the time I got to the actual list of five names (Slatten, Slough, Liberty, Heard, Ridgeway), I'd lost the rhythm and had to go back up to line 75 to re-anchor.

Severity is HARD because I genuinely got lost — not "wanted more," but "stopped reading and reread to recover." The same move recurs faintly at line 35 ("MH17 carries the spine") which works fine, so the device isn't the problem; this one specific cross-case time-jump, dropped inside an unrelated procedural list, is where I fell out.

### H2 — Recognition: the chapter told me its recognition twice before I could discover it, and named the move I was supposed to make (lines 131, 175, 177, 179)

The chapter has two recognitions: (1) the proxy/sponsor architecture is one shape across three cases, and (2) interception is itself multi-channel. The first I discovered honestly — by the time I reached the three-question rule (line 139) I felt I'd assembled the shape myself from Simferopol + Buk + Baghdad. That landed.

The second recognition did not land as mine. It was *delivered*. At line 131:

> "The proxy-deniability arrangement is a single method. The institutions facing it are also a method..."

Then again at line 175:

> "This is the recognition. The proxy-deniability arrangement is one method, and the institutions that can intercept it are several methods."

Then at line 177 the chapter steps fully outside itself and *grades my position on a map I haven't been shown*:

> "That is the second move from S1.5 to S2. The first move, in chapter 3, was to install the diagnostic. The second move, here, is to see the diagnostic running across a multi-channel architecture..."

"The second move from S1.5 to S2" stopped me cold — I have no idea what S1.5 or S2 are. These look like internal build-stage labels (a map of where I am in the book) that leaked into the prose. As a reader I wasn't shown that map, so the sentence reads as a coordinate from a system I'm locked out of. And the surrounding sentences ("This is the recognition") do the recognizing *for* me — three times across lines 131, 175, 179 the chapter announces what I should now see, rather than letting me see it.

The seven-channel matrix (lines 161–169) is genuinely good and I worked through it channel by channel — I *could* have arrived at "interception is multi-channel and reversible" on my own from reading the matrix. But the chapter pre-told me the conclusion at line 131, before the matrix, so by the time I reached the matrix I was confirming a thesis rather than discovering one. The recognition was asserted, then illustrated — the reverse of the order that would have let me earn it.

Severity is HARD because the chapter's second (load-bearing) recognition was delivered, not earned — and the "S1.5 to S2" label used a concept (the build-stage map) before I could grasp it.
