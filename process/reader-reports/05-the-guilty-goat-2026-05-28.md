## What I experienced, axis by axis

### 1. Comprehension — CLEAN
I followed the whole chapter without rereading a sentence to recover the thread. The Bhopal scene is concrete and dated; the Boeing scene is concrete and dated; the parallel between them is stated plainly enough that I never had to guess what the chapter wanted me to hold. The "eight questions" framework arrives labeled as "the diagnostic of chapter 3," and even without that chapter open in front of me, the chapter re-states the six/eight dimensions (control, benefit, knowledge, preventability, record, cost) inside the Bhopal walkthrough, so I never needed to flip back. The aircraft/aviation vocabulary (MCAS, AOA sensor, runaway-stabilizer cut-out, trim-wheel forces) is technical, but each term is given a function in-line — I understood that MCAS was pushing the nose down repeatedly on one bad sensor, and that the cut-out fix didn't work because the wheel was too hard to turn at speed. That was enough to track the argument.

### 2. Engagement — CLEAN (one SOFT note)
The opening — Anderson stepping off the plane into custody, photographed, released on 25,000 rupees of bail, gone by nightfall, dead in Florida at 92 — is a strong hook and pulled me in immediately. The "There is another document" beat (the 1982 safety survey, "The arrest came first. The audit came later. The order matters.") is a genuinely good engine; it made me want to know what was in the audit. Engagement held through the Boeing section. The one place I felt the pace dip is flagged below as SOFT.

### 3. Recognition — CLEAN
This is the axis I was told to watch, and it landed. The "two layers, two categories" turn was **earned, not told**, for me. Here is why, in my own experience of reading it: by the time the chapter reaches the "A note about how we use cases" paragraph (and again at the "But Anderson was charged" objection), I had already been walked, one floor at a time, up the Bhopal chain — operators, local management, Indian engineering, South Charleston engineering, Danbury corporate, Anderson at the apex. So when the chapter says "Chapter 2 read this case at the corporate-form floor; we read it here at the Anderson-personification floor," I already *felt* the building had floors, because I'd just climbed them. The metaphor of a stacked building wasn't asserted at me — I'd been made to walk the stairs first. The recognition click ("a single case can stack laundering at multiple layers") arrived as something I could have said myself a paragraph early. That is the earned version.

### 4. Orientation — CLEAN
I always knew which case I was in and which floor of which case. The section headings help ("The May 1982 safety survey," "But Anderson was charged," "Climb to the floor above"). The two-case structure (Bhopal, then Boeing, then both held side by side in a comparison table) is signposted so clearly that I never lost track of which crash was which or which crew was which. The back-reference to chapter 2 (Bhopal as system/object alibi) and the forward gesture ("the rest of this book") gave me a stable sense of where I sit in the larger argument. The comparison table near the end consolidated everything I'd been carrying loosely.

### 5. Trust — CLEAN
The chapter did the hardest trust-work in front of me rather than behind my back. The "But Anderson was charged" section is the test: a first-time reader's instinct really is "wait, he WAS charged, this isn't a scapegoat case." The chapter raises that objection at full strength, in italics, before answering it — and the answer (visible-consequence test vs. chain test; "did anyone get punished?" vs. "did responsibility follow control, benefit, knowledge, preventability?") is one I believed at the moment it was made, because it had been built brick by brick in the eight-question walkthrough. I never felt the prose reaching past its evidence. Where the record is thin, it says so out loud: "the transmittal chain inside Danbury... is the load-bearing document our evidence still partially depends on"; Keshub Mahindra's "final disposition our evidence ledger marks for verification." Those admissions *raised* my trust rather than lowered it.

### 6. Takeaway — CLEAN
I can complete the takeaway test. After reading, I can say: *Now I can recognize the partial-scapegoat move — a named actor who really IS in the chain, named with confidence, while the floor above them is never asked to sign anything — and I know to run the eight questions one floor up, and again, until the chain is exhausted or a new alibi appears.* The chapter even gave me sharper sub-tools that I retained: the four properties of a chosen partial scapegoat (foreign, junior, deceased, absent) and the "settlement-before-archive" signature. The closing "Any of us can be positioned as a partial scapegoat... When we sign alone, the chain stops at us" applied the diagnostic to the reader's own life without moralizing. The takeaway is a capacity, not just a feeling.

### 7. Mechanics — CLEAN
No production markers visible in the reader-facing prose. I checked specifically (per the brief): no `[EVIDENCE NEEDED]`, no `[Stephen.]` or other bracketed crew notes, no leftover instructions. The footnote markers ([^180] etc.) render as superscript reference calls and each resolves to a definition in the References section at the bottom — they read as ordinary endnote markers, not as broken inline targets. The one HTML comment in the References block (`<!-- These [^N]: definitions live both here... -->`) is inside the references apparatus, not in the chapter prose, and explains the build behavior; a reader who reaches it will see a production note, but it sits below the prose in the machinery section, not in the reading flow.
