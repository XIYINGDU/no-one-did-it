Chapter 8 ("When Power Calls Itself the Goat") runs on the **mechanism-as-abuse** shape: an accountability mechanism (prosecution, records statute, court) is publicly named as the prior abuse, and dismantling it is named as the correction. Watergate (Cox firing), Iran-Contra (Independent Counsel + pardons), and the second-term 2025 proclamations all fit this.

The al-Aulaqi OLC memo (Obama 2010), added 2026-05-28 as the Democratic cross-party echo, does NOT fit that shape. It is the **opinion-as-instrument** shape: an internal OLC opinion converts a contestable executive decision into a closed legal conclusion, insulated from external/adversarial review. That is the same shape as the Bybee/Yoo torture memos (ch-2 slot 5; ch-12 "OLC opinion-shop pattern").

**Why:** the chapter's synthesis paragraph (was line 76) asserted all three echoes "share one structural shape" = mechanism-as-abuse, forcing al-Aulaqi into a shape it doesn't occupy to complete a clean cross-party roster. That is internal laundering (rule-12 V8) — smoothing a structural seam to improve the story. My 2026-05-28 red-team flagged it CLEAR-WITH-FIXES (Finding 1). Note: the chapter's beat-10 sentence (was line 198) already describes al-Aulaqi correctly as opinion-as-instrument — so line 76 was internally inconsistent with line 198.

**How to apply:** in any future ch-8 audit, or any audit of a cross-administration / cross-valence claim in this book, check whether the connective "they share one shape" sentence actually fits every case, or whether one case is being force-fit. The honest framing is two related shapes co-present, both crossing party lines (Bybee/Yoo + Barron = opinion-as-instrument; the rest = mechanism-as-abuse), OR a higher genuine abstraction both share ("a contestable executive decision converted into a settled conclusion by the branch that controls and benefits, insulated from external review").
