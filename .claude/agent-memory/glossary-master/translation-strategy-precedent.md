## Established strategy types (Glossary Master rulings)

### source-metaphor-unrecoverable
When the source language metaphor is not recoverable in Chinese without excessive explanatory apparatus, use functional equivalent + retain English in brackets on first occurrence. Requires `translation_strategy: source-metaphor-unrecoverable` in glossary entry.

**Applicable when:** literal translation would be incomprehensible or misleading; the term is a known term of art in its domain (and therefore has searchable/linkable value in the original language); the metaphor cannot survive the language transfer.

**Precedent:** `meat eaters` → `「主动索贿者」(meat eaters)` / `grass eaters` → `「被动受贿者」(grass eaters)` — Knapp Commission terminology, literal `「肉食者」/「草食者」` is incomprehensible in Chinese.

### functional-equivalent
When the source term has no established Chinese equivalent and no recoverable metaphor, use a target-language term that captures the functional essence. No bracket English required if the Chinese is self-explanatory.

**Applicable when:** the term describes an institutional practice whose Chinese functional equivalent naturally exists in the target culture's semantic field even if the label is new; the resulting Chinese is self-explanatory.

**Precedent:** `pad system` → `「保护费制度」` — the "protection money" association in Chinese (black-market protection racket) happens to be precisely accurate for the NYPD pad system, not misleading.

### cs-metaphor-extended
When a computer science metaphor is used in analytical nonfiction prose, the CS term in Chinese is acceptable if the context (established via the same metaphor frame) provides sufficient comprehension scaffolding. Translator's note on first occurrence is sufficient; no bracket English needed in body text.

**Applicable when:** the same metaphor is set up earlier in the chapter (e.g., "stack" → `层级阶梯`); the CS term in Chinese has sufficient lay-person recognizability; the pattern being described maps cleanly onto the CS concept.

**Precedent:** `recursion` → `「递归」` — preceded by `"climb the institutional stack"` → `「登上制度的层级阶梯」`; the stack/climbing metaphor prepares the reader for the recursive operation.
