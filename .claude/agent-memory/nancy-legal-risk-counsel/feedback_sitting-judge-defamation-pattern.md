When a book case turns on a legal opinion/document authored by someone who is **now a sitting federal judge** (e.g., David Barron, First Circuit, authored the 2010 al-Aulaqi OLC memo), the defamation analysis has a stable shape:

**Rule:** Name the author as the author. Confine every sentence about them to authorship/conclusion verbs (authored, drafted, concluded, reasoned). Never use killing/authorizing/deciding verbs (the author wrote an instrument; someone else acted on it). Never impute mental state or integrity (knew, bad faith, rubber-stamped, to order). Never assert a legal conclusion (lawful/unlawful/murder/assassination) that no court actually reached.

**Why:**
- Current judicial office does NOT make conduct that predates it "fair game" — it raises the reputational stakes and the litigation incentive, and bad-faith imputation against a sitting judge is a per-se-adjacent professional-integrity attack. The US actual-malice shield protects accurate reporting and honest opinion, not a false factual assertion of bad faith; never treat the shield as a drafting license.
- Anonymizing the author ("the OLC," "the memo's authors") does NOT reduce risk and usually increases it: vaguer phrasing breeds rule-07 implication, and hiding the human author behind the institution makes the book commit the very system/object-alibi move it diagnoses. The safest formulation is the bare verifiable fact ("X authored the memorandum that concluded Y"), attributed to a court-released public document.
- The protective triad: fair-report privilege (covers accurate report of the document/court record, NOT editorial gloss on top), opinion/fair-comment (protected only if it discloses the facts it rests on and implies no undisclosed defamatory facts), substantial truth (available for documented facts; NOT available for a contested legal conclusion because there is no adjudicated truth to match).

**How to apply:** Build the clearance around the *verb* the author is the subject of, and around the precise dispositional grounds of every case (a Bivens special-factors dismissal is NOT a ruling that the act was lawful; a standing/political-question dismissal reaches no merits). Always check author-naming symmetry across the book — naming Republican-administration authors but anonymizing a Democratic-administration author (or vice versa) is itself a partisan-asymmetry surface. See [[al-aulaqi-cross-valence-clearance]] for the worked instance.
