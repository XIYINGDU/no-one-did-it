The eight-question diagnostic is ONE fixed instrument, installed verbatim in ch3 (`book/chapters-v5/03-who-could-have-stopped-it.md`, block-quote at lines 49-58) and consolidated in ch13's field guide. Count and identity are fixed book-wide: (1) Who/what was publicly blamed? (2) Who had control? (3) Who benefited? (4) Who knew or should have known? (5) Who could have prevented recurrence? (6) Who controlled the record? (7) Who bore the cost? (8) What would responsibility look like if it followed control instead of visibility?

**Why:** Three rounds of cold reads on ch8/9/10 (2026-05-29) each independently hit a comprehension HARD: a mid-book chapter says "the eight-question diagnostic applies as written" and runs eight questions, but a reader cannot confirm from that chapter alone that these are *the* eight — the chapter assumes the tool is held in hand. A mid-book chapter read in isolation genuinely cannot carry a tool installed 5 chapters earlier; but in-sequence and audiobook readers benefit from a light re-grip too.

**How to apply:** When a downstream chapter runs the diagnostic, design a one-clause/one-sentence **re-grip** (NOT a re-install, NOT a numbered restatement) on first invocation — a compressed naming of the spine in canonical order ("the eight questions installed in ch3 — blame, control, benefit, knowledge, preventability, record-control, cost, and what responsibility looks like if it followed control") that lets a reader confirm identity without re-teaching. Forbid drift: never let a chapter say "five of the eight shift" then list five differently-worded bullets (ch8 round-1/2 fail), and never let the canonical eight get re-paraphrased into a different count (4/5/8 wobble). The re-grip echoes ch3's wording; partial walks (deployment/evaluation in ch9) must be explicitly framed as "the same eight, abbreviated because layer X bends only questions A/B/C" so the "run it three times" promise visibly cashes out.
