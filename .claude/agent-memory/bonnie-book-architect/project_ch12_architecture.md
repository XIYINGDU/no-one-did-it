# Chapter 12 architecture — Part IV middle (2026-05-26)

**Design rationale:** ch-12 builds on ch-11. Where ch-11 binds control to named individuals, ch-12 binds named decisions to the recorded artifact. The two together close the laundering surface: ch-11 prevents the named individual from being absent; ch-12 prevents the documented decision from being absent.

## The three record-discipline rules (beat 3)

1. **Write it down.** When control is exercised, when a decision is made, when an exception is approved, the decision and its maker enter the record on the same day.
2. **Timestamp and chain-of-custody.** Authentication is the floor; FRE 901(a). Without chain-of-custody, the record degrades to assertion.
3. **Make destruction visible.** Federal Records Act (44 U.S.C. ch. 31 §§ 3101 *et seq.*) + Presidential Records Act (44 U.S.C. ch. 22 §§ 2201 *et seq.*) + FRCP 37(e) graduated-spoliation regime. Destruction without inventory is itself the record.

## Counter-positions installed at full strength

- **Vermeule / Sunstein displacement** (Laura HARD-gate). Rule 1 in its strong form does carry displacement cost — laundering moves to unrecorded channels (Signal, in-person, encrypted). Chapter took option (a): concede the cost and argue the asymmetry-table trade. Did NOT narrow rule 1 to destruction-logging only.
- **Adversary-jurisdiction scope condition** (Laura HARD-gate). The three rules apply to institutions acting within a non-adversarial legal order where counter-record-control by interceptors is feasible. In adversary-controlled jurisdictions (Russian Federation Decrees 330 + 585 on Ukrainian children), the operative mechanism is counter-record from outside (OHCHR HRMMU, JIT, ECtHR, ICC). The Russian Decree row in the asymmetry table is labeled "outside scope; counter-record is the operative mechanism" rather than rule-failure.
