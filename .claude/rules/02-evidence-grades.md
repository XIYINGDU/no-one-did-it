**Grade every important factual claim A, B, C, or D before it earns a place in the book.**

A chapter whose anchor claim lacks primary-source support misleads the reader and exposes the book to defamation challenge; grading is the gate that catches both failure modes before publication.

# Evidence Grades

## A — High-confidence public record
Court judgment, official inquiry, inspector general report, GAO report, UN/ICC finding, statute, executive order, regulator finding, primary document, transcript, audited technical report.

## B — Credible but secondary or investigative
Major investigative journalism, reputable NGO investigation, scholarly article, expert forensic analysis, documentary record with partial access.

## C — Live, contested, incomplete, or allegation-stage
Ongoing litigation, allegations, single-source reports, unadjudicated claims, active conflict-zone events, cases with conflicting forensic claims.

## D — Do not use as factual anchor
Viral claims, unsourced anecdotes, partisan summaries, rumor, emotionally satisfying but unverified stories.

Rule: a chapter-level claim cannot rest only on C-grade evidence unless the chapter is explicitly about uncertainty or contested record control.

## Why this rule exists

The book accuses other institutions of letting weak evidence harden into fact. The same charge applies to the crew. Grading every claim before it earns space in the manuscript is how the book practices what it preaches — and how Stephen, Laura, and Nancy can intercept a B-grade claim that pretends to be A.
