**Citations follow the Chicago Manual of Style, 17th edition, Notes-Bibliography system, with back-of-book endnotes grouped by chapter and a paired selected bibliography; in-prose source-naming is required for every load-bearing source; inline `[CITE:]` markers carry only the card slug.**

# Citation Form

**Scope:** binds every prose artifact in `book/chapters-v2/`, `book/evidence/case-files/`, `process/review-memos/`, and `book/proposals/`. Sibling to rule `06-quote-integrity.md` (which governs quote alteration) and rule `12-reader-experience-values.md` (which governs reader-experience via V10 audio-survivability). Constitutional anchor for `/cite-density-audit` and the `/compile-book` Chicago-formatter pass.

## The three-layer model

Citation responsibility splits across three artifacts:

| Layer | Artifact | Carries | Audience |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Prose** | The chapter sentence | Source identity named in narrative voice (speaker, venue, date, court, inquiry) | Reader and audiobook listener |
| **2. Inline marker** | `[CITE: <card-slug>]` in chapter prose | The card identifier and nothing else | Compile step, reviewers, fact-checker |
| **3. Endnote** | Back-of-book endnote, generated by `/compile-book` from card metadata | Full Chicago NB citation, archive URL, corroboration list, access notes | Reader who flips to the back; copyeditor; future scholar |

Each layer carries what its audience needs. Inline `[CITE:]` brackets DO NOT carry citation metadata; that is the endnote's job.

## Layer 1: in-prose source identity (required)

Every load-bearing source must be named in the prose voice — speaker, venue, date, court, inquiry, document name — within the paragraph that cites it. The audiobook listener and the reader who skips endnotes must both know where the claim is grounded.

| Source type | In-prose pattern |
|---|---|
| **Statement / testimony / tweet** | "On Twitter, at 2:08 p.m. on June 17, Kirstjen Nielsen wrote: ..." |
| **Inquiry / commission report** | "The Iraq Inquiry's executive summary, published 6 July 2016, found that ..." |
| **Court ruling** | "In *Hamilton & Others v Post Office Ltd*, the Court of Appeal in April 2021 quashed thirty-nine convictions ..." |
| **Statute / regulation / executive order** | "The Ethics in Government Act of 1978, Title VI, established ..." |
| **Government report** | "The Senate Armed Services Committee inquiry, declassified April 2009, concluded that ..." |
| **Academic / book** | "In Leveson and Turner's IEEE Computer investigation, the race condition was identified as ..." |
| **News investigation** | "The Pulitzer-winning *Seattle Times* series by Dominic Gates and Mike Baker, beginning March 2019, documented that ..." |
| **Primary document** | "The 1 August 2002 OLC memorandum by Jay Bybee redefined ..." |
| **ICC / international body** | "The International Criminal Court's Pre-Trial Chamber II issued arrest warrants on 17 March 2023, finding that ..." |
| **Documentary / broadcast** | "In the March 2015 Rossiya-1 documentary *Crimea: The Way Home*, ..." |

The source can be named once per anchor paragraph; subsequent claims in the same paragraph may reference the source by short form ("the inquiry also found", "the same series documented"). The first mention is the audio-test mention.

## Layer 2: inline `[CITE: <card-slug>]` marker

Every cited claim carries one inline marker pointing to the source-ledger card slug. Nothing else lives in the bracket.

```markdown
[CITE: nielsen-tweet-no-policy-2018-06-17]
[CITE: chilcot-iraq-inquiry-report-2016]
[CITE: leveson-turner-therac-25-ieee-1993]
[CITE: bybee-yoo-olc-torture-memo-2002-08-01]
```

The marker is short (typically ≤ 50 characters total including brackets). The `/cite-density-audit` skill fails any inline `[CITE:]` whose content exceeds 60 characters or contains commas, semicolons, or "see also" text.

A single claim supported by multiple cards uses semicolon-separated slugs in one bracket:

```markdown
[CITE: chilcot-iraq-inquiry-report-2016; ssci-phase-i-iraq-prewar-intelligence-2004]
```

## Layer 3: Chicago NB endnote (compile-generated)

The `/compile-book` skill generates endnotes by reading the source-ledger card for each unique slug. Endnotes follow Chicago Manual of Style, 17th edition, Notes-Bibliography system:

- **Per source type**, the formatter applies the correct Chicago NB template (`scripts/format_citations_chicago.py`).
- **First mention per chapter** uses the full Chicago citation.
- **Subsequent mentions in the same chapter** use Chicago shortened form (author + short title + page, or author + page).
- **Endnotes are grouped by chapter** in a back-of-book Endnotes section. The chapter heading appears as a sub-heading; notes are numbered continuously through the volume.
- **Selected Bibliography** appears after Endnotes. Sources are deduplicated and alphabetized by author surname (or title for unauthored documents).

### URL verification — internal QA only, never reader-facing

Link verification is a private quality gate, **not reader-facing copy**. A published endnote carries only the **live link** plus, where one exists, an **`[archived snapshot]` link** (and any access note). It must NOT carry inline verification stamps — no `(verified YYYY-MM-DD)`, no `(captured YYYY-MM-DD)`, no `[primary URL no longer resolves as of …]` prose, no `(verification checkpoint-dependent)`. To a reader those date-stamps are noise; the link and the archive link suffice.

The verification *state* is still tracked — it is just never printed. `scripts/verify_card_urls.py` records each card's `verification.url_check` block, and that metadata drives editorial choices (which link is canonical, what to re-source). It lives on the source-ledger card, never in the manuscript:

- Healthy primary (`ok`/`redirect`): the endnote shows the live link (+ the `[archived snapshot]` link if one was captured). No stamp.
- Broken primary, archive OK: the `[archived snapshot]` link becomes the canonical link; the card records the dead primary. No "no longer resolves" prose in the endnote.
- Both broken: the endnote keeps the best available link; the card flags it for re-sourcing.
- Checkpoint-dependent (GFW-suspect domain): live link + archive link; the checkpoint caveat lives on the card only.

`scripts/verify_card_urls.py` stays re-runnable (cadence: galley-lock-minus-30-days, galley-lock, and on any distribution rebuild) but updates **card metadata only** — it must not write annotations into the chapters, `book/back-matter/`, or the manuscript. The `/compile-book` formatter and any URL-annotation step must likewise emit links and archive links, never the verification dates.

## Cite-density invariant

The cite-density audit enforces three rules:

1. **Slug-only inline.** Inline `[CITE:]` brackets contain only one or more card slugs separated by `;`. No commas. No semicolons (except slug-list separator). No "see also." No "reproduced verbatim in." No archive notes. No tier-1 lists.
2. **Length cap.** Inline `[CITE:]` brackets are ≤ 60 characters per bracket (single slug typically 30-50 chars including brackets).
3. **Card existence.** Every slug in an inline marker must resolve to an existing card under `book/evidence/source-ledger/cards/<slug>.md`. Compile fails on missing slugs.

Violations fail `/cite-density-audit` and the pre-commit `scan-cite-density.py` hook. Migration of pre-existing verbose `[CITE:]` brackets to slug-form is governed by the corpus migration sweep described in `process/plans/fiction-craft-rewrite-workflow.md`.

## Edge cases the convention handles

### Multiple sources for one claim
Slug-list in one bracket:
```markdown
[CITE: taguba-report-2004; fay-jones-report-2004; sasc-detainee-inquiry-2008]
```
Endnote groups all three under one numbered note with semicolon-separated citations in Chicago NB form.

### Source not yet in the source-ledger
Use the legacy `[EVIDENCE NEEDED: <description>]` marker per rule 30 (evidence). When Stephen creates the card, the marker is replaced with the new slug.

### Pending Stephen lock
Use `[CITE: <slug> — pending-stephen-lock]` only when the card exists but a specific page/paragraph anchor still requires primary-source verification. The `pending-stephen-lock` suffix is removed when the lock lands. This is the only permitted non-slug content inside an inline `[CITE:]`.

### Foreign-language sources
Source named in prose in the original language plus translation if useful (per Chicago 14.99). Endnote carries original-language citation plus "(author's translation)" or attributed-translator note where applicable. Examples already in ch-01 Generalbundesanwalt passage.

### Tweets / social-media statements
Chicago 17th edition's social-media guidance is evolving; this project's discipline overrides where Chicago is ambiguous:
- In-prose: account handle and timestamp ("On Twitter, at 2:08 p.m. on June 17, Kirstjen Nielsen wrote...")
- Endnote: full handle + platform + date + time + original URL + archive URL + verbatim-corroboration list (since social-media posts can be deleted post-publication; the capture date stays on the source-ledger card, not in the endnote)
- Bibliography: account + platform + post-date

### Verbatim quotes per rule 06
Layer-1 in-prose source naming satisfies rule 06's attribution requirement automatically (speaker + venue + date appear in the prose).

### Court cases
Full Chicago form first mention: `*Hamilton & Others v Post Office Ltd*, [2021] EWCA Crim 577, ¶ 23.`
Shortened: `*Hamilton v Post Office*, ¶ 23.`

### Statutes
Full first: `Ethics in Government Act of 1978, Pub. L. No. 95-521, Title VI, 28 U.S.C. §§ 591–599 (as in force December 1986).`
Shortened: `EIGA, Title VI.`

## Manuscript output specification

`/compile-book` produces:

```text
manuscript/
  no-one-did-it.md
    # Part I, Part II, ... — chapter prose (Layer 1 + Layer 2 markers replaced by ⁴⁷-style superscripts)
    # Notes
    ## Chapter 1 — The Altar Moves
      1. <Chicago NB endnote 1>
      2. <Chicago NB endnote 2>
      ...
    ## Chapter 2 — The Four Goats
      ...
    # Selected Bibliography
      <alphabetized Chicago NB bibliography entries, deduplicated>
```

Audiobook export (if produced) strips the superscript markers and the back-matter Endnotes section, keeping only the prose (Layer 1's in-prose source naming carries the source identity for listeners). The Selected Bibliography is excluded from audio.

## Why this rule exists

Without a declared convention, citation form drifts. The ch-06 Nielsen catch-up landed a 60-word metadata brick inside a sentence; an analogous expansion across the 12 active chapters would render the prose un-narratable and undermine the book's V10 (audio-survivable) commitment.

Chicago Manual of Style 17th edition is the recognized standard for serious trade nonfiction in the book's comp set (Caro, Snyder, Coll, Tooze, Applebaum). Adopting it explicitly:
- gives agents, editors, and copyeditors a recognized authority,
- handles every source type the book cites (court, statute, regulation, inquiry, news, journal, book, ICC, tweet, archive),
- provides built-in first-mention/shortened-mention discipline,
- pairs naturally with a Selected Bibliography readers expect,
- compiles cleanly from the existing source-ledger card metadata.

The three-layer model (prose / cite-id / endnote) is the project's craft choice on top of Chicago. It serves the audio-first commitment (rule 12 V10), the defamation discipline (rule 05 + rule 06), and the source-ledger pipeline already in place. The cite-density audit makes the convention enforceable rather than aspirational.
