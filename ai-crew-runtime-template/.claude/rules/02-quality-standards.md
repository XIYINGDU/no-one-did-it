# Quality Standards — Framework Layer

**Grade every important claim A, B, C, or D before it earns a place in a deliverable. A deliverable whose anchor claim lacks primary-source support is not promotion-ready.**

## Evidence / Source Grades

### A — High-confidence primary source
Official record, court judgment, inquiry report, regulator finding, statute, executive order, primary document, transcript, audited technical report.

### B — Credible but secondary or investigative
Major investigative work, reputable NGO report, scholarly article, expert forensic analysis, documentary record with partial access.

### C — Contested, incomplete, or allegation-stage
Ongoing litigation, allegations, single-source reports, unadjudicated claims, active events with conflicting accounts.

### D — Do not use as factual anchor
Viral claims, unsourced anecdotes, partisan summaries, rumor, emotionally satisfying but unverified stories.

## Grade-claim matching

- A-grade claim → can carry definitive prose
- B-grade claim → prose should name the source explicitly
- C-grade claim → prose must carry hedging; the contestation must be visible
- D-grade claim → must not appear as a factual anchor

## Self-review requirement

Every Producer must self-review against the quality checklist before handing off to Reviewer. Self-review is a gate: a deliverable that hasn't been self-reviewed is not ready for peer review.

## Why this rule exists

Quality that depends on Reviewer alone is fragile — Reviewer becomes a bottleneck, Producer loses the muscle of self-checking, and the review cycle lengthens. Grading every claim before it enters review makes peer review a verification step rather than a discovery step.
