# Chapter 5 — The Guilty Goat

> He complains of the tyrant who lives in your reign;
> For each wrong he commits unto you will pertain.
>
> — Saʿdi, *Būstān*, trans. G. S. Davie (*The Garden of Fragrance*, 1882)

On December 7, 1984, Warren M. Anderson stepped off a Pan American flight at Bhopal's airport and into the custody of the Madhya Pradesh state police. He was the chairman of Union Carbide Corporation. Four days earlier, on the night of December 2–3, approximately forty tonnes of methyl isocyanate had escaped from Tank E610 at the Union Carbide India Limited pesticide plant on the city's northern edge. The cloud had drifted south over the J.P. Nagar settlement. The Government of India would later tell its own Supreme Court that more than three thousand people had died in the first days.

Anderson had flown to India to inspect, to apologize, to be seen. The Madhya Pradesh police charged him with culpable homicide. He was driven to a Union Carbide guest house, photographed under guard, and within hours released on bail of twenty-five thousand rupees. The same day he boarded a state government aircraft to Delhi, and from Delhi he flew home. He did not return to India. India's formal extradition request, filed in 2003 and pursued through 2011, was never honored by the United States. Anderson died at his home in Vero Beach, Florida, on September 29, 2014, at the age of ninety-two, the named bearer of corporate blame for the worst industrial accident in modern history.

The arrest at Bhopal airport is the single most photographed moment of corporate-executive accountability in the 1980s. It is also where we begin. A man in the chain of authority was named. A man in the chain of authority was charged. A man in the chain of authority left, and the case was, in important senses, considered closed.

There is another document. In May 1982, a Union Carbide team based in the United States completed an Operational Safety Survey of the UCIL Bhopal plant. The survey identified sixty-one hazards, of which thirty were classified major. It was transmitted to UCC management in Danbury, Connecticut.[<sup>1</sup>](#c5-1) It was not in the public domain on December 7, 1984. It would not be in the public domain for another decade and a half. The arrest came first. The audit came later. The order matters.

We are about to look at what happens when the named actor is genuinely in the chain. It is the hardest case the diagnostic of chapter 3 has to handle, because the easy answer — *he did it* — is in some narrow sense true.

## Sabotage, subsidiary, chairman

Three official stories ran from the night of the release into the second decade after it. Each is dated. Each was pierced. Each protected what the prior story had not yet been required to surrender. The sequence is our first instrument.

The first story was *sabotage by a disgruntled employee*. In March 1985, Union Carbide commissioned an internal investigation that became the Kalelkar report; in 1988 it commissioned a more detailed analysis from Arthur D. Little, Inc., titled *Report on the Bhopal Methyl Isocyanate Incident*.[<sup>2</sup>](#c5-2) Both attributed the release to deliberate sabotage — water introduced into Tank E610 by an unnamed worker. The Indian Central Bureau of Investigation did not accept the theory and did not adopt it as the basis of any charging decision. Indian courts did not adopt it. No Indian or US adjudication has ever rested on it. As an explanation it functioned not as a finding but as a hypothesis offered into the journalistic and corporate-legal documentation where the absence of any contrary finding allowed it to persist.

The second story was *the plant failed, not the corporation*. Union Carbide Corporation, the US parent, framed Union Carbide India Limited as an Indian-managed subsidiary operating under Indian regulation. UCC held a 50.9 percent equity interest. The framing acknowledged ownership and denied operational authority. In the United States it took legal form in *In re Union Carbide Corp. Gas Plant Disaster at Bhopal*, 809 F.2d 195 (2d Cir. 1987), affirming the Southern District's dismissal of consolidated US tort suits on forum non conveniens grounds.[<sup>3</sup>](#c5-3) The Bhopal disaster, the Second Circuit held, would be litigated in India against UCIL primarily. The chain that ran to UCC Danbury would stay in Danbury; the case would stay in Bhopal.

The third story was *Anderson is the responsibility-holder*. The decade-long extradition pursuit, formally filed in 2003, displaced into public attention the question of *whether Anderson would be brought to court* and away from the question of *what UCC Danbury had known and decided*. The pursuit was real. The personification was real. The two stood in for each other.

Three stories. Each ran for years. Each moved blame one layer — to a saboteur, to the subsidiary, to the chairman. Together they constructed the partial-scapegoat alibi.

## The May 1982 safety survey

The diagnostic of chapter 3 — its eight-question structure — works on cases where the named actor is innocent or peripheral. The harder case is the one in front of us. The named actor is in the chain. He really did sign things. He really was in charge. The pure-scapegoat shape that closes a case by accusing a wholly innocent substitute is the easier laundering to detect; the partial-scapegoat shape that closes a case by accusing a genuinely involved actor is the laundering we install here.

A definition, plainly. A **partial scapegoat** is a named actor whose culpability at the execution or local-decision layer is real, but at whom the chain of attribution is halted by design so that responsibility cannot climb to the design layer, the cost-cutting layer, the regulatory layer, the documentation-curating layer above. The diagnostic question is not *did the named actor do anything?* but did the chain stop where it stopped because the named actor's culpability is commensurate with the harm, or because their culpability is the boundary the chain was designed to halt at?

The Bhopal chain, walked one layer at a time:

At the level of *control*, design authority for the methyl-isocyanate unit lived at UCC's engineering department in South Charleston, West Virginia. UCC controlled, by majority equity and by technical service agreements, the safety standards and the budget envelope inside which UCIL operated. UCIL local management exercised day-to-day operational control of the plant, including the decision to keep the Tank E610 refrigeration unit off through 1984.

At the level of *benefit*, dividend extraction flowed from UCIL to UCC. The cost-reduction program that reduced safety staffing and maintenance at Bhopal in 1983 and 1984 saved money at the subsidiary that was reported at the parent.

At the level of *knowledge*, the May 1982 UCC Operational Safety Survey identified sixty-one hazards at Bhopal, thirty of them major, and was transmitted to UCC management at Danbury. The transmittal chain inside Danbury — who at the parent received the report, what action followed — is the load-bearing document our evidence still partially depends on. *Sahu v. Union Carbide Corporation*, litigated in the Southern District of New York from 1999 through 2014, introduced internal UCC engineering documents into the US court docket. The 2014 dismissal turned on statute-of-limitations grounds rather than on the substantive parent-knowledge question.

At the level of *preventability*, four decisions stand out. The refrigeration system on Tank E610 was disabled in mid-1984. The vent gas scrubber was undersized for the actual release rate by design. The flare tower was offline for maintenance on the night of release. The storage volume in Tank E610 exceeded UCC's own operational guidance. Each is a decision; each was made; each could have been made differently.

At the level of *record*, the relevant archives sit in jurisdictions whose interests in closing the case did not align with the interests of the cost bearers. UCC archives lived at Danbury, then in Dow Chemical's custody after the 2001 acquisition. UCIL records were seized by the Government of India. The 1989 settlement record was controlled jointly by UCC and the Government of India; the survivor groups did not control it.

At the level of *cost*, the Government of India told its Supreme Court in 1991 that more than three thousand people had died in the first days. Indian Council of Medical Research longitudinal studies and state government figures place total disaster-attributable deaths across the years 1984 to 1994 in a range of fifteen thousand to twenty-five thousand. More than five hundred thousand people were exposed and entered the ICMR cohorts. The 1989 settlement was $470 million. Site contamination at the abandoned plant has been documented by Greenpeace in *The Bhopal Legacy* (1999) and by the Centre for Science and Environment in its 2009 study, Contamination of Soil and Water inside and outside the Union Carbide India Limited, Bhopal. Remediation cost has not been accepted.[<sup>4</sup>](#c5-4)

Eight questions; eight answers. The chain ran from UCIL operators to UCIL local management to UCIL Indian engineering managers to UCC engineering at South Charleston to UCC corporate management at Danbury and to Warren Anderson at its apex. The single human personification of blame in 1984 sat at the apex of the corporate chain and at the bottom of the visibility chain — the most photographed actor, named in headlines for a decade, the failed extradition target through 2011, the deceased subject by 2014. The seven Indian UCIL officials who would be convicted at the Bhopal Chief Judicial Magistrate's Court in *State of Madhya Pradesh v. Keshub Mahindra et al.* on June 7, 2010, of culpable homicide not amounting to murder, with sentences of two years each, sat at the operational layer beneath him.[<sup>5</sup>](#c5-5) UCC engineering at South Charleston, where the MIC unit had been designed and the May 1982 hazards documented, was not adjudicated. UCC corporate management at Danbury, where the cost-reduction directives originated, was not adjudicated. The chain stopped.

A note about how we use cases. Chapter 2 named the Bhopal case in its first installation. There it stood for a different mechanism — the parent-subsidiary legal architecture as a *system/object alibi*, the legal-entity layer that the forum non conveniens dismissal locked in. We are using the same case here one floor below: the Anderson personification, the layer at which a publicly blamed individual absorbed the moral attention that might otherwise have climbed to Danbury. The two readings are not in conflict. A laundering case can stack laundering layers. Chapter 2 reads the layer at which the corporate form is reified as cause; we read here the layer at which a publicly blamed chairman is reified as cause. Same case; two layers; two diagnostic categories. When we return later to a case already named, we are reading a different floor.

## Two crews, three hundred forty-six dead

A second case, thirty-four years later, in a different industry, with a different infrastructure of consequence.

On October 29, 2018, Lion Air Flight JT 610, a Boeing 737 MAX 8, took off from Soekarno-Hatta International Airport in Jakarta. Thirteen minutes after takeoff, it impacted the Java Sea off Karawang. All 189 people aboard were killed. The flight data recorder, retrieved by Indonesia's National Transportation Safety Committee — KNKT — would later show, in the agency's Final Report of October 25, 2019, that the aircraft's Maneuvering Characteristics Augmentation System had commanded twenty-one nose-down trim inputs over approximately ten minutes against the crew's manual corrections, on a single, miscalibrated angle-of-attack sensor input.[<sup>6</sup>](#c5-6) The captain was Bhavye Suneja. The first officer was Harvino. Both were in the chain. Both were piloting the aircraft when MCAS triggered.

Boeing's public communications, across the four and a half months that followed, emphasized two non-Boeing factors. The first was the AOA sensor — characterised, in the Operations Manual Bulletin TBC-19 of November 6, 2018, as an isolated edge-case input.[<sup>7</sup>](#c5-7) The second was crew procedure. Boeing's then-CEO Dennis Muilenburg, in a November 2018 conference call as reported by Dominic Gates and colleagues in the *Seattle Times*, told investors that the existing runaway-stabilizer memory item should have been sufficient. "We provide all the information that's needed to safely fly our aircraft."[<sup>8</sup>](#c5-8) Lion Air's prior EU-blacklist history, removed in 2016, and the carrier's maintenance record were available in the trade press for those who wanted them.

The framing was the same shape, at a different scale. The named actor was in the chain. The named actor was held responsible. The attribution stopped at the named actor. The layer above — the MCAS architecture, the 2016 expansion of MCAS authority that had not been flagged to FAA reviewers as safety-significant, the decision not to disclose MCAS in the pilot training manual to preserve the Type Rating commercial differentiator, the Organization Designation Authorization framework under which Boeing employees acted as the FAA's certifying authority over Boeing's own aircraft — was not opened.

On March 10, 2019, Ethiopian Airlines Flight ET 302, also a 737 MAX 8, took off from Addis Ababa Bole International Airport. Six minutes after takeoff, it impacted the ground near Bishoftu. All 157 people aboard were killed. The captain was Yared Getachew. The first officer was Ahmed Nur Mohammed Omer. They had received the post-Lion-Air guidance. They executed the runaway-stabilizer cut-out per the Emergency Airworthiness Directive Boeing and the FAA had issued in November 2018. The trim wheel forces at airspeed exceeded their physical capacity to recover.

The total across the two crashes was 346 dead. The first crash was the laundering attempt. The second crash was the cost-of-delay between the laundering and the interception. The structural shape — a named actor genuinely in the chain absorbing attribution from the design layer above — was the partial-scapegoat shape walked at the level of a pesticide plant in 1984 and the level of a transport-category aircraft in 2018. The same diagnostic detects both.

## What the flight data recorder said

The records that locked the Bhopal alibi were Indian and American, civil and criminal, judicial and procedural. On February 14 and 15, 1989, in proceedings before the Indian Supreme Court reported at *Union Carbide Corporation v. Union of India* (1989), 2 SCC 540, Union Carbide Corporation and the Government of India announced a settlement of $470 million covering all civil claims arising from the disaster.[<sup>9</sup>](#c5-9) The settlement was upheld, after constitutional and survivor-group challenge, in *Union Carbide Corporation v. Union of India*, AIR 1992 SC 248 (October 3, 1991). On the US side, *In re Union Carbide Corp. Gas Plant Disaster at Bhopal*, 809 F.2d 195 (2d Cir. 1987), held that India provided an adequate forum for the litigation, and the chain that ran to Danbury was administratively confined to courts in which UCC's parent role was already framed as that of a distant shareholder. The two artifacts together closed two record-control channels against the parent-corporation chain — one through settlement of the civil case in the host state, one through dismissal of the tort case in the home state.

The records that locked the Boeing pilot-blame alibi were sovereign and forensic, and they hardened against the manufacturer rather than for it. On November 28, 2018, one month after Lion Air JT 610, KNKT released its Preliminary Report under the accident-investigation number KNKT.18.10.35.04.[<sup>10</sup>](#c5-10) The flight data recorder readout was made public. The twenty-one nose-down trim commands against the crew's manual corrections were on the record. The framing shifted in print from *the crew did not follow procedure* to *the aircraft was repeatedly trimming itself nose-down on a single faulty sensor*. On April 4, 2019, twenty-five days after Ethiopian Airlines ET 302, the Ethiopian Civil Aviation Authority's Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau released its Preliminary Report on ET-AVJ at a Ministry of Transport press conference in Addis Ababa.[<sup>11</sup>](#c5-11) The flight data and cockpit voice recorder traces showed the ET 302 crew had executed the cut-out procedure as written, in the sequence the post-Lion-Air guidance had prescribed, and had still been unable to recover. Boeing's first formal acknowledgment of MCAS's role in ET 302 followed the same day.

Same record-hardening function; opposite directional effect. Bhopal's record-locking closed channels against the chain climbing. Boeing's record-locking opened a channel for the chain to climb one floor up. The diagnostic detects both, because the diagnostic is interested in the direction of the chain, not in the satisfaction of the institution that produced the record.

## But Anderson was charged

The strongest objection to what we have built is internal to our own argument.

It runs this way. Bhopal is not a partial-scapegoat case. Anderson was personally charged with culpable homicide and again with the offense in 1991. UCC's name became globally synonymous with industrial disaster. The corporation paid $470 million. Seven UCIL officials were criminally convicted in 2010. To call this 'partial scapegoat' is rhetorical over-reading; visible blame attached to a chairman, a settlement was reached, criminal proceedings were re-opened, convictions were entered. The case file itself classifies Bhopal as primary system/object alibi with secondary partial scapegoat. The argument here inverts the classification without saying so.

Treat the objection at full strength. The visible-consequence facts are real. Anderson was charged. The settlement was paid. The Indian Supreme Court did re-open criminal proceedings in 1991. The 2010 CJM judgment did establish in court that the disaster was not a one-employee sabotage event. The corporation's name did become a byword. Any account of Bhopal that treats it as a case where nothing happened is wrong on its face.

The answer is the layered architecture we have been building. The case file's primary classification — Bhopal as a system/object alibi at the parent-subsidiary legal-entity layer — is the right reading at the layer where the corporate form was reified as cause and the forum non conveniens dismissal locked the parent's procedural distance into place. That reading governs chapter 2, where the system is what gets named. We are reading here a different layer of the same case. At the Anderson-personification layer, the partial-scapegoat pattern is the dominant pattern, because a publicly blamed chairman absorbed moral attention from the design and cost-cutting decisions above and beside him. Neither classification is wrong. They apply to different layers. A case can stack laundering structures.

The test the objection runs is *did anyone get punished?* That test is real, and Bhopal would not be the most photographed industrial-disaster prosecution of the 1980s if the answer were no. But the test this book runs is different. The test this book runs is *did responsibility follow control, benefit, knowledge, and preventability?* On the visible-consequence test, Bhopal looks like a working case of corporate accountability. On the chain test, the chain ran from operations to local management to engineering at South Charleston to corporate management at Danbury, and the chain stopped at the boundary between Anderson the chairman and UCC the legal-entity-with-design-authority. UCC engineering at South Charleston, where the MIC unit was designed and the May 1982 hazards documented, was not personally adjudicated. UCC corporate management at Danbury, where the cost-reduction directives originated, was not personally adjudicated. The $470 million was a fraction of the cumulative cost the disaster imposed on the surviving population — medical, environmental, and economic — that has continued to accumulate across four decades. Seven Indian officials at two years each is not commensurate with the deaths the Indian Supreme Court itself recorded.

The Boeing version of the objection runs the same shape. Boeing was prosecuted. Boeing paid $2.5 billion under the 2021 Deferred Prosecution Agreement. The FAA's ODA framework was reformed by the Aircraft Certification, Safety, and Accountability Act of 2020. This is not a laundering case.[<sup>12</sup>](#c5-12) The answer is the same shape. The chain climbed from the crews to MCAS to the certification process, and at the certification-process layer it found a different alibi waiting. Mark Forkner, the only individual indicted under the DPA-related conduct, was acquitted at a Northern District of Texas jury trial on March 23, 2022.[<sup>13</sup>](#c5-13) Patrik Gustavsson, the other 737 Chief Technical Pilot named in the DPA Statement of Facts, was not personally charged. Dennis Muilenburg, Boeing's CEO across the certification and crash period, was not personally charged. Other Boeing executives and FAA managers named in the House Committee record were not personally charged. Visible consequence to the corporation is real; it does not answer the chain question. Both objections collapse visible consequence into chain completion. The diagnostic distinguishes them.

The Bhopal alibi weakened across twenty-five years, in three principal events. On October 3, 1991, the Indian Supreme Court delivered *Union Carbide Corporation v. Union of India*, AIR 1992 SC 248.[<sup>14</sup>](#c5-14) The judgment upheld the 1989 settlement amount and formally rejected the position that the civil settlement extinguished criminal liability, reinstating criminal proceedings against UCC and Anderson. On June 7, 2010, the Bhopal Chief Judicial Magistrate's Court convicted seven Indian UCIL officials of culpable homicide not amounting to murder, with sentences of two years each. Among them was Keshub Mahindra, former chairman of UCIL, whose subsequent appeal status is on the public record but whose final disposition our evidence ledger marks for verification. From 1999 to 2014, *Sahu v. Union Carbide Corporation* — decided at the Second Circuit at 548 F.3d 59 (2008) and dismissed at the Southern District of New York in 2014 — introduced internal UCC engineering documents into the US court record. The 2014 dismissal turned on statute-of-limitations grounds rather than on the substantive parent-liability question.[<sup>15</sup>](#c5-15)

The Boeing alibi weakened across twenty months, in three principal events. On October 25, 2019, KNKT released its Final Report, KNKT.18.10.35.04. The report identified nine contributing factors, with MCAS design and Boeing's documentation and training assumptions named explicitly.[<sup>16</sup>](#c5-16) On September 16, 2020, the US House Committee on Transportation and Infrastructure, under Chairman Peter DeFazio and Aviation Subcommittee Chair Rick Larsen, released its 245-page Majority Staff Final Committee Report, The Boeing 737 MAX Aircraft: Costs, Consequences, and Lessons from its Design, Development, and Certification. The report named five broken processes at Boeing and the FAA: production pressures, faulty design and performance assumptions, culture of concealment, conflicts of interest within the ODA process, and grossly insufficient FAA oversight.[<sup>17</sup>](#c5-17) On December 27, 2020, Congress enacted the Aircraft Certification, Safety, and Accountability Act, Pub. L. 116-260, Division V, Title I, reforming the ODA framework.[<sup>18</sup>](#c5-18)

Twenty-five years against twenty months. The same diagnostic pattern, very different time scales. The speed at which an alibi weakens is not a measure of the moral magnitude of the harm. It is a measure of the institutional infrastructure available to the people who want the alibi opened. Bhopal had Indian constitutional courts, Indian criminal procedure, and the slow accumulation of US discovery record. Boeing had two sovereign accident-investigation authorities operating under ICAO Annex 13, the US House Committee's investigative apparatus, and the Department of Justice's federal criminal jurisdiction. Where the infrastructure is thicker the alibi yields faster. Where it is thin the alibi yields late or not at all.

## Climb to the floor above

The rule is portable and it is short.

When a named actor is genuinely in the chain and is named as the cause, do not stop. Run the eight questions on the actors immediately above. The partial scapegoat is identified by two markers. The named actor's culpability is real but their share of control, knowledge, benefit, and preventability is smaller than their share of public attention. And at least one actor above them in the chain has more of one or more of those four, and faces less consequence.

The pure-scapegoat shape — Dreyfus in France, whose conviction the Cour de Cassation vacated in 1906 — stops responsibility from naming any of the chain. The partial-scapegoat shape names the lowest actor in the chain and stops there. The two shapes are different laundering patterns and the diagnostic distinguishes them. We are working on the second.

The rule does not stop at guilty. It stops at where the chain stops climbing. Most institutions apply a lower standard to themselves. And because a single case can stack laundering at multiple layers — the same Bhopal that chapter 2 read at the corporate-form floor sits here at the Anderson-personification floor — the rule has to be re-run at each floor a case occupies, not just the one most visible from outside.

## Twenty-five years against twenty months

Two interceptions. Two infrastructures of consequence. Two different terminal layers.

The Bhopal interception was Indian. *Charan Lal Sahu v. Union of India*, AIR 1990 SC 1480, sustained the constitutional architecture of the Bhopal Gas Leak Disaster (Processing of Claims) Act, 1985, which had concentrated representation of the survivors in the Government of India.[<sup>19</sup>](#c5-19) *Union Carbide Corporation v. Union of India*, AIR 1992 SC 248, reinstated criminal proceedings against the parent corporation and its chairman. The Bhopal Chief Judicial Magistrate's Court convicted seven UCIL officials on June 7, 2010, more than twenty-five years after the release. The interception climbed *within India*. It did not climb across the sovereign border. The 1987 Second Circuit forum non conveniens dismissal held. The 2003–2011 extradition request was never honored. UCC engineering at South Charleston and UCC corporate management at Danbury were never substantively adjudicated. The Bhopal interception stalled at the boundary between the host state and the home state of the parent corporation.

The Boeing interception was international and fast. KNKT's Preliminary Report of November 28, 2018, released the flight data recorder readout and the twenty-one MCAS trim commands. ECAA's Preliminary Report and the Ethiopian Ministry of Transport press conference of April 4, 2019, released the flight data and cockpit voice recorder traces showing the ET 302 crew had executed the cut-out procedure and had still been unable to recover. The two foreign accident-investigation authorities, operating under ICAO Annex 13 with the National Transportation Safety Board as accredited representative — a procedural participant, not a veto-holder — published factual findings that the manufacturer's home regulator was structurally inclined to soften. By April 4, 2019, the pilot-error framing was institutionally untenable. The KNKT Final Report on October 25, 2019, sustained it. The ECAA Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau's Final Report on ET-AVJ, released December 23, 2022, sustained it against NTSB technical comments filed under ICAO Annex 13 as state-of-design dissent.[<sup>20</sup>](#c5-20) The chain climbed one floor — from the crews to MCAS and the certification choices — in roughly five months from the first laundering attempt to the load-bearing interception.

And then it stopped. At the next floor up, the certification-process layer, a different alibi was waiting. The 2021 Deferred Prosecution Agreement absorbed the corporate admission into a procedural shell. The DOJ filed a breach determination in *United States v. The Boeing Company*, No. 4:21-cr-00005-O (N.D. Tex.), on May 14, 2024. A plea agreement filed in July 2024 was rejected by Judge Reed O'Connor in December 2024 on monitor-selection grounds. A non-prosecution agreement announced by the DOJ on May 23, 2025, and disclosed in Boeing's 8-K Exhibit 10.1 of May 29, 2025, replaced the rejected plea.[<sup>21</sup>](#c5-21) Boeing was not convicted. No Boeing executive was criminally prosecuted to verdict. Forkner was acquitted. The named FAA managers in the certification chain identified in the House Committee record were not personally charged. The corporation is a legal-form alibi for the people inside it; our job here is not to read that floor — chapter 2 has already named it — but to point out that it is there.

Two cases, separated by thirty-four years, in different industries, on different continents. The same partial-scapegoat pattern. The same diagnostic detects it in both. Different interception infrastructures: a national supreme court versus an international accident-investigation regime. Different speeds: decades versus months. Different terminal alibis: the sovereign border versus the next floor up.

Blame climbed one floor and then found a different alibi waiting. Partial-scapegoat laundering rarely lives alone. It lives in a stack, and the diagnostic climbs the stack one floor at a time. When interception succeeds, the next question is what is on the floor above.

Chapter 2 named Abu Ghraib as the partial-scapegoat install for the torture-and-detention domain: junior military police were genuinely in the chain, they were genuinely convicted at courts-martial, and the layer above them — the Office of Legal Counsel memoranda and the senior commanders — was not adjudicated through that record. Three cases now, across three domains: a pesticide plant in 1984, a military prison in 2003 and 2004, and a transport-category aircraft in 2018 and 2019. The pattern is the same shape at each scale. Naming the lowest actor genuinely in the chain and stopping the attribution there is not a 1984 mistake. It is a recurring institutional method.

There are places in our own lives where the partial-scapegoat pattern is not historical at all. They are recognizable by the same markers Bhopal and Boeing made visible: the named actor is genuinely involved, the named actor is named with confidence, and the actors above the named actor are not being asked to sign anything.

We see it in workplace investigations. An incident occurs. An analyst ran the model that produced the wrong output. A contractor installed the part that failed. A team lead signed the report that misstated the result. The investigation names the analyst, the contractor, the team lead — actors who are genuinely involved at the execution layer. The investigation does not name the manager who set the performance metric. It does not name the procurement decision that chose the supplier. It does not name the staffing decision that put a single team lead on a sign-off that required two. The investigation closes. *He really did sign it.* He really did. Run the questions of chapter 3 on the floor above him.

We see it in coverage of accidents. The named cause is the operator — driver, pilot, nurse, line worker — whose execution was the proximate trigger. The driver was distracted. The pilot did not follow procedure. The nurse miscalibrated the pump. The line worker bypassed the interlock. Each of these may be true. None of them, by itself, answers whether the training, the staffing, the equipment, the supervisor, the work schedule, or the design above the named actor had more of the chain's control and preventability share. If fewer than four of the eight questions of chapter 3 are answered in the reporting, the reporting itself is part of the laundering.

We see it in product-liability narratives. The cause was the rogue employee. The cause was an isolated maintenance failure. The cause was a single miscalibrated sensor — isolated, *unfortunate*, *anomalous*. These are the descendants of the Arthur D. Little 1988 sabotage report. The shape of the move has not changed. A named actor genuinely in the chain is offered to the public as the cause. The layer above has not been asked anything.

There is a sharper version of the diagnostic for the partial scapegoat. Partial scapegoats are typically chosen for one of four properties: they are foreign to the institution that controls the narrative, or they are junior to the deciders above them, or they are deceased, or they are *absent*. Each property limits the actor's ability to compel the chain to climb. Anderson was absent. The UCIL officials were structurally junior to UCC Danbury. The Lion Air and Ethiopian crews were dead and foreign. The Lion Air first officer's experience and the Ethiopian first officer's flight hours were quoted in trade-press coverage with a precision the Boeing engineering team's design history did not receive. When the named actor has one of these four properties and the next floor up does not, the diagnostic should pause longer at the next floor.

There is also a second sharper marker. The settlement, or the closure, is announced before the archive is open. The 1989 Bhopal settlement preceded the Sahu SDNY discovery by a decade. The early Boeing post-Lion-Air framing preceded the KNKT FDR release. Settlement-without-archive is a partial-scapegoat signature. When a closure is offered before the documents are open, ask which documents the closure is closing.

The pattern reaches our own floor at the moment a closure arrives with our name on it. We are genuinely involved in a chain that has upstream actors with more control, benefit, knowledge, and preventability; the record-controller is positioning us as the most-visible actor for an event whose causes were decided above our layer; and the people above us are not being asked to sign anything. The warning sign is exactly that asymmetry — a sole signature requested at the bottom of a chain whose top stays blank. The single sharpest move against it is the one the Lion Air families made when they pressed for MCAS to be in the training manual and the AOA Disagree alert to be standard: aim one floor up, in writing. When a workplace investigation names a person as the cause, ask, in writing, what decision was upstream and who made it. A record that names the next floor cannot easily close at yours without saying so.

The diagnostic that opens at chapter 3 still works. It just has to be asked one floor higher. And then, if that floor yields, one floor higher again — until either the chain is exhausted, which is rare, or another laundering pattern is found waiting, which is the rest of this book.
