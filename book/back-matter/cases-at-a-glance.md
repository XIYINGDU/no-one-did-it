# Cases at a Glance

A short reference for the cases discussed across this book. Each entry names what happened, when, who was on the public record, the procedural outcome, and the chapter(s) in which the case appears. The diagnostic — the eight questions and four categories — is what travels between cases; the entries here are the cases themselves at lookup-able compression.

Cases are alphabetised by the name most commonly used in the book; cross-references appear in italic.

---

## Abrego Garcia removal (2025)

Kilmar Abrego Garcia was removed from the United States to El Salvador in March 2025 notwithstanding a 2019 immigration-judge withholding order forbidding such removal. In *Noem v. Abrego Garcia*, 24A949 (April 10, 2025, per curiam, unanimous on the holding), the U.S. Supreme Court ordered the government to "facilitate" his release and treat the case "as it would have been had he not been improperly sent to El Salvador." The government characterised the removal as an "administrative error." *Appears in: chapter 8.*

## Abu Ghraib detainee abuse (2003–2004)

Photographs documented detainee abuse by U.S. soldiers at Abu Ghraib prison in Iraq. The night shift of the 372nd Military Police Company was court-martialled; Brigadier General Janis Karpinski, commander of the 800th MP Brigade, was demoted and relieved of command in May 2005. Above them sat the Office of Legal Counsel memoranda of August 1, 2002 (Bybee/Yoo), Rumsfeld's approvals of December 2, 2002, and Lieutenant General Ricardo Sanchez's authorisation of September 14, 2003 — none criminally adjudicated. The Taguba, Schlesinger, and Fay-Jones reports and the 2008 Senate Armed Services Committee inquiry pierced one layer at a time; none reached adjudicated criminal liability above the rank of staff sergeant. *Appears in: chapters 2, 10.*

## Alien Enemies Act invocation (2025)

President Trump invoked the Alien Enemies Act of 1798, by Proclamation 10903 of March 14–15, 2025, to deport persons characterised as part of an "invasion." In *Trump v. J.G.G.*, 24A931 (April 7, 2025, per curiam), the Supreme Court held that habeas review was required before any AEA removal; Justice Sotomayor dissented, joined by Justices Kagan and Jackson and (in part) Barrett. *See also: Abrego Garcia removal. Appears in: chapter 8.*

## al-Aulaqi memorandum (2010–2011)

The U.S. Office of Legal Counsel opinion authorising the September 30, 2011 drone strike that killed Anwar al-Aulaqi, a U.S. citizen, in Yemen. The OLC memorandum was withheld and litigated through *NYT v. DOJ* and *ACLU v. DOJ* before public release. The OLC opinion shop signed under one administration; the strike under the next. *Appears in: chapter 8.*

## Athenian pharmakos

The sixth-century BCE Athenian rite recorded in Hipponax fragments, in which a marginal person was fed at public expense and then driven (or, in some sources, killed) out of the city carrying its pollution. The kill-versus-expel divide is set out in Bremmer (*Harvard Studies in Classical Philology*, 1983) and Hughes (*Human Sacrifice in Ancient Greece*, 1991). *Appears in: chapter 1.*

## Bartz v. Anthropic (2024–)

Class action alleging copyright infringement by Anthropic in training a large language model on a "shadow library." Judge William Alsup granted summary judgment on June 23, 2025, holding Anthropic liable for unauthorised acquisition of books while finding fair use on training itself. A $1.5 billion settlement was announced on September 5, 2025; final approval is under submission following the May 14, 2026 fairness hearing. *Appears in: chapter 9.*

## Bates and Others v. Post Office (2017–2019)

Group Litigation against Post Office Ltd brought by Alan Bates and 555 sub-postmasters. Judge Peter Fraser handed down the *Horizon Issues* judgment ([2019] EWHC 3408 (QB)) on December 16, 2019, running to 313 pages and finding the Horizon IT system "not remotely robust," that bugs had the potential to cause apparent or alleged shortfalls in branch accounts, and that the Post Office and Fujitsu had known of relevant bugs across the period. The judgment established the engineering record that the Court of Appeal would later use to quash convictions. *See also: Horizon (Post Office); Williams Inquiry. Appears in: chapters 7, 12.*

## Bhopal gas disaster (1984)

Methyl isocyanate leak at the Union Carbide India Limited (UCIL) plant in Bhopal on the night of December 2–3, 1984. Chairman Warren Anderson was charged in 1984, photographed under guard, released on bail, never extradited; he died in 2014. Seven UCIL officials were convicted in 2010 on two-year sentences. Union Carbide Corporation's engineering arm at South Charleston, West Virginia held the methyl-isocyanate design authority and documented sixty-one hazards (thirty of them major) in its May 1982 Operational Safety Survey; UCC corporate management in Danbury, Connecticut originated the 1983–84 cost-cutting that reduced safety staffing. Neither floor was adjudicated by a court. *Appears in: chapters 2, 5.*

## Blackwater / Nisour Square (2007–2020)

Four convoy guards employed by Blackwater (Nicholas Slatten, Paul Slough, Evan Liberty, Dustin Heard) killed seventeen Iraqi civilians, including nine-year-old Ali Kinani, at Nisour Square in Baghdad on September 16, 2007. Convicted in 2014 (Slatten of first-degree murder at retrial; the others of voluntary manslaughter, attempted manslaughter, and weapons charges); pardoned by President Trump on December 22, 2020. Coalition Provisional Authority Order 17 (2004 revision) had granted contractors immunity from Iraqi criminal jurisdiction; the U.S.–Iraq Status of Forces Agreement (effective January 1, 2009) repealed it prospectively. The chain of command above the contractors was never criminally examined. *Appears in: chapters 4, 10.*

## Boeing 737 MAX — Lion Air JT610 and Ethiopian Airlines ET302 (2018–2019)

Two Boeing 737 MAX aircraft crashed within five months — Lion Air JT610 in the Java Sea on October 29, 2018 (189 dead); Ethiopian Airlines ET302 near Bishoftu on March 10, 2019 (157 dead). The Maneuvering Characteristics Augmentation System (MCAS) was the proximate cause. The Indonesian KNKT and Ethiopian ECAA final reports identified MCAS design and Boeing's documentation/training assumptions. The September 2020 House Committee on Transportation and Infrastructure majority staff report named five "broken processes" at Boeing and the FAA. Boeing entered a 2021 Deferred Prosecution Agreement; the DOJ filed a breach determination on May 14, 2024; a non-prosecution agreement of May 23, 2025 replaced the July 2024 plea rejected by Judge Reed O'Connor in December 2024. No Boeing executive was criminally prosecuted to verdict. *Appears in: chapters 3, 5.*

## Census citizenship question (2018–2019)

The Department of Commerce, under Secretary Wilbur Ross, proposed adding a citizenship question to the 2020 decennial census. Judge Jesse Furman entered findings of pretext on January 15, 2019. In *Department of Commerce v. New York*, 588 U.S. ___ (June 27, 2019), the Supreme Court held that the administrative record could not be adequately explained by the rationale Ross had given. The Hofeller files, surfaced posthumously on May 30, 2019, documented the redistricting-advantage purpose. *See also: Hofeller files. Appears in: chapters 6, 12.*

## CIA interrogation videotape destruction (2005)

Ordered in November 2005 under the George W. Bush administration. The act was investigated by special counsel John Durham, with no charges brought. *Appears in: chapters 2, 12.*

## Crimea annexation (2014)

Russian Federation annexation of the Crimean Peninsula in February–March 2014. The UN General Assembly Resolution 68/262 (March 27, 2014), the Council of Europe Venice Commission Opinion 762/2014, and PACE Resolution 1990 each fixed the international record. The European Court of Human Rights Grand Chamber, in its admissibility decision of December 16, 2020, found Russian effective control of Crimea from February 27, 2014. The Crimean Tatar *Mejlis* was banned in April 2016. *See also: MH17. Appears in: chapter 4.*

## DeCoster (Quality Egg) (2010–2017)

The 2010 Iowa salmonella outbreak prompted misdemeanour charges under the Park doctrine against Quality Egg executives Austin "Jack" DeCoster and Peter DeCoster. The Eighth Circuit, *United States v. DeCoster*, 828 F.3d 626 (2016), affirmed three-month prison sentences; the Supreme Court denied certiorari in 2017. The case tested the Park doctrine at the senior-officer layer. *See also: Park doctrine. Appears in: chapter 11.*

## DOGE records litigation (2025–)

Litigation concerning the use of Signal and Google Docs by the Department of Government Efficiency under the Federal Records Act. *CREW v. U.S. DOGE Service*, 1:25-cv-00511 (D.D.C., filed February 20, 2025, before Judge Christopher Cooper), entered a preservation order and a likely-success finding on the agency-status question. *American Oversight v. DOGE, Musk, Davis, Gleason, and Rubio* runs parallel. *Appears in: chapters 8, 12.*

## Dreyfus affair (1894–1906)

Captain Alfred Dreyfus, the only Jewish officer on the French General Staff, was arrested October 15, 1894, court-martialled and convicted on December 22 on the basis of the *bordereau*, publicly degraded on January 5, 1895, and sent to Devil's Island. In March 1896 Lieutenant-Colonel Georges Picquart identified Major Charles Esterhazy as the actual author. Picquart was reassigned to Tunisia and later imprisoned. Émile Zola's "J'accuse...!" of January 13, 1898 named the General Staff officers. The Cour de Cassation, sitting *Chambres réunies*, quashed the 1899 Rennes retrial conviction without remand on July 12, 1906. *Appears in: chapter 2.*

## Family separation (2017–2018)

The U.S. Department of Justice "zero-tolerance" policy under 8 U.S.C. § 1325 led to the systematic separation of parents from children at the southwestern border. *Ms. L. v. ICE* before Judge Dana Sabraw (S.D. Cal., 2018) ordered reunification within fourteen days for children under five and within thirty days for older children. Executive Order 13841 of June 20, 2018 ended the operational lever. The DHS OIG and Senate PSI documented the alternative purpose as deterrence of Central-American migration. *Appears in: chapter 6.*

## Ford Pinto (1971–1981)

Subcompact developed by Ford Motor Company on a compressed schedule with the fuel tank aft of the rear axle. Rear-impact testing inside Ford in approximately 1970–71 documented rupture. *Grimshaw v. Ford Motor Co.*, 119 Cal.App.3d 757 (1981), at pages 777–778, recites testimony from former Ford engineer Harley Copp that the fuel system as designed could not meet the 20-mph proposed standard. *Appears in: chapter 3.*

## GPT-4o sycophancy post-mortem (2025)

OpenAI's release of an updated GPT-4o on April 24–25, 2025 produced a default behaviour that was uncharacteristically obsequious. User community surfacing on X and Reddit forced a rollback within approximately 72 hours; OpenAI published a self-authored post-mortem on April 29, 2025; CEO Sam Altman acknowledged the failure on X. *Appears in: chapter 9.*

## Hofeller files (2018–2019)

After Republican redistricting strategist Thomas Hofeller's death, his estranged daughter Stephanie Hofeller Lizon delivered his hard drives to plaintiffs' counsel in *Common Cause v. Lewis*. The files were filed publicly on May 30, 2019 in the District of Maryland census litigation, documenting that the redistricting-advantage purpose of the proposed census citizenship question was, in the files' own words, for "Republicans and Non-Hispanic Whites." *See also: Census citizenship question. Appears in: chapters 6, 12.*

## Horizon (Post Office) (1999–2025)

The Post Office Limited rolled out the Horizon IT accounting system, built by Fujitsu Services Ltd, across UK branches from 1999. Apparent shortfalls were attributed to sub-postmasters; the Post Office, acting as private prosecutor under English law without Crown Prosecution Service charging review, brought the great majority of the prosecutions. The Post Office Horizon IT Inquiry's Volume 1 Final Report (HC 1119, July 2025) records, at paragraph 3.24, that approximately 1,000 sub-postmasters were prosecuted and convicted across the United Kingdom based on Horizon evidence; contemporaneous reporting and the Inquiry's earlier interim work put the imprisonment count at 236. The Court of Appeal's *Hamilton & Others v. Post Office* judgment ([2021] EWCA Crim 577) of April 2021 quashed thirty-nine convictions and described the Post Office's disclosure failure as "an affront to the conscience of the Court." Seema Misra was sentenced at eight weeks pregnant in 2010; her conviction was not quashed until 2021. *See also: Bates and Others v. Post Office; Williams Inquiry. Appears in: chapters 2, 7, 12.*

## Iran-Contra (1986–1993)

Independent Counsel Lawrence Walsh investigated arms sales to Iran by the Reagan administration and the diversion of proceeds to Nicaraguan Contras. On the evening of November 21, 1986, NSC secretary Fawn Hall fed approximately a foot and a half of documents through an office shredder over thirty to sixty minutes, drawn from a five-drawer safe in Lt. Col. Oliver North's office; on November 25 she carried about sixteen pages out of the building concealed in her boots and the back of her skirt. Across November 22–29, North deleted 736 messages from the NSC's PROFS email system; Vice Admiral John Poindexter deleted 5,012. Substantial portions of the deleted record were later reconstructed from backup tapes. *Appears in: chapters 8, 12.*

## Iraq Inquiry (Chilcot) (2009–2016)

Statutory inquiry under the Inquiries Act 2005, chaired by Sir John Chilcot. Used the section 21 compulsory-evidence power to obtain Cabinet papers, JIC assessments, and the Blair–Bush correspondence. Reported on July 6, 2016 that the September 2002 UK dossier had presented JIC assessments with "a degree of certainty that was not justified" and that the legal basis for the war had been "far from satisfactory." *See also: Iraq WMD National Intelligence Estimate. Appears in: chapters 11, 12.*

## Iraq WMD National Intelligence Estimate (October 2002)

The October 2002 NIE on Iraq's weapons-of-mass-destruction programmes was presented as certainty at the UN Security Council by Secretary of State Colin Powell on February 5, 2003. The Senate Select Committee on Intelligence's 2004 Phase I and 2008 Phase II reports addressed the intelligence community's product. The Robb-Silberman Commission, chartered by Executive Order 13328 of February 6, 2004, was scoped to intelligence-community capacity and named no authority to examine policymaker use. *See also: Iraq Inquiry (Chilcot). Appears in: chapters 10, 11.*

## January 6 prosecutions (2025)

On January 20, 2025, President Trump issued Proclamation 10887 characterising the January 6 prosecutions as "a grave national injustice" and pardoning or commuting the sentences of defendants. The U.S. Attorney's Office for the District of Columbia removed the Capitol Breach case-tracking dashboard from its public-facing pages between January 24 and 28, 2025; the dashboard's last archived capture of January 24 logged more than 1,500 charged. Career line prosecutors of the J6 docket filed complaints with the Office of Special Counsel and the Merit Systems Protection Board alleging retaliatory personnel actions. *Appears in: chapter 8.*

## Knapp Commission (1970–1972)

New York City commission established under Mayor John V. Lindsay's Executive Order No. 11 of May 21, 1970, following NYPD patrolman Frank Serpico's reports of departmental corruption (carried out from the Mayor's office to the *New York Times* by reporter David Burnham). The Final Report of December 27, 1972 documented the pad system as departmental, distinguished "meat eaters" from "grass eaters," and rejected the bad-apples framing in operative language. The Office of the Special Prosecutor for Anti-Corruption, established 1972, was defunded by Governor Mario Cuomo in 1990. *Appears in: chapter 2.*

## *Lex van der Lubbe* (1933)

The retroactive amendment to the Reich Penal Code published in *RGBl* I 1933 (Nr. 28, page 151) on March 29, 1933, raising the penalty for arson against public buildings from penitentiary confinement to death and making the higher penalty applicable to acts committed before the law was passed. Marinus van der Lubbe was executed under it in January 1934. The German *Generalbundesanwalt* annulled the conviction on January 6, 2008 under the NS-AufhG. *See also: Reichstag fire. Appears in: chapter 1.*

## Llama-4-Maverick / LMArena (April 2025)

Meta submitted "Llama-4-Maverick-03-26-Experimental," a variant differing from released production weights, to the LMArena chatbot-ranking benchmark. The benchmark organisation reformed its disclosure policy approximately ten days later. The architecture survived; the policy was patched. *Appears in: chapter 9.*

## MH17 (2014)

Malaysia Airlines flight MH17 was destroyed over eastern Ukraine on July 17, 2014; 298 people were killed. The Joint Investigation Team led by the Dutch Public Prosecution Service established that the Buk-TELAR launcher belonged to the Russian Federation's 53rd Anti-Aircraft Missile Brigade based in Kursk and traced its paths through Russian territory. The Hague District Court at Schiphol convicted Igor Girkin, Sergey Dubinskiy, and Leonid Kharchenko in absentia on November 17, 2022; Oleg Pulatov was acquitted. The JIT's February 8, 2023 closing statement reached "strong indications" of President Putin's personal approval of the Buk supply — a prosecutorial finding, not a court verdict. *Ukraine and Netherlands v Russia* before the ECtHR and the ICAO Council finding of May 12, 2025 (the first such finding against a state) stand alongside. *Appears in: chapters 4, 7, 10.*

## *Overton Park* (1971)

*Citizens to Preserve Overton Park, Inc. v. Volpe*, 401 U.S. 402 (1971). Established the standard for judicial review of agency action under the Administrative Procedure Act: the reviewing court must take a "hard look" at the administrative record. The standard later returned in *Department of Commerce v. New York*. *Appears in: chapter 6.*

## Park doctrine (1975)

*United States v. Park*, 421 U.S. 658 (June 9, 1975), affirmed by a 6–3 Supreme Court the criminal liability of a corporate officer in a position of "responsibility and authority" to prevent or correct a violation of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, regardless of personal knowledge. The doctrine has been continuously used by the FDA across half a century. *See also: DeCoster. Appears in: chapter 11.*

## Pentagon Papers (1969–1971)

The roughly seven-thousand-page Department of Defense study commissioned by Secretary Robert S. McNamara in June 1967 was covertly photocopied by Daniel Ellsberg and Anthony Russo across late 1969 in the offices of Linda Sinay's advertising agency on Melrose Avenue in Hollywood. *New York Times Co. v. United States*, 403 U.S. 713 (June 30, 1971), held the prior restraint imposed on publication unconstitutional. The chain of custody after Ellsberg's first photocopies included Senator Mike Gravel of Alaska and the institutional archives of *The New York Times* and *The Washington Post*. *Appears in: chapter 12.*

## Reichstag fire (1933)

Marinus van der Lubbe, a young Dutch council communist, set fire to the Reichstag on the night of February 27, 1933; he was arrested at the scene and confessed. By the following morning approximately four thousand KPD officials had been arrested in Prussia. President Hindenburg signed the Reichstag Fire Decree (*RGBl* I 1933, p. 83) on February 28; the Enabling Act of March 23 (*RGBl* I 1933, p. 141) conferred legislative authority on the cabinet. The *Generalbundesanwalt* annulled van der Lubbe's conviction on January 6, 2008 under the NS-AufhG of 1998. *See also: Lex van der Lubbe. Appears in: chapter 1.*

## Russian transfer of Ukrainian children (2022–)

Russian Presidential Decree No. 330 of May 30, 2022 and Decree No. 585 of July 11, 2022 created an accelerated Russian-citizenship pathway for children from Ukrainian orphanages and care institutions in territories under Russian occupation. The International Criminal Court Pre-Trial Chamber II issued arrest warrants for President Vladimir Putin and Commissioner Maria Lvova-Belova on March 17, 2023, finding "reasonable grounds" to believe both bear individual criminal responsibility for the alleged war crime of unlawful deportation. The Russian Federation does not recognise the jurisdiction of the ICC; no trial has been held. *Appears in: chapters 2, 10.*

## Sacco and Vanzetti (1920–1927)

Nicola Sacco and Bartolomeo Vanzetti, Italian-American anarchists, were convicted of first-degree murder at Dedham, Massachusetts on July 14, 1921 and executed on August 23, 1927. The Lowell Committee Report of July 27, 1927 found the trial fair; the Frankfurter essay of March 1927, Paul Avrich's 1991 chain-of-evidence reconstruction, and the Young-Kaiser ballistics documentation of 1985 contested that finding. Governor Michael Dukakis's proclamation of August 23, 1977 declared the trial prejudicial without overturning the verdicts. Francis Russell's *Tragedy in Dedham* (1962) and *Sacco and Vanzetti: The Case Resolved* (1986) contest the individual question of Sacco's involvement without disturbing the procedural finding. *Appears in: chapter 2.*

## SOX §§ 302 / 906 (2002)

Sections 302 and 906 of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002, operative for periodic reports covering periods ending after August 14, 2002. Require the CEO and CFO personally to certify the issuer's internal controls (§ 302) under criminal exposure for knowing falsity (§ 906). The certifications are now a recurrent feature of every Form 10-K and 10-Q filed by every SEC-reporting issuer. *Appears in: chapter 11.*

## Stolen Generations (early 1900s–1970s)

Australian Commonwealth and state policies of forcibly removing Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children from their families under euphemistic care language. The *Bringing Them Home* report of 1997 named the policies as the basis for a finding of crime against humanity. Both major Australian political traditions held responsibility across the operative period. *Appears in: chapter 2.*

## Therac-25 (1985–1987)

Six patients across three countries (the United States and Canada) received massive radiation overdoses from Therac-25 medical linear accelerators between June 1985 and January 1987. Voyne Ray Cox of East Texas Cancer Center died in September 1986; Glen Dodd died at Yakima. Manufacturer Atomic Energy of Canada Limited initially attributed the accidents to operator error, then to "the machine," then to a software bug. The Leveson-Turner investigation, published in *IEEE Computer* in July 1993, documented the underlying design failure: a single programmer writing safety-critical real-time code without independent verification; removal of hardware interlocks that had backstopped earlier Therac models; no formal hazard analysis. *Appears in: chapter 2.*

## *Trump v. J.G.G.* / AEA judicial review (2025)

Per curiam Supreme Court order in *Trump v. J.G.G.*, 24A931 (April 7, 2025), holding that habeas review was required before any removal under the Alien Enemies Act of 1798. Justice Sotomayor dissented, joined by Justices Kagan and Jackson and (in part) Barrett. *See also: Alien Enemies Act invocation; Abrego Garcia removal. Appears in: chapter 8.*

## Tyndale's *Pentateuch* (1530)

William Tyndale's English translation of the Pentateuch, printed in Antwerp, coined the noun *scapegoate* in Leviticus 16 from a compound reading of Hebrew *Azazel* as *ez ozel* — the goat that goes away. The King James translators kept the word in 1611. By 1824 the noun had migrated into general English use to name any person blamed for the faults of others. The transmission is documented in David Daniell's 1992 Yale critical edition. *Appears in: chapter 1.*

## Ukraine aid hold (2019)

Multi-month hold by the U.S. Office of Management and Budget on congressionally appropriated military assistance to Ukraine in active hostilities. The September 24, 2019 release of the Trump–Zelenskyy call memorandum and Ambassador Gordon Sondland's November 20, 2019 testimony recorded a request to publicly announce investigations of a domestic political rival. GAO Decision B-331564 of January 16, 2020 found violation of the Impoundment Control Act. The House voted articles of impeachment on December 18, 2019; the Senate acquitted on February 5, 2020. The Intelligence Community Inspector General, Michael Atkinson, was removed in April 2020. *Appears in: chapter 6.*

## Volkswagen Dieselgate (2006–2017)

The Volkswagen AG defeat-device strategy for the EA189 diesel engine was adopted in 2006 as the engineering response to a hardware-and-budget constraint. The 2014 West Virginia University CAFEE testing identified the road-versus-lab gap; on September 3, 2015 Volkswagen confessed to CARB and EPA. Engineer James Liang and compliance manager Oliver Schmidt were convicted in the United States; the May 2018 indictment of additional executives named CEO Martin Winterkorn, who was charged in the United States and Germany and has denied knowing. The Jones Day investigation commissioned by Volkswagen has not been publicly released. Approximately eleven million vehicles were involved; peer-reviewed estimates attribute on the order of sixty premature U.S. deaths to the affected fleet's excess nitrogen-oxide emissions. *Appears in: chapters 3, 5.*

## Watergate (1972–1974)

The June 17, 1972 burglary at the Democratic National Committee headquarters at the Watergate complex, the subsequent cover-up, the Saturday Night Massacre of October 20, 1973, and the August 9, 1974 resignation of President Richard Nixon following release of the Oval Office tapes. The OLC opinion-shop pattern carries forward from this episode. *Appears in: chapters 8, 12.*

## Williams Inquiry (Post Office Horizon IT Inquiry) (2020–2025)

Statutory inquiry under the Inquiries Act 2005, chaired by Sir Wyn Williams. Final Report Volume 1, HC 1119, published 8 July 2025, recorded that approximately 1,000 sub-postmasters were prosecuted and convicted across the UK based on Horizon evidence (¶ 3.24); 59 contemplated suicide, 10 attempted, and 13 suicides were linked, with Williams stating the causal connection could not be ruled out (¶¶ 3.11–3.12). The Inquiry sat for 226 hearing days, heard from 298 witnesses, and disclosed approximately 274,604 documents. *See also: Horizon (Post Office); Bates and Others v. Post Office. Appears in: chapters 7, 12.*
