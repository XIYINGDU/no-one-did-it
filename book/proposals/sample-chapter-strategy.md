# Sample Chapter Strategy — *No One Did It*

## Recommendation: send chapters 3 and 10

For the first send to any literary agent or acquiring editor, attach two chapters: **chapter 3 ("Who Could Have Stopped It?")** from Part I and **chapter 10 ("The Model Did It")** from Part III. The two chapters together demonstrate the manuscript's two load-bearing capacities — installing the diagnostic and stress-testing it against contested modern terrain — in roughly the same combined word count as a single long magazine essay.

## Why chapter 3 (Part I diagnostic)

Chapter 3 installs the eight-question diagnostic that Parts II, III, and IV run on every case. It is the manuscript's single most portable artefact and the locus where the comparative-method commitment is most legible. Chapter 3 walks Volkswagen Dieselgate (system/object alibi with deliberate-fraud overlay) and the Ford Pinto case of the 1970s (partial scapegoat with system/object alibi secondary) through the same eight questions in sequence — the asymmetry of the two interceptions (VW: civil settlement plus named-individual criminal convictions but no US prosecution of the CEO; Pinto: *Grimshaw v. Ford Motor Co.* civil verdict locked 1981, Indiana criminal acquittal 1980) is its load-bearing teaching.

An agent reading chapter 3 sees three things at once. First, the analytic apparatus — eight discrete questions, deployed as a working instrument rather than a moral frame. Second, the comparative method — two cases forty years apart, the diagnostic surviving the era gap. Third, the prose register — severe, evidence-anchored, with the recognition arriving through the asymmetry rather than through authorial pronouncement. Chapter 3 also functions as a defamation-discipline showcase: Liang and Schmidt are named per court of conviction; Winterkorn is named per documented procedural status; Iacocca's role in the Pinto chain is described per *Grimshaw* record only. An agent who reads chapter 3 grasps the manuscript's commitment to rule-05 procedural-stage discipline without consulting the rules document.

## Why chapter 10 (Part III stress test)

Chapter 10 stress-tests the diagnostic against the contested modern terrain the manuscript most needs to demonstrate it survives. Three layers of the AI stack are walked at the same diagnostic depth as the historical chapters — input layer (the training-data litigation cluster, including the $1.5 billion *Bartz v. Anthropic* settlement of June 2025); deployment layer (the GPT-4o sycophancy incident of 24-29 April 2025, with OpenAI's own post-mortem using system-agency grammar as the documented record); evaluation layer (the Llama 4 / LMArena benchmark controversy of 5-11 April 2025, with LMArena's subsequent policy revision making the procedural shell visible).

Chapter 10 carries the load that no historical chapter can carry: the demonstration that the diagnostic survives the case domain editors most expect a comparative-method work to flinch on. Chapter 10 explicitly refuses the "AI is special" frame; the algorithm is named as the newest altar, not a unique civilisational rupture. An agent reading chapter 10 sees the comp-set positioning (Snyder / Tooze / Coll register applied to the AI stack rather than O'Neil / Crawford register applied to the historical cases) and grasps why the manuscript belongs on the trade-nonfiction front-of-store shelf rather than the AI-shelf.

## Why not chapter 1, chapter 2, or chapter 13

**Chapter 1 ("The Altar Moves")** opens the manuscript on the Reichstag Fire and the ancient referent (Leviticus 16, the Athenian *pharmakos*, Tyndale's 1530 coinage). It is the metaphor-install chapter; the analytic apparatus is not yet present. As a sample, chapter 1 risks reading as literary-historical essay; an agent might miss the diagnostic that arrives in chapters 2 and 3. Chapter 1 should ride with the full manuscript, not lead the sample.

**Chapter 2 ("The Four Goats")** installs the taxonomy across four anchor cases and four counter-cases. It is structurally dense and works best once the metaphor-install of chapter 1 has landed first. As a stand-alone sample, chapter 2 carries more taxonomic apparatus than a first-touch read can absorb; the four-category installation lands better with chapter 1's setup in hand.

**Chapter 13 ("A Reader's Field Guide")** is the field-guide payoff and explicitly NOT a sample candidate. Read in isolation, chapter 13 reads as a checklist; its function is to return the instrument after twelve chapters have earned it. Sending chapter 13 as a sample would invite the wrong shelving (self-help-adjacent) and obscure what the manuscript actually does.

## Alternative selections if the agent requests different chapters

If the agent prefers a Part II pattern chapter over chapter 3, recommend **chapter 7 ("The Record Is the Battlefield")** — the Horizon walk is the manuscript's strongest case at chain-of-custody depth and demonstrates the source-ledger discipline at full scale.

If the agent prefers a non-AI Part III stress test, recommend **chapter 8 ("War Is the Perfect Laundry")** — Iraq WMD as anchor with four prior-chapter callbacks. Chapter 8 carries more comp-set resonance (Coll / Mayer) than chapter 10 and may suit an editor at a house with a strong national-security list. The trade-off: chapter 8 lacks the current-relevance signal that chapter 10's AI material supplies for the 2026 publication window.

If the agent prefers a Part IV anti-laundering chapter, recommend **chapter 11 ("Make Responsibility Follow Control")** — the four statute-and-doctrine walk (Park, SOX, SMCR, Inquiries Act §21) demonstrates the manuscript's design-rule capacity but lacks a narrative anchor scene; pair with chapter 3, not on its own.
