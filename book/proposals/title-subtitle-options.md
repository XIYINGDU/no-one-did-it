# Title and Subtitle Options

The lock — already on the cover and ISBN record:

**No One Did It: Responsibility Laundering, from the Scapegoat to the Algorithm**

Four alternatives follow as comparison only; none is recommended over the lock.

## Defence of the lock

The title states what an institution says when a chain has been laundered. It performs the move the pattern diagnoses — letting us hear it as artefact rather than statement. The subtitle leads with the coinage ("responsibility laundering"), then pairs the oldest case ("the Scapegoat") with the newest ("the Algorithm") through singular archetypes. Five reads carry it:

1. Diagnostic-from-the-inside titles are more memorable than diagnostic-from-the-outside ones; *Bullshit Jobs* and *Manufactured Consent* travel because the noun phrase *is* the diagnosis.
2. The coinage in the subtitle plants the term at first contact, where the lay-press summary will repeat it. Owning a term is how serious trade nonfiction gets quoted.
3. "From the Scapegoat to the Algorithm" carries the historical sweep (Snyder/Tooze comp shelf) and the AI signal (front-of-store relevance) in one phrase, without pinning the work to a single news cycle.
4. The phrase "no one did it" is defamation-neutral — attributing nothing to anyone — and the subtitle names a method, not an actor.
5. The trade-off (a cold browser must infer what "responsibility laundering" means) is the work the subtitle is supposed to do; that is what comp-set titles do at the same price point.

## Option A — *The Procedure Did It: How Modern Power Hides Inside Its Own Paperwork*
Foregrounds the system/object alibi category; loses the historical floor (Reichstag, Sacco, Dreyfus) and the cost-bearing-goat material (Ukrainian children). Narrows four shapes to one.

## Option B — *The Newest Altar: From Scapegoats to AI, How Power Keeps Moving the Blame*
Leans into the 2026 AI shelf. The cost is mis-signal: an AI title invites the O'Neil/Crawford shelf and dates fast against a news cycle the work outlasts by design. Front-of-store traction is real; positioning loss is larger.

## Option C — *The Substitute: A History of Responsibility-Hiding, from the Reichstag Fire to the AI Stack*
Honest about the long-horizon comparative method. "A History of" registers as academic for trade; "responsibility-hiding" is weaker than "responsibility laundering" because it loses the procedural-cleanliness implication the laundering metaphor carries.

## Option D — *Who Could Have Stopped It? A Field Guide to Responsibility Laundering*
Puts question 5 of the eight-question diagnostic in the title; signals the instrument well. "Field guide" softens the historical ambition and invites self-help-adjacent shelving. Strong with the practitioner audience; weaker with the serious-trade-nonfiction comp set the work actually sits in.

## Recommendation

Keep the lock. If a foreign-rights or paperback-partner editor reports cold browsers bouncing off "responsibility laundering," the fallback is the plain-English verb phrase ("How Power Hides from Blame"), not Option B.
