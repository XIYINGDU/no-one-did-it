# Audience — *No One Did It*

## Primary — serious general readers in the Snyder / Applebaum / Tooze register

The first segment is the same readership that bought *On Tyranny* in hardcover, kept *Twilight of Democracy* on the bedside table for a month, and finished *Crashed* with a notebook. It wants a single durable apparatus to carry across cases, not a list of scandals. It tolerates severity, prefers the long horizon to the hot take, and is sceptical of partisan symmetry and single-villain narratives. It reads serious trade nonfiction because the apparatus changes how it reads the next morning's news.

This segment will recognise the structural commitment from page one — chapter 1 opens on the Reichstag fire and the decree signed in the same week — and stay with the four-category taxonomy through chapter 2 because the taxonomy promises analytic transferability rather than another retelling. We expect front-of-store hardcover purchase plus one gifted copy in the same demographic; chapter 13's six artefacts are built for that second-hand reuse. Segment size signal: trade nonfiction in the Snyder/Tooze comp range routinely sustains five-figure first-year hardcover runs in the US/UK and substantial paperback follow-on; we do not invent a number.

## Secondary — practitioners with institutional reading habits

**Lawyers.** Civil-rights litigators, corporate-compliance attorneys, public-integrity prosecutors, and inquiry counsel recognise the case material on first sight — the Park doctrine, Sarbanes-Oxley §§ 302/906, the UK Senior Managers Regime, the Inquiries Act 2005 §21, the Horizon chain-of-custody walk, the DPA architecture. Channels: ABA Journal review path; CLE adoption; bar-association reading lists.

**Compliance officers, internal auditors, risk professionals.** Chapter 11 (Park, SOX, SMCR) and chapter 12 (record discipline) read as a working artefact alongside the laundering moves that fence those designs. Channels: SCCE conferences; compliance-week roundups.

**Investigative journalists and editors.** Chapter 7 (Horizon record control) and chapter 12 (four record survivals: Pentagon Papers, Walsh, Chilcot, the Hofeller files) function as method documentation. Channels: IRE; Nieman Lab; longform Bluesky/Substack circulation where comp-set authors have crossover.

**AI safety, AI policy, AI governance practitioners.** Chapter 10's three-layer treatment of training, deployment, and evaluation is calibrated to a working policy reader. Channels: GovAI; CSET; Stanford Cyber Policy Center; AI alignment fora. Comp shelf is Snyder/Tooze, not O'Neil/Crawford.

## Tertiary — graduate teaching adoption (year-two)

Political-science (democratic-erosion, accountability), law (admin law, corporate-criminal, national-security, AI-and-the-law clinics), and STS programs each have a documented appetite for trade nonfiction at this depth. The Chicago-NB endnote apparatus invites the adoption; the field guide rewards it. No launch-time marketing into this channel.

## Disqualifying segments

Three audiences are explicitly not served and the pitch must not court them: partisans seeking ammunition (chapter 6's 2014 Obama-era family-detention precursor + chapter 9's documented intercepts make the diagnostic non-partisan); AI-doom and AI-boosterism audiences (the algorithm is the newest altar, not a unique apocalypse); and audiences wanting a single named villain (the central commitment is that chains climb).
