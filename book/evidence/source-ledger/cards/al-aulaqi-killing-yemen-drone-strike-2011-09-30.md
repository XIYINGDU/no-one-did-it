# Killing of Anwar al-Aulaqi, U.S. drone strike in Yemen, September 30, 2011

The card anchors the central event of the cross-valence al-Aulaqi case: the deliberate, pre-authorized
killing of a U.S. citizen by a U.S. drone strike, the act the Barron OLC memorandum was written to
authorize. Together with the memo card and the two dismissal cards, this establishes the full
opinion-as-shield structure: a discretionary decision to kill a citizen, converted into a legal
conclusion by an OLC opinion, executed, and never adversarially tested.

## Why this card exists

Loki packet Candidate 1, factual summary. The killing is the act; the memo is the alibi; the
dismissals are the absence of external review. This card carries the act.

## Attribution discipline (load-bearing)

The descriptor "operational leader of al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula" is the GOVERNMENT's
characterization. It was never adjudicated (both suits were dismissed on threshold/special-factors
grounds without reaching the merits). Wayne must write it as the government's description, not as a
court finding. This is exactly the kind of un-tested factual claim the chapter's own diagnostic
("the citation is the first step, not the last") teaches the reader to interrogate.
