# Al-Aulaqi v. Panetta, 35 F. Supp. 3d 56 (D.D.C. 2014) — post-strike damages suit dismissed

The card anchors the second accountability-avoidance leg of the cross-valence al-Aulaqi case: after
the strikes, a Bivens suit seeking to hold named officials personally liable was dismissed on
special-factors grounds, so no individual was held accountable through the courts. Together with the
pre-strike dismissal (Al-Aulaqi v. Obama), this completes the "the suits were dismissed on
justiciability/special-factors grounds, never reaching the merits" finding in the Loki packet.

## Why this card exists

Loki packet Candidate 1 lists this as source #4 (A-grade). It establishes the no-individual-accountability
fact that is load-bearing for the system/object-alibi classification: the opinion authorized; the act
was carried out; and no court ever held any named human individually responsible.

## Cost-bearer note (secondary taxonomy)

This card also documents the deaths of Abdulrahman al-Aulaqi (16, U.S. citizen, killed in a separate
Oct. 14, 2011 strike, not alleged to be a combatant) and Samir Khan (killed in the same Sept. 30, 2011
strike as Anwar). Per the Collyer opinion, Abdulrahman and Khan "were not targeted and their deaths
were unanticipated." Loki's packet correctly caps the cause-of-death CHARACTERIZATION of Abdulrahman's
death at C-grade (no adversarially-tested official accounting); the chapter must hedge any account of
WHY he died beyond the court's "not targeted / unanticipated" language. The fact that he was a
16-year-old U.S. citizen killed by a U.S. strike is itself A-grade (court record). See
[[al-aulaqi-cost-bearer-abdulrahman-khan-deaths-2011]] for the cost-bearer cluster.
