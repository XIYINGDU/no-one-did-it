## Why this card exists

Chapter 3 cites the Stadler conviction at line 137 as part of the beat-6 deliberate-fraud
counterargument cluster, alongside the Liang and Schmidt US sentencings. The Stadler conviction
extends the chapter's analysis into German procedure and shows that accountability eventually
reached one C-suite executive (the former Audi AG CEO) seven years after the EPA Notice of
Violation.

## Diagnostic significance

For the chapter's Q8 (what would responsibility look like) question, the Stadler conviction is
the procedural data point that shows the chain did eventually climb above the mid-management
level — but only across a different jurisdiction, on a plea-based settlement, and with a
suspended sentence. The chapter uses this to maintain the partial-scapegoat classification: the
chain reached one C-suite executive in Germany but did not adjudicate the indicted former
Volkswagen AG CEO (Winterkorn) at any level.
