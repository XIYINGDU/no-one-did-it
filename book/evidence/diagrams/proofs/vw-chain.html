Volkswagen “Dieselgate”: the responsibility chain

What the story blamed — and where responsibility actually sat.
What the story blamed
Individual engineers — and “the software”
VW first described “a few” engineers; James Liang and Oliver Schmidt were convicted in the United States.
… but the eight questions point upward:
Where responsibility sat
Control
VW engineering and management approved the defeat-device approach to meet US emissions limits without giving up performance or cost.
Benefit
Volkswagen AG — diesel sales and the “Clean Diesel” market position; the cost of genuinely compliant engineering was avoided.
Knowledge
The device was built deliberately to detect the test cycle (EPA and DOJ findings). How far up knowledge reached is contested — CEO Martin Winterkorn was charged in the US and Germany and has denied knowing.
Preventability
A compliant — and costlier — engineering path existed and was not taken.
Record control
The deception lived inside the car’s own software: it sensed the test and changed the emissions, so the records lied.
Cost borne by
Roughly 11 million vehicle owners; the public breathing the excess NOₓ; and the individual engineers who were prosecuted.
Blame landed on the people and the machine at the bottom of the chain. Control and benefit sat at the top.
