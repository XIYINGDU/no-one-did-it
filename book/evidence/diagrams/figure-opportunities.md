# Figure-Opportunity Survey — *No One Did It*

Owner: Bonnie / Book Architect / Developmental Editor
Date: 2026-05-29
Purpose: A book-wide map of where a chart, table, or diagram would make a *buried* argument legible. This is the opportunity map only — it specifies what to visualize and how to frame it (rule 03 visual-material ownership). It does not produce the figures, supply the data, write the captions, or render anything. Stephen grades the data; Wayne captions; Nancy clears any figure naming living people; the diagrams workflow renders.

Audio-survivability constraint (rule 12 V10): the book is committed to working read aloud. A figure is always an AID, never the place the recognition happens. Every figure proposed below sits on top of prose that already delivers the click without it; a listener with no figure must still get there. The book has already established the right pattern for this — chapters 4 and 12 carry an explicit "audio-form rendering" paragraph immediately before their tables, so the listener hears the table's content in sentence form and the reader sees it laid out. Every table-type figure on the shortlist inherits that requirement: it must be paired with an audio-rendering paragraph, or it does not ship. No figure on this map is permitted to carry load the prose should carry; where a chapter's recognition is already fully prose-borne and a figure would only decorate, the entry says so plainly.

A note on what the book already does. This is a table-literate manuscript. Chapters 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 11, and 12 each already carry one comparison or channel table in v5 prose. Several of those are strong and should simply be *formalized* as designed figures (consistent visual grammar, audio-rendering paragraph confirmed) rather than invented. The genuine gaps are: (1) the book's SIGNATURE visual — the responsibility-chain map contrasting the publicly-blamed party against the control/benefit/knowledge/preventability/record/cost chain — appears *nowhere* as a figure, only in prose and in case-file YAML; and (2) two chapters (8, 9) whose central architecture is currently prose-only and would carry one well-chosen figure. The risk for this book is over-figuring, not under-figuring. The shortlist is deliberately short.
