## Cross-cutting rules (apply to every candidate)

1. **No victim-injury imagery.** Grimshaw burns, the iconic Bhopal buried-child photograph, graphic Abu Ghraib frames — out of tone with the book's analytical coldness and a dignity/rights risk. Exclude.
2. **Abu Ghraib appears twice** (ch-02 partial-scapegoat install; ch-10 war). Default: **exclude** (Nancy NO-GO on both). If an edition insists, detainee-only / redacted crop, heavy status caption, Stephen provenance — never soldier-faces.

4. **Document images are the safe, high-yield class.** Gazette pages, court orders, the Federal Register, system/model cards, leaderboards, the Powell vial, the PROFS note — favor these over people-photographs.
5. **No caption may name a Meta / OpenAI individual** (ch-09), nor assert command / intent / causation beyond the record (war chapters).

  **Worked example of a verification (one image, run 2026-05-29).** A candidate image for this story — a BBC-hosted frame (`ichef.bbci.co.uk/.../013a7dd0-1ca8-11f1-...`, the 976px WebP render) — shows young children eating in an institutional canteen, calm and well-fed. Running the actual chain:
  - The **downloaded file is provenance-inert**: WebP/VP8 CDN derivative, all EXIF/IPTC/XMP stripped; the only timestamps are the download date. Seeing the pixels verifies nothing.
  - The **BBC article page** carries it: "Russia's deportation of Ukrainian children amounts to crime against humanity, UN says" (BBC, 10 Mar 2026). Credit `copyrightHolder: "AP"` / "Image source, AP". Caption (verbatim): *"Most of the children mentioned in the report, like these orphans at a camp in southern Russia, are from Donetsk and Luhansk regions of Ukraine."* Original 1856×1044.
  - **What that resolves:** owner = AP (a wire service, **not** a Russian-state handout) → wire license required, not public domain.
  - **What it does NOT resolve (the trap persists):** (1) the photo was shot *at a camp in southern Russia* — i.e. under Russian-granted press access — so an independent credit does not mean an uncurated scene; (2) "orphans" is a **contested Russian-framing label** (children classified as orphans to justify adoption); echoing it uncritically re-performs the alibi; (3) the caption's claims are **B-grade journalism** — the A-grade anchor is the UN Commission of Inquiry finding, which is what the chapter must cite; (4) photographer name, exact date, AP image ID, and precise location remain unverified (they live in AP's archive, not on the BBC page).
  - **Standing rule this demonstrates for every photo:** verification = resolve to the original distributor → confirm owner/photographer/date/place → grade the caption's claims → flag adversary-facilitated access → caption to documented (A-grade) status, never to the wire caption's framing. None of this is shortcut by the image resolving or by holding the file.
