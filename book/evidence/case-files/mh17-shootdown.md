# MH17 — The Buk, the Brigade, and the Boundary the Proxy Could Not Hide

Case: Shoot-down of Malaysia Airlines Flight MH17 by a Buk surface-to-air missile over eastern Ukraine; subsequent attribution to a unit of the Russian 53rd Anti-Aircraft Missile Brigade and conviction in absentia of three individuals at The Hague.
Domain: war / statecraft / proxy attribution / civilian harm
Dates/place: 17 July 2014, ~13:20 UTC, eastern Donetsk Oblast (Ukrainian sovereign territory under partial control of armed groups designated "DPR"); investigations 2014–2022 (Joint Investigation Team); trial 2020–2022 (The Hague District Court at Schiphol); inter-state proceedings (Netherlands and Ukraine v. Russia, ECtHR) ongoing.
Case type: partial scapegoat

Classification note: Hybrid per `rules/01-case-taxonomy.md` and
`.claude/skills/taxonomy-classification/SKILL.md`. The chapter's mechanism is
proxy-deniability across a state border; the book's taxonomy resolves it as:
  - **Partial scapegoat (primary):** the visible blame container during the
    operational window and after was the "DPR" armed group — a real
    participant in the chain (per the JIT, the crew that fired the Buk
    operated in territory controlled by DPR forces and coordinated with
    DPR command). Blame stopped at the DPR / individual operators and
    was meant not to climb to the Russian Federation, which supplied
    the Buk and to which the launch vehicle returned. Three named
    individuals were convicted in absentia at The Hague (17 November
    2022); their personal culpability is adjudicated.
  - **System/object alibi (secondary):** the "DPR" entity itself
    functions as a non-state legal-political shell; Russian denials
    have invoked alternative-cause framings (Ukrainian Su-25, "Buk
    from Ukrainian stocks") that try to relocate causation onto
    Ukrainian state systems. The book's chapter must address both
    layers.

Crisis:
  At 16:20 local time (13:20 UTC) on 17 July 2014, Malaysia
  Airlines Flight MH17, a Boeing 777-200ER en route from
  Amsterdam Schiphol to Kuala Lumpur, broke up in flight over
  eastern Donetsk Oblast, Ukraine. All 298 people on board
  were killed: 196 Dutch nationals, 43 Malaysians (including
  the crew of 15), 27 Australians, 12 Indonesians, 10 British
  nationals, four Germans, four Belgians, three Filipinos,
  one Canadian, one New Zealander. Wreckage fell across an
  active conflict zone in territory partially controlled by
  armed groups designating themselves the "Donetsk People's
  Republic" (DPR). The Dutch Safety Board (Onderzoeksraad
  voor Veiligheid, OVV) was given lead responsibility for the
  air-safety investigation under ICAO Annex 13 protocols; a
  separate Joint Investigation Team (JIT) led by the
  Netherlands and including Australia, Belgium, Malaysia, and
  Ukraine was constituted for the criminal investigation.

Official story (Russian government):
  Russian officials advanced multiple alternative-cause
  framings sequentially: (a) that a Ukrainian Su-25
  ground-attack aircraft had been observed near MH17;
  (b) that the Buk missile system involved had been supplied
  to Ukrainian forces from Soviet-era stocks held by Ukraine;
  (c) that the Buk used belonged to the Ukrainian 156th
  Anti-Aircraft Missile Regiment. The Russian Ministry of
  Defence held a 21 July 2014 press conference advancing
  these claims; subsequent statements were issued by the
  Russian MoD, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, and the
  manufacturer Almaz-Antey (which held its own press
  briefings in June and October 2015). Russia consistently
  denied that any Russian military unit was involved.

Blame container (during the operational window and after):
  Primary: the "Donetsk People's Republic" armed group — the
  category functions as a non-state legal-political shell into
  which command-and-control for the Buk crew was meant to
  disappear. Secondary: the named individual operators —
  Igor Girkin (a.k.a. "Strelkov"), Sergey Dubinsky, Leonid
  Kharchenko, and Oleg Pulatov — as DPR-affiliated commanders,
  not as agents of the Russian Federation. The JIT named
  these four as suspects in June 2019; The Hague District
  Court at Schiphol convicted Girkin, Dubinsky, and Kharchenko
  in absentia on 17 November 2022 of murder of all 298
  persons on board and of causing the crash of an aircraft
  resulting in death; Pulatov was acquitted. All four were
  tried in absentia; none was in Dutch custody. None has
  served the sentences.

Actual responsibility chain (as the record now stands):
  1. The crew that fired the Buk surface-to-air missile from
     a launch site near Pervomaiskyi, eastern Ukraine, on
     17 July 2014. The JIT and Dutch court findings:
     individuals named above operated within DPR
     command-and-control structures coordinated with Russian
     military authorities.
  2. The Russian 53rd Anti-Aircraft Missile Brigade,
     Kursk-based (Russian Federation, Western Military
     District): the JIT concluded on 24 May 2018 that the
     Buk-TELAR (Transporter Erector Launcher And Radar) used
     to shoot down MH17 came from the 53rd Brigade's
     inventory and was transported from Russia to Ukrainian
     territory on 17 July 2014 and returned to Russia
     thereafter. Bellingcat open-source-intelligence
     investigations (2015–2019) and the JIT presentation of
     14 November 2019 identified the convoy route, the
     specific Buk by serial-number-equivalent markings, and
     individual Russian servicemen by name, rank, and
     telephone-intercept identification. Some Russian
     servicemen so identified are designated by the JIT as
     persons of interest; further criminal proceedings remain
     possible.
  3. Russian military and intelligence leadership: the JIT
     announced on 8 February 2023 that there are "strong
     indications" that the decision to supply the Buk was
     taken at a level above brigade — naming, in the JIT's
     phrasing, that this included presidential-level
     awareness; the JIT closed the criminal investigation
     citing insufficient evidence to bring further criminal
     charges to court. The 8 February 2023 statement is a
     prosecutorial finding short of indictment, not a court
     verdict; chapter wording must preserve the procedural
     gap.
  4. Russian state: the European Court of Human Rights in
     *Ukraine and the Netherlands v. Russia*, applications
     nos. 8019/16, 43800/14, and 28525/20, Grand Chamber
     admissibility decision of 30 November 2022, found
     Russia had exercised jurisdiction (effective control)
     over the territory from which the Buk was launched
     since 11 May 2014. Subsequent ECtHR Grand Chamber
     judgment of 9 July 2025 in the joined cases on merits
     found multiple violations of the Convention, including
     in respect of MH17. Chapter must reflect the most
     recent ECtHR posture as of draft-ready date.

Control:
  Operational control of the Buk-TELAR: per JIT findings,
  Russian armed forces (53rd Brigade personnel) for transit;
  DPR-affiliated crew for the launch itself. Strategic
  control of the supply: per the JIT's 8 February 2023
  statement, decisions to supply the Buk to forces in
  eastern Ukraine were taken at a senior Russian command
  level; the JIT framing reaches the Office of the President.
  Territorial control of the launch site: per the ECtHR
  Grand Chamber admissibility finding, Russia exercised
  effective control over the territory from which the Buk
  was launched, from 11 May 2014.

Benefit:
  The named beneficiary in the operational frame was the
  armed-group offensive against Ukrainian air assets during
  July 2014; Russia's broader benefit was the destabilization
  of eastern Ukraine via deniable support, of which the Buk
  supply was one instance. No benefit framing in this case
  treats civilian deaths as intended; the JIT and OVV
  findings characterize MH17 as misidentification of a
  civilian airliner as a military target.

Knowledge:
  Russian military knowledge of the Buk's deployment and
  return is documentary at JIT-source level (intercepted
  communications; matched satellite imagery; convoy
  reconstruction). The JIT's 8 February 2023 statement on
  presidential-level awareness is a prosecutorial finding,
  not a court verdict; the chapter must say so.

Preventability:
  Each step was preventable: do not supply a Buk-TELAR to
  an armed group in an active conflict zone with civilian
  air traffic above; close the relevant flight corridors
  (Ukrainian state aviation authority had restricted some
  airspace below FL320 but kept higher altitudes open; ICAO
  reviews followed); do not authorize the cross-border
  movement.

Record controller:
  The record is split. JIT (led by the Dutch Public
  Prosecutor): criminal evidence, partially public. OVV
  (Dutch Safety Board): air-safety report, public (13
  October 2015). The Hague District Court at Schiphol:
  trial record (2020–2022), judgment public (17 November
  2022). Bellingcat and other OSINT teams: open-source
  reconstructions, public. Russian MoD: held the
  internal-record of unit movements; some publicly
  released (e.g., 21 July 2014 press conference) and later
  contradicted by JIT findings. Almaz-Antey:
  manufacturer's reports (June and October 2015) — the
  manufacturer's own technical position changed across
  the two releases.

Cost bearer:
  The 298 dead and their families. The crash site
  community in eastern Ukraine. The Ukrainian state
  (sovereign territory, civilian airspace authority).
  The Netherlands as a state and the families of the 196
  Dutch nationals killed (the Dutch state took on the
  lead-investigation cost and decade-long litigation
  burden). Malaysia, Australia, and the other countries
  of nationality.

How the alibi hardened:
  - The DPR shell — a non-state legal-political category
    not recognized by Ukraine, the EU, the US, or the UN —
    into which command for the Buk crew was meant to
    disappear.
  - The unmarked or repainted Buk-TELAR: convoy footage
    and photographs from 17–18 July 2014 show a Buk
    moving with reduced markings.
  - Russian-state alternative-cause framings advanced
    consecutively: the Su-25 claim; the "Ukrainian Buk
    from Ukrainian stocks" claim; the 156th Regiment
    attribution. Each alternative attempted to relocate
    causation onto Ukrainian state systems.
  - The Almaz-Antey press conferences of June 2015
    (claiming the missile was a 9M38, supplied to
    Ukrainian forces in 1986 and used by Ukraine) and
    October 2015 (claiming the launch had come from
    Ukrainian-controlled Zaroshchenske, not from
    DPR-controlled Pervomaiskyi). Both were addressed in
    the OVV report (October 2015) and the JIT
    presentation (September 2016) by physical and
    forensic analysis.
  - Russia's June 2015 veto of UN Security Council
    Resolution S/2015/562, which would have established a
    UNSC-mandated international tribunal for MH17.

How the alibi weakened:
  - OVV final report, 13 October 2015: cause was a
    9N314M warhead carried by a 9M38-series missile
    launched by a Buk system.
  - JIT presentation of 28 September 2016: launch site
    identification near Pervomaiskyi; the Buk had been
    brought into Ukraine from Russia and returned the
    same night.
  - JIT presentation of 24 May 2018: identification of
    the Buk as originating from the Russian 53rd
    Anti-Aircraft Missile Brigade, Kursk.
  - JIT presentation of 19 June 2019: indictment of
    Girkin, Dubinsky, Kharchenko, and Pulatov for
    murder of 298 persons and causing the crash of an
    aircraft resulting in death.
  - The Hague District Court at Schiphol, judgment of
    17 November 2022: convictions in absentia of
    Girkin, Dubinsky, and Kharchenko on both counts;
    acquittal of Pulatov; life imprisonment imposed on
    the three convicted; ordered compensation to next
    of kin.
  - JIT closing statement, 8 February 2023: closure of
    the criminal investigation absent further evidence
    permitting prosecution of additional suspects;
    statement of "strong indications" of senior-level
    Russian decision-making in supplying the Buk.
  - ECtHR Grand Chamber admissibility decision of 30
    November 2022 and subsequent merits judgment in
    *Ukraine and the Netherlands v. Russia*.
  - UN Security Council Resolution 2166 of 21 July 2014
    (passed unanimously, including by Russia) demanded
    international access to the crash site and a full,
    independent international investigation.
  - The Council of the International Civil Aviation
    Organization (ICAO Council) found, on 12 May 2025,
    that the Russian Federation had violated its
    obligations under Article 3 bis of the Chicago
    Convention in respect of MH17 — the first such
    finding by ICAO against a state.

Best counterargument:
  Strongest defense of the Russian official position: the
  JIT is led by the Netherlands, a country whose own
  citizens died in the largest number, raising a question
  of investigatory neutrality; the JIT investigation
  relied in part on intercepted communications whose
  chain-of-custody Russia disputes; the Dutch court tried
  the four individuals in absentia, without
  cross-examination of the defendants. Reply: the JIT's
  evidentiary base is multi-modal (intercepts,
  satellite, photographic, witness, physical fragment
  analysis, Almaz-Antey's own admissions of missile
  type) and was tested adversarially in a national court
  judgment in the Netherlands. Pulatov's acquittal —
  the only defendant represented by counsel at trial —
  is itself evidence that the court differentiated the
  evidence per defendant.

  Second counterargument the chapter must address:
  attributing the launch decision to the Russian state
  (as opposed to the DPR-affiliated crew that operated
  the launch) rests on the JIT's 8 February 2023
  prosecutorial finding, not on a court verdict. Reply:
  the chapter must say so. The "strong indications" of
  presidential-level awareness are a prosecutorial
  conclusion at the close of investigation; they are not
  a criminal judgment. The convictions are of the three
  named individuals, not of the Russian state. The state-
  responsibility frame is supplied separately by the
  ECtHR, not by The Hague District Court.

Evidence grade: A

  shootdown (OVV report); (2) attribution of the Buk to
  the Russian 53rd Anti-Aircraft Missile Brigade (JIT

  convictions of Girkin, Dubinsky, and Kharchenko and

  (4) the ECtHR Grand Chamber admissibility decision of

  (5) the ICAO Council finding of 12 May 2025. B for

  not court verdict. C for any further individual-level
  attribution within the 53rd Brigade beyond the
  individuals named by the JIT.
  Procedural-stage cap: convictions in absentia are
  convictions for chapter use, provided the chapter
  states that they were rendered in absentia and that
  the defendants have not served. The three convicted
  individuals — Igor Vsevolodovich Girkin, Sergey
  Nikolayevich Dubinsky, Leonid Volodymyrovych
  Kharchenko — may be named with the convicted-in-

  Russian-Federation-level state attribution: use the
  ECtHR finding for state responsibility, not the JIT's
  closing statement, for the strongest legally-anchored
  framing. Presidential-level awareness language
  requires the JIT-prosecutorial-finding hedge.

  Proxy and the Sponsor"). Demonstrates: (a) the visible

  deniability whose alibi collapsed on the sponsor's
