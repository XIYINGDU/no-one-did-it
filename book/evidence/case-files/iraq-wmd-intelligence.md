# Iraq WMD Intelligence — "The Intelligence Was Wrong"

Case: Iraq WMD intelligence and the 2003 war justification
Domain: war / statecraft / intelligence-policy interface
Dates/place: 2001–2003 case-construction window; 2004–2016 inquiry
window; Washington DC, London, UN Security Council, CIA HQ Langley,
Office of the Vice President, BND Munich, UNMOVIC operations in Iraq.
Case type: system/object alibi
Secondary case type: pure scapegoat (Curveball / Rafid Ahmed Alwan al-Janabi as the late-stage blame-container node)

Classification note: The dominant post-war framing — "the intelligence
was wrong" — reified the intelligence community's product as the
named cause of the war, absorbing what the Senate Phase II Report
(May 25, 2007) and the Chilcot Inquiry (Jul 6, 2016) both found
to have been a *policy* choice: senior officials presented certainty
the underlying intelligence did not support. That is the system/object
alibi (Block 3). A pure scapegoat secondary fires for Rafid Ahmed
Alwan al-Janabi ("Curveball"), the BND-handled Iraqi defector whose
fabricated mobile bioweapons-lab story was repackaged in Powell's
Feb 5, 2003 UN presentation; the Robb-Silberman Commission (Mar 31,
2005) and Curveball's own 2011 Guardian admission cast him as the
late-stage blame container for a chain that had multiple opportunities
to discount his account. CIA Director George Tenet ("slam dunk")
functions as a partial-scapegoat node within the same chain.

```text
Crisis:
  Between the September 11, 2001 attacks and the March 19, 2003 US-led
  invasion of Iraq, the Bush administration and the Blair government
  publicly asserted that Iraq possessed active weapons-of-mass-destruction
  programs (chemical, biological, and reconstituting nuclear) and posed
  an imminent or near-imminent threat justifying preventive war. The
  October 1, 2002 National Intelligence Estimate, "Iraq's Continuing
  Programs for Weapons of Mass Destruction" (NIE 2002-16HC), summarized
  to "Iraq is reconstituting its nuclear weapons program" and asserted
  with "high confidence" that Iraq possessed chemical and biological
  weapons. Powell's Feb 5, 2003 UN Security Council presentation made
  the public case using mobile-biolab claims sourced primarily to
  Curveball and aluminum-tube claims contested inside the IC. After
  the invasion, the Iraq Survey Group's Duelfer Report (Sep 30, 2004,
  with Mar 2005 addenda) concluded that Iraq's WMD programs had been
  effectively dismantled by 1991 sanctions and that no stockpiles
  existed. The justification had collapsed.

Official story (post-collapse framing):
  "The intelligence was wrong." President George W. Bush, in multiple
  statements from 2004 onward, attributed the WMD claims to intelligence
  community failure (Bush, Dec 14, 2005 remarks at Woodrow Wilson
  Center: "It is true that much of the intelligence turned out to be
  wrong"). Tony Blair similarly attributed the British case to
  intelligence assessments later found "established beyond doubt"
  language he subsequently characterized as having been the IC's
  judgment. The Robb-Silberman Commission (Mar 31, 2005) was
  chartered to investigate the intelligence community's performance
  and was explicitly barred by its founding executive order from
  examining how policy-makers used the intelligence. That charter
  scoped the alibi: the investigation looked where the alibi pointed,
  not where the responsibility chain led.

Blame containers:
  1. The intelligence community as institutional object — "the IC got
     it wrong." The October 2002 NIE became the documentary fulcrum.
  2. CIA Director George J. Tenet, whose reported "slam dunk" remark
     (recounted in Bob Woodward's *Plan of Attack*, 2004, p. 249) was
     deployed publicly as evidence the IC, not the White House, had
     pushed certainty. Tenet resigned Jun 3, 2004; received the
     Presidential Medal of Freedom Dec 14, 2004; published his own
     account (*At the Center of the Storm*, 2007) disputing the
     framing.
  3. Curveball / Rafid Ahmed Alwan al-Janabi — the Iraqi chemical
     engineer who defected to Germany in 1999, was debriefed by the
     BND, and provided the mobile-bioweapons-lab descriptions that
     became the load-bearing image of Powell's UN presentation. The
     BND warned the CIA that Curveball was unreliable; the warnings
     were not load-bearing in the chain that produced Powell's text.
     Al-Janabi self-identified in a Guardian interview (Feb 15, 2011)
     and stated he had fabricated the account hoping to provoke
     regime change.
  4. Specific analysts and line offices within the CIA, DIA, and
     INR — named in the Robb-Silberman Report's analytic-tradecraft
     critique without parallel critique of policy use.

Actual responsibility chain:
  1. Office of the Vice President (Richard B. Cheney; chief of staff
     I. Lewis "Scooter" Libby): the Senate Phase II Report (May 25,
     2007), specifically the "Postwar Findings" component, documented
     that public statements by senior officials, including the Vice
     President, exceeded what the underlying intelligence supported
     on (a) the alleged Iraq–al-Qaeda operational relationship and
     (b) the alleged Mohamed Atta–Prague meeting. Pressure on
     analytic lines via multi-visit trips by the VP and chief of
     staff to CIA HQ (Langley) during 2002 is documented in SSCI
     Phase I (Jul 7, 2004) Conclusions on the IC's analytic
     environment.
  2. National Security Council / White House Iraq Group (WHIG): a
     communications coordination group formed in Aug 2002 (the New
     York Times reported its existence Sep 7, 2002) to shape the
     public case; its members included Karl Rove, Karen Hughes, Mary
     Matalin, James R. Wilkinson, Nicholas E. Calio, Condoleezza
     Rice, Stephen Hadley, and Libby. The Chilcot Report documents
     parallel UK coordination (the "September Dossier" production
     under Alastair Campbell's communications oversight).
  3. Office of the Secretary of Defense (Donald H. Rumsfeld; Under
     Secretary for Policy Douglas J. Feith): the OSD Office of
     Special Plans (OSP) produced and circulated alternative
     intelligence assessments emphasizing Iraq–al-Qaeda ties. The
     DoD Inspector General report on Feith's office (Feb 9, 2007)
     found the OSP work "did not provide 'the most accurate
     analysis of intelligence to senior decisionmakers'" and that
     conclusions were "inappropriate" because they were presented
     as IC-vetted assessments when they were not.
  4. CIA leadership and the analytic line: Tenet and Deputy Director
     John E. McLaughlin signed off on the Oct 2002 NIE, including
     its "high confidence" judgments on chemical and biological
     stockpiles. The Phase I Report concluded that the NIE's key
     judgments were "either overstated, or were not supported by,
     the underlying intelligence reporting" (SSCI Phase I, Jul 7,
     2004, Conclusion 1).
  5. State Department: Powell, with then-Chief of Staff Lawrence B.
     Wilkerson, prepared the UN presentation over four days at
     Langley (late Jan–early Feb 2003). Wilkerson stated publicly
     (interviews including PBS Frontline "The Dark Side," Jun 20,
     2006, and CNN's "Late Edition" Aug 19, 2007) that material was
     "pushed" into the speech from non-INR sources over INR
     objections. Powell publicly disavowed the presentation in
     multiple subsequent statements (e.g., Powell on ABC's *This
     Week*, Sep 8, 2005: "I'm the one who presented it on behalf of
     the United States to the world, and [it] will always be a part
     of my record. It was painful. It's painful now").
  6. UK Cabinet Office and 10 Downing Street: the Chilcot Report
     (Jul 6, 2016) found that judgments about Iraq's WMD capabilities
     "were presented with a certainty that was not justified"
     (Executive Summary, ¶ 499), that the legal basis for war "was
     far from satisfactory" (Executive Summary, ¶¶ 875–876), and
     that the September Dossier (Sep 24, 2002) presented the JIC
     assessments with "a degree of certainty that was not justified"
     (Executive Summary, ¶ 510).
  7. The BND (Bundesnachrichtendienst, German foreign intelligence):
     handled Curveball, transmitted his reporting with caveats
     including a German liaison warning Tenet directly that Curveball
     should not be considered reliable for public statements
     (documented in Drumheller's Senate testimony and *On the Brink*,
     2006). The caveats were not load-bearing in the Powell speech
     drafting.

Control:
  Control over the public WMD case sat with the White House (WHIG),
  the Vice President's office, OSD policy, and 10 Downing Street.
  Control over the analytic product sat with Tenet, McLaughlin, the
  National Intelligence Council, and the line analysts who drafted
  the Oct 2002 NIE under compressed timelines (the NIE was produced
  in approximately three weeks; SSCI Phase I documents the time
  pressure). Control over the final UN-presentation text sat with
  Powell, Wilkerson, and the OVP/NSC drafters who, per Wilkerson,
  pushed material in. The Chilcot Report found that the UK decision
  to commit to military action was effectively taken by Blair
  ahead of the formal Cabinet process (Executive Summary, ¶¶ 374–432
  on the path to decision).

Benefit:
  Policy benefit: the WMD framing produced UN Security Council
  Resolution 1441 (Nov 8, 2002), congressional Authorization for Use
  of Military Force Against Iraq (Oct 16, 2002, P.L. 107-243), and
  domestic political coalition for the war. Institutional benefit:
  expanded OSD authorities, expanded surveillance and detention
  authorities downstream. Career benefit: most senior architects
  remained in or rose to higher positions; Tenet received the
  Presidential Medal of Freedom (Dec 14, 2004); Bremer received the
  same; Rice was promoted to Secretary of State (Jan 26, 2005);
  Hadley succeeded Rice as National Security Adviser. None of the
  senior officials named in SSCI Phase II or Chilcot faced criminal
  process; none lost their pensions or security clearances on this
  record alone.

Knowledge:
  - SSCI Phase II "Postwar Findings on the Use by the Intelligence
    Community of Information Provided by the Iraqi National Congress"
    (Sep 8, 2006) and "Report on Whether Public Statements Regarding
    Iraq by U.S. Government Officials Were Substantiated by
    Intelligence Information" (May 25, 2007) documented that public
    statements on Iraq's nuclear program, Iraq–al-Qaeda links,
    aluminum tubes, and the Atta–Prague meeting exceeded the
    intelligence record available at the time of those statements.
  - The Drumheller account (Tyler Drumheller, then chief of the CIA
    European Division): German liaison warned Tenet personally that
    Curveball was unreliable; account given in Senate testimony and
    in *On the Brink* (2006), plus 60 Minutes interview Apr 23, 2006.
    Tenet's *At the Center of the Storm* disputes whether the warning
    reached him personally before the Powell speech.
  - DoD IG Report on the OSP (Feb 9, 2007) documented that Feith's
    office knew its conclusions were not IC-vetted yet presented
    them in IC-vetted form.
  - Chilcot found that JIC chair John Scarlett's drafting process
    for the September 2002 Dossier was insufficiently independent of
    No. 10's communications operation (Chilcot Executive Summary,
    ¶¶ 498–510).

Preventability:
  Multiple inflection points where the chain could have been broken:
  - The Sep 2002 NIE compressed-timeline production (the IC could
    have refused; SSCI Phase I documents the time pressure imposed
    by Senate Democrats requesting an NIE before the AUMF vote, and
    the IC's accommodation rather than refusal).
  - The Drumheller-route Curveball warning to Tenet (the speech
    could have dropped the mobile-biolab claim).
  - INR objections to the aluminum-tubes and Niger-yellowcake claims
    (recorded as footnotes in the NIE rather than as headline
    dissents).
  - IAEA Director General Mohamed ElBaradei's Mar 7, 2003 Security
    Council report finding the Niger documents were forgeries
    ("based on a thorough analysis, the IAEA has concluded, with the
    concurrence of outside experts, that these documents…are in fact
    not authentic"). The invasion proceeded twelve days later.
  - UNMOVIC Executive Chairman Hans Blix's reports through Jan–Mar
    2003 finding cooperation increasing and no WMD stockpiles
    located despite increasingly intrusive inspections.

Record controller:
  Initially the executive branch (CIA, OSD, OVP, NSC, No. 10). The
  decisive break came from outside-executive venues:
  - Senate Select Committee on Intelligence under Pat Roberts
    (chair) and Jay Rockefeller (vice-chair / later chair):
    Phase I (Jul 7, 2004) and Phase II (released in components
    Sep 8, 2006 and May 25, 2007). Phase II was delayed two years
    by intra-committee dispute; the May 2007 report finally
    documented the public-statement gap.
  - The Robb-Silberman Commission (Mar 31, 2005), even within its
    intelligence-only charter, documented Curveball as a
    "fabricator" (Robb-Silberman Report, Chapter 1, "The Biological
    Warfare Story").
  - The Iraq Inquiry under Sir John Chilcot (announced Jun 15, 2009;
    reported Jul 6, 2016): 12 volumes, 2.6 million words, 150
    witnesses including Blair, Brown, Straw, Hoon, Scarlett,
    Powell (Jonathan), Campbell. The report's authority came from
    its subpoena access to UK Cabinet papers, the Manning–Rice
    memos, and the Bush–Blair correspondence ("I will be with you,
    whatever," Blair to Bush, Jul 28, 2002).
  - The Duelfer Report (Iraq Survey Group, Sep 30, 2004) as the
    on-the-ground forensic record that no stockpiles existed.
  - Investigative journalism: Risen, Lichtblau, Drogin's *Curveball*
    (2007); Suskind's *The One Percent Doctrine* (2006); the
    Knight-Ridder reporting by Warren Strobel and Jonathan Landay
    that dissented from the consensus pre-invasion coverage.

Cost bearer:
  Cost bearers across multiple registers:
  - Iraqis: documented civilian deaths from the invasion and
    occupation. Estimates vary by methodology; the Iraq Body Count
    project (passive media-monitoring of confirmed deaths) recorded
    approximately 100,000–110,000 documented civilian deaths from
    violence 2003–2011 (IBC database, accessed for Chilcot-era
    estimates). The Lancet/Roberts et al. (Oct 11, 2006) household
    survey estimated approximately 654,000 excess deaths through
    Jun 2006; methodology contested. The Iraq Family Health Survey
    (NEJM, Jan 31, 2008) estimated approximately 151,000 violent
    deaths Mar 2003–Jun 2006. Use the IBC figure as the documented
    floor; the survey-based estimates as contested upper bounds;
    flag the methodology dispute, not the underlying fact of
    large-scale civilian death.
  - US, UK, and coalition military: approximately 4,491 US military
    deaths (DoD, Operation Iraqi Freedom + Operation New Dawn
    through 2011); 179 UK military deaths (UK MoD); thousands more
    coalition-contractor and security-personnel deaths recorded
    separately.
  - Displaced Iraqis: UNHCR estimated 2 million internally displaced
    and 2 million refugees by 2007.
  - Curveball / al-Janabi: subsequently treated as the documented
    fabricator in the public record, though structurally one defector
    among many sources in a chain that had multiple opportunities to
    discount him. He is the pure-scapegoat node within the larger
    system/object alibi: the fabrication is real, but the chain that
    used it bears the laundering charge.
  - Iraqi institutional capacity, infrastructure, and historical
    archive (museum looting, ministry destruction, de-Baathification
    of professional civil service) — costs that fall on the
    population for decades.

How alibi hardened:
  - "Slam dunk." The Tenet remark, sourced through Woodward, became
    a media shorthand absorbing senior White House certainty into a
    single CIA director's word.
  - Robb-Silberman charter scope. The executive order (Feb 6, 2004)
    establishing the commission excluded examination of how
    policy-makers used the intelligence, fencing the alibi.
  - Tenet's resignation (Jun 3, 2004) and Medal of Freedom (Dec 14,
    2004) symbolically closed the IC chapter while simultaneously
    rewarding the senior actor whose "wrong intelligence" took the
    blame.
  - Powell's gradual disavowal of the UN speech reframed his role as
    victim of the IC product rather than as participant in the
    drafting (Wilkerson disputes this framing).
  - Phase II delay (2004–2007) meant the documentary evidence that
    public statements exceeded the intelligence record arrived after
    the 2004 reelection and after the public conversation had moved
    on.
  - "Everyone thought so." The widespread pre-war international
    consensus (UN inspectors continued to investigate; France,
    Germany, and Russia opposed war but not on the grounds that
    Iraq had no weapons program at all) was retrofitted as a
    universal IC failure rather than as a question about how
    certainty was manufactured from contested assessments.

How alibi weakened:
  - Iraq Survey Group Duelfer Report (Sep 30, 2004; addenda Mar
    2005): no stockpiles, no active programs. The forensic floor.
  - Robb-Silberman Report (Mar 31, 2005): identified Curveball as a
    fabricator; even within its charter, documented chain-of-handling
    failures specific to the bioweapons claim.
  - SSCI Phase I (Jul 7, 2004): NIE key judgments "either overstated,
    or were not supported by, the underlying intelligence reporting"
    (Conclusion 1).
  - SSCI Phase II (Sep 8, 2006 and May 25, 2007): public statements
    by senior officials exceeded the intelligence record.
  - DoD IG Report on OSP (Feb 9, 2007).
  - Curveball self-identification (Guardian, Feb 15, 2011: "I gave
    this information to my country first… I had the chance to
    fabricate something to topple the regime. I and my sons are
    proud of that").
  - Chilcot Report (Jul 6, 2016): senior UK decision-making
    documented as committing to military action on a basis the
    intelligence did not support; the legal basis "far from
    satisfactory."

Best counterargument:
  "Many intelligence services, including those of governments that
  opposed the war (France, Germany, Russia), believed Iraq had active
  WMD programs as of late 2002. Iraq's documented history of WMD use
  (chemical weapons against Iran and Kurdish civilians, 1980s) and
  its post-1991 obstruction of UNMOVIC's predecessor UNSCOM gave
  reasonable basis to conclude programs persisted. Saddam Hussein
  himself, after capture, told FBI interviewer George Piro he had
  cultivated WMD ambiguity to deter Iran. Therefore the assessments
  reflected genuine uncertainty rather than a manufactured case, and
  the policy choice to act on that uncertainty is contestable on the
  merits but not laundering."

  Response: this counterargument is the steelman the chapter must
  answer, not dismiss. The book's claim is not that the underlying
  assessments were certainly fabricated; it is that the *gap between
  the underlying assessments and the public statements* was
  systematically widened by policy-makers and then attributed back
  to the IC after collapse. The SSCI Phase II Report and Chilcot are
  the load-bearing documents because they specifically measure that
  gap. The widespread foreign-intelligence consensus argument is
  partially correct on the prior; it does not reach the public-
  statements-vs-intelligence-record gap that SSCI Phase II and
  Chilcot establish. Saddam's deterrence ambiguity strategy is real
  and is documented in the FBI interrogation reports (declassified
  Jul 2009) — and it explains some, not all, of the chain. The
  laundering charge attaches specifically to the system/object alibi
  ("the IC got it wrong") that absorbed the policy choice ("we
  presented certainty the IC's product did not support").

  Second counterargument: "The Robb-Silberman charter, not the
  commission's findings, is what fenced the inquiry; the commission
  itself acted in good faith." Response: agreed; the charter is the
  fence. The laundering operates at the structural choice to charter
  an IC-only commission rather than a use-of-intelligence commission.
  Chilcot operated under a broader charter and reached the policy-use
  question; SSCI Phase II eventually did too.

  Third counterargument: "Curveball is genuinely culpable; calling
  him a pure scapegoat understates his fabrication." Response: the
  pure-scapegoat-secondary classification holds. Curveball did
  fabricate; the laundering operates by treating his fabrication as
  load-bearing for the war when (a) the BND had transmitted
  caveats, (b) the CIA had not directly debriefed him, (c) multiple
  in-chain actors had discount opportunities, and (d) other claims
  (aluminum tubes, Niger yellowcake, Atta–Prague) were independently
  contested. His fabrication is real; its load-bearing status in the
  chain is what the laundering installed.
```
