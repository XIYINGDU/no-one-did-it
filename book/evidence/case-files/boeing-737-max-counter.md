# Boeing 737 MAX — Counter-Case (ch-5 echo for Bhopal partial-scapegoat anchor)

Counter-case name: The Lion Air KNKT and Ethiopian ECAA accident investigations — the institutional refusal to stop blame at the flight crews, and the climb of attribution to MCAS, Boeing's certification choices, and the FAA's ODA framework.
Paired scapegoat case: bhopal-union-carbide (ch-5 primary anchor — Anderson partial-scapegoat); boeing-737-max (the modern Boeing case file is the within-case substrate; this counter-case file documents the interception that pierced the pilots-blame layer).
Domain: international accident investigation under ICAO Annex 13; commercial aerospace; sovereign-regulator authority of foreign accident-investigation boards over US-manufactured aircraft.
Dates and place: October 29, 2018 (Lion Air JT 610, Java Sea off Karawang, Indonesia, 189 fatalities) → November 6, 2018 (Boeing Operations Manual Bulletin TBC-19 framing the AOA sensor as proximate cause) → November 28, 2018 (KNKT Preliminary Report, identifying MCAS-induced trim sequence) → March 10, 2019 (Ethiopian Airlines ET 302, Bishoftu near Addis Ababa, 157 fatalities) → April 4, 2019 (Ethiopian Minister of Transport press conference + ECAA Preliminary Report releasing FDR/CVR data showing crew executed the post-Lion-Air procedure) → October 25, 2019 (KNKT Final Report, KNKT.18.10.35.04, released to ICAO and the public) → March 9, 2020 (ECAA Interim Investigation Report, one-year anniversary of ET 302) → September 16, 2020 (US House T&I Committee Final Report) → January 7, 2021 (DOJ DPA Statement of Facts adopting MCAS / certification framing) → December 23, 2022 (ECAA Final Report, AI-01/19). Setting: Indonesian and Ethiopian accident-investigation authorities operating under ICAO Annex 13 with the NTSB as accredited representative, against early US-side and Boeing-side framing that emphasised crew procedure and maintenance.

Laundering attempt:
  Pattern (from Cross_Pattern_Matrix.md): Partial scapegoat by
  geographic / racial / training-deficit framing — the publicly
  blamed actors (the Lion Air and Ethiopian flight crews) were
  in the chain (they handled the malfunction) but the attribution
  was deliberately stopped at the crew level, with implied or
  explicit suggestion that "Western-trained" or "more experienced"
  crews would have recovered, in order to prevent responsibility
  from climbing to (i) Boeing's single-AOA-sensor MCAS architecture,
  (ii) the 2016 expansion of MCAS authority to a wider flight
  envelope that was not flagged to FAA reviewers as
  safety-significant, (iii) Boeing's decision not to disclose MCAS
  in the pilot training manual to preserve the Type Rating
  commercial differentiator, and (iv) the FAA Organization
  Designation Authorization (ODA) framework under which Boeing
  employees acting as FAA Authorized Representatives signed off
  on Boeing's own system safety analyses.

  Goat-candidate: The Lion Air JT 610 flight crew (Captain Bhavye
  Suneja, First Officer Harvino) and the Ethiopian ET 302 flight
  crew (Captain Yared Getachew, First Officer Ahmed Nur Mohammed
  Omer). Both crews were named in early Boeing-aligned framing
  as having failed to execute the existing "runaway-stabilizer"
  memory item. The Ethiopian first officer's 200 flight-hour
  experience and the Lion Air maintenance team's AOA-sensor
  replacement record were used in trade-press and official-source
  commentary to suggest the crashes were attributable to crew
  competence and operator practice rather than aircraft design.

  Alibi being constructed: A partial-scapegoat alibi — the crews
  were in the chain (they were piloting the aircraft when MCAS
  triggered) but blame stopped at the crew level, shielded by
  three load-bearing rhetorical structures:
  (a) the runaway-stabilizer procedure was framed as sufficient,
      making the crews' failure to recover the proximate cause;
  (b) the AOA sensor failure (Lion Air) and the bird strike
      hypothesis (early Ethiopian framing) were positioned as
      isolated faults, making the MCAS architecture's
      single-sensor design appear adequate;
  (c) the foreign provenance of the operators (Lion Air's then-
      EU blacklist history; Ethiopian's relatively young copilot)
      was used to suggest the crashes were attributable to
      operator practice rather than aircraft design.
  The alibi was hardened by Boeing's institutional weight, by the
  FAA's role as the home regulator of the manufacturer, and by
  the NTSB's participation under ICAO Annex 13 as accredited
  representative — a role that gave US authorities procedural
  proximity to both investigations but, crucially, no veto over
  the host-state investigation board's conclusions.

Interception mechanism:
  Type: independent technical verification + jurisdictional
  persistence — load-bearing institutional pair. Two sovereign
  accident-investigation authorities (KNKT and ECAA), operating
  under ICAO Annex 13 with US (NTSB), Boeing, and FAA as
  accredited representatives or technical advisers, refused to
  defer to the manufacturer-favorable framing and produced
  factual findings that compelled the laundering layer above
  the crew to be opened.

  Named institutional actor: Three load-bearing actors, sequenced:

  (1) KNKT — Komite Nasional Keselamatan Transportasi
      (Indonesia's National Transportation Safety Committee).
      Chair during the JT 610 investigation: Soerjanto Tjahjono.
      Investigator-in-Charge: Nurcahyo Utomo. KNKT's Preliminary
      Report of November 28, 2018 (one month after the crash)
      released the Flight Data Recorder readout showing the MCAS
      trim-down sequence — twenty-one nose-down trim commands by
      MCAS against the crew's manual trim corrections, on a single
      AOA sensor input that had been miscalibrated. The release of
      the FDR data shifted the public framing from "the crew did
      not follow procedure" to "the aircraft was repeatedly
      trimming itself nose-down based on a single faulty sensor."
      KNKT's Final Report (KNKT.18.10.35.04, October 25, 2019)
      concluded that nine contributing factors caused the
      accident, with MCAS design and Boeing's documentation /
      training assumptions named explicitly. KNKT issued
      recommendations to Boeing and the FAA, not solely to Lion
      Air.

  (2) ECAA — Ethiopian Civil Aviation Authority Aircraft Accident
      Investigation Bureau. Investigator-in-Charge: Amdye Ayalew
      Fanta. The Ethiopian Ministry of Transport, fronted by
      Minister Dagmawit Moges, held a press conference on April
      4, 2019 (twenty-five days after the crash) releasing
      preliminary FDR/CVR findings that showed the ET 302 crew
      had executed the post-Lion-Air guidance — they had
      identified the runaway-trim condition, engaged the stab
      trim cut-out switches per the Boeing/FAA Emergency
      Airworthiness Directive (2018-23-51), and were still
      unable to recover because manual trim wheel forces at
      high airspeed exceeded crew physical capacity. The
      Preliminary Report (issued the same day) and the Interim
      Investigation Report (March 9, 2020) and the Final Report
      (AI-01/19, December 23, 2022) sustained this finding
      against contested NTSB technical comments. The ECAA
      conclusion that the crew followed the procedures as
      written and were still unable to recover is the
      load-bearing factual finding that collapsed the
      pilot-error layer of the alibi.

  (3) The US House Committee on Transportation and
      Infrastructure (T&I), Majority Staff under Chairman Peter
      DeFazio and Aviation Subcommittee Chair Rick Larsen.
      Final Committee Report "The Boeing 737 MAX Aircraft:
      Costs, Consequences, and Lessons from its Design,
      Development, and Certification" issued September 16, 2020
      after eighteen months of investigation and the document
      productions the KNKT and ECAA findings had made
      politically unavoidable. The Committee's findings — five
      "broken processes" at Boeing and the FAA, including the
      conflicts of interest within the ODA framework — converted
      the foreign-investigation factual findings into a US
      institutional record. The Aircraft Certification, Safety,
      and Accountability Act of 2020 (Pub. L. 116-260, Division
      V, Title I, enacted December 27, 2020) followed.

  Key act of interception (what specifically happened): The
  ECAA press conference and Preliminary Report of April 4, 2019.
  The FDR/CVR release on that day showed the ET 302 crew had
  executed the runaway-stabilizer procedure as Boeing had said
  would be sufficient, and had still been unable to recover.
  The fact pattern made the pilot-error framing untenable
  within weeks; Boeing's first formal acknowledgment of MCAS's
  role in ET 302 followed the same day (Muilenburg statement,
  April 4, 2019). The KNKT FDR release of November 28, 2018 had
  primed the ground; the ECAA release of April 4, 2019 broke
  the alibi. The two foreign accident-investigation authorities,
  acting under their ICAO Annex 13 prerogative, refused to
  permit the laundering layer to remain closed.

  Date of interception: April 4, 2019 (ECAA Preliminary Report
  + Ethiopian Ministry of Transport press conference releasing
  FDR/CVR data showing crew execution of post-Lion-Air procedure).
  The interception was begun on November 28, 2018 (KNKT
  Preliminary Report), confirmed on October 25, 2019 (KNKT Final
  Report), and institutionally entrenched on September 16, 2020
  (House Committee T&I Final Report) and December 23, 2022 (ECAA
  Final Report).

Amplifiers (mechanisms that supported the interception but were not
load-bearing):
- The Seattle Times investigative series (Dominic Gates, Mike
  Baker, Steve Miletich, Lewis Kamb), beginning "Flawed analysis,
  failed oversight: How Boeing, FAA certified the suspect 737
  MAX flight control system" on March 17, 2019 — eight days
  after ET 302, two-and-a-half weeks before the ECAA press
  conference. The Seattle Times reporting documented the FAA
  ODA gaps and the System Safety Analysis understatement of
  MCAS authority by approximately a factor of four; it won the
  Pulitzer Prize for Investigative Reporting in 2020. The
  reporting was a powerful amplifier of the foreign-investigation
  findings but is not the load-bearing institutional interception
  — the KNKT and ECAA accident-investigation reports are.
- Sully Sullenberger's congressional testimony, June 19, 2019
  (House Aviation Subcommittee), which contested Boeing's
  "any competent crew would have recovered" framing on the
  authority of his own commercial-pilot experience.
- The grounding cascade: CAAC (March 11, 2019, immediately
  after ET 302), EASA and CAA-UK (March 12, 2019), and finally
  the FAA (March 13, 2019, twelve days after FAA had publicly
  affirmed airworthiness on March 11). The foreign regulators'
  refusal to wait for FAA cover is itself part of the
  jurisdictional-plurality lesson — but it is the accident
  investigation authorities (KNKT, ECAA) rather than the
  certification regulators whose factual findings did the
  load-bearing work.
- The DOJ Deferred Prosecution Agreement of January 7, 2021,
  which adopted the MCAS / AEG-deception framing in the
  Statement of Facts. By the time of the DPA, the climb of
  blame from pilots to certification was institutionally
  established; the DPA encoded what KNKT, ECAA, and the House
  Committee had already proven.

Cost of escape:
  Time from laundering to interception:
  Approximately five months from the first laundering attempt
  (Boeing's post-Lion-Air framing, November 2018) to the
  load-bearing interception (ECAA Preliminary Report, April 4,
  2019). One year and twenty-five days from the JT 610 crash
  (October 29, 2018) to the KNKT Final Report (October 25, 2019).
  Four years and two months from JT 610 to the ECAA Final Report
  (December 23, 2022). The interception was unusually fast for
  this pattern; most partial-scapegoat layers in industrial
  accidents persist for decades (Bhopal — over a decade before
  the parent-corporation question was substantively litigated).

  Reputational, financial, or career cost to the goat-candidates
  during the laundering:
  - Captain Bhavye Suneja and First Officer Harvino (Lion Air JT
    610) were posthumously characterised in Boeing-aligned trade-
    press coverage and in early FAA briefings as having failed
    to execute runaway-stabilizer procedure. Lion Air's
    institutional reputation absorbed substantial damage during
    the window between October 2018 and April 2019; the airline
    publicly disputed Boeing's framing and demanded the AOA
    Disagree alert be made standard equipment.
  - Captain Yared Getachew and First Officer Ahmed Nur Mohammed
    Omer (Ethiopian ET 302) faced the same posthumous framing
    in the early weeks; Ethiopian Airlines and the Ethiopian
    Ministry of Transport publicly rejected the "training
    deficit" framing.
  - Both airlines bore reputational cost during the months when
    Boeing's framing attributed the crashes to operator practice.
    Lion Air's prior EU blacklisting and Ethiopian's relatively
    young first officer were both deployed rhetorically against
    the operators in the pre-ECAA-Preliminary window.

  Cost absorbed by cost-bearers who could not wait:
  - 346 fatalities (189 + 157) across the two crashes. The
    second crash (ET 302) is the irreducible cost-of-delay:
    had the 737 MAX been grounded after Lion Air (November
    2018), ET 302 would not have flown on March 10, 2019. The
    decision not to ground the fleet between the crashes was
    made by named FAA officials in coordination with Boeing
    and is documented in the House Committee report; the
    laundering window between the two crashes is precisely
    where the cost-of-delay is concentrated.
  - The post-grounding loss to the global airline industry
    during the twenty-month grounding period (March 2019 –
    November 2020) ran into the tens of billions of dollars
    and was absorbed by 737 MAX operators worldwide.
  - 737 MAX program suppliers (Spirit AeroSystems and others)
    absorbed production-rate cost during the grounding.

  Cost to the intercepting actors:
  - KNKT and ECAA both faced sustained NTSB and Boeing
    technical pressure during their investigations to revise
    findings in directions more favorable to crew-error
    framing. The ECAA Final Report (December 2022) includes
    NTSB technical comments (filed under ICAO Annex 13 as
    state-of-design dissent) on specific procedural points;
    the ECAA's conclusions were maintained over those dissents.
  - The Seattle Times reporters faced sustained pushback from
    Boeing during the investigation period; the Pulitzer Prize
    (April 2020) was the institutional confirmation of the
    reporting's quality but came after the load-bearing
    interception.
  - The House Committee's investigation absorbed significant
    staff and document-production resources across eighteen
    months.

Outcome:
  Partial reversal at the responsibility-chain level; the
  laundering layer above the crew was successfully opened.
  (i) The crew-error framing collapsed institutionally within
  months of the second crash; Boeing's MCAS architecture
  decisions, certification practices, and pilot-training
  documentation choices became the named cause in the official
  US institutional record (House T&I Report, September 2020) and
  in the DOJ DPA (January 2021). (ii) The FAA ODA framework was
  reformed by the Aircraft Certification, Safety, and
  Accountability Act of 2020. (iii) Boeing paid $2.5 billion
  under the 2021 DPA and entered the 2025 NPA. The reversal is
  incomplete in documented respects: (a) the named Boeing 737
  Chief Technical Pilot prosecuted under the DPA conduct (Mark
  Forkner) was acquitted at trial in March 2022, leaving the
  individual-attribution layer un-adjudicated; (b) no Boeing
  executive or FAA manager has been criminally prosecuted; (c)
  the 2024 DPA breach and 2025 NPA created a renewed procedural
  shell — the case file at boeing-737-max.md classifies the
  case as system/object alibi at the certification-process layer
  precisely because the climb of blame from pilots to MCAS
  to certification did not fully reach the named individuals
  inside Boeing engineering and management. For ch-5 ("The
  Guilty Goat") purposes, the load-bearing finding is the
  climb above the crew layer — the pilots-blame partial
  scapegoat was pierced, even though the certification-process
  layer above it has its own laundering structure addressed
  at ch-7 / ch-2.

Transferable lesson (one sentence, diagnostic not motivational):
Institutional plurality across jurisdictions can break a single
jurisdiction's pilot-blame default — when the host state of the
accident is not the home state of the manufacturer, the host
state's accident-investigation authority, acting under ICAO
Annex 13 with the home state as accredited representative
rather than veto-holder, can force the climb of attribution
above the operator layer that the manufacturer's home
jurisdiction is structurally inclined to resist.

Limits of the lesson (where this counter-case does NOT generalise):
- The lesson generalises to laundering patterns where (a) the
  accident occurs in a sovereign jurisdiction other than the
  manufacturer's home jurisdiction, (b) an internationally
  recognised investigation framework (ICAO Annex 13, IMO MSC.255
  for maritime, etc.) gives the host state's investigation
  authority the right to publish factual findings even against
  accredited-representative dissent, (c) the host state's
  accident-investigation authority has the technical capacity
  and political independence to act on the framework, and (d)
  a second incident occurs that collapses the first incident's
  operator-error framing on its own factual record. The Boeing
  case had all four conditions in unusual measure: two crashes
  in two non-US jurisdictions, both ICAO Annex 13 compliant,
  with KNKT and ECAA each possessing the technical capacity
  to read FDR/CVR data and resist NTSB technical pressure, and
  the second crash's FDR record collapsing the runaway-
  stabilizer-procedure-was-sufficient framing.
- The Bhopal pattern (the chapter's primary anchor) had only
  one of these conditions — sovereign jurisdiction outside the
  manufacturer's home — and the international-investigation
  framework for industrial accidents is far weaker than ICAO
  Annex 13 for aviation. The Indian Supreme Court did
  eventually re-open the criminal case (AIR 1992 SC 248), but
  the corporate-parent question never received an institutional
  fact-finding equivalent to the KNKT or ECAA reports. The
  Boeing lesson is therefore narrower in transferability than
  the chapter's metaphor of "blame climbed" might suggest: it
  generalises cleanly within aviation and within domains with
  comparable international investigation regimes, but the
  Bhopal-shaped industrial-accident case lacks the
  jurisdictional infrastructure that made the Boeing
  interception fast.
- The lesson does not generalise to patterns where the
  manufacturer and the accident-investigation authority share
  a home jurisdiction (US-manufacturer / US-investigation
  accidents — the NTSB has no foreign counterpart to override
  its framing). The lesson does not generalise to patterns
  where the host state's accident-investigation authority is
  politically captured by the manufacturer's home state or by
  commercial pressure.
- The interception pierced the pilots-blame layer but did not
  pierce the certification-process layer that succeeded it.
  The chapter must say so: the climb from pilots to MCAS to
  certification was the load-bearing reversal, but the
  certification-process layer (boeing-737-max.md primary
  classification: system/object alibi) is itself laundered.
  This is a partial-scapegoat-pierced-by-foreign-authority case
  in which the layer above the partial scapegoat had its own
  alibi waiting.
- The interception was unusually fast (five months from
  laundering to load-bearing interception) compared with other
  partial-scapegoat-pierced cases (Bhopal, Sacco-Vanzetti,
  Horizon). The speed is attributable to the second-crash
  factual record collapsing the first-crash framing on its
  own terms; this combination of two same-pattern incidents
  in rapid sequence is rare and not transferable.

Best counterargument:
"The Boeing case is not a foreign-authority-resistance counter-
case because the FAA, NTSB, and DOJ — all US authorities —
ultimately adopted the MCAS / certification framing. Boeing was
prosecuted by the US, the House investigation was a US
institutional process, the ODA reform was US legislation. The
foreign accident-investigation authorities (KNKT, ECAA)
contributed factual findings but were not the load-bearing
institutional intercept. Framing this as 'foreign authorities
resisting US/Boeing pressure' overstates the foreign role and
under-states the role of US journalists (Seattle Times) and US
Congress (House T&I). A cleaner counter-case for the pilots-
blame partial-scapegoat pattern would be the Air France 447
investigation (BEA, France, 2009–2012), where the French
accident-investigation authority concluded that pitot-tube
icing and Airbus's automation behavior were causal alongside
the crew response — a single-jurisdiction case that did not
require foreign authority resistance."

Shirley's response: The counterargument is its strongest at the
post-2020 institutional level (US House report; US DOJ DPA) and
weakest at the November-2018-to-April-2019 window where the
interception was fought. Between Lion Air and Ethiopian, the
US-side regulator (FAA) and the US-side manufacturer (Boeing)
maintained that runaway-stabilizer procedure was sufficient;
the US-side accident-investigation authority (NTSB) participated
as accredited representative but does not produce host-state
findings under ICAO Annex 13. The foreign authorities (KNKT
preliminary, KNKT FDR release of November 28, 2018, and
crucially the ECAA Preliminary Report and FDR/CVR release of
April 4, 2019) produced the factual record that made the
pilot-error framing institutionally untenable. The US
institutions then converted the foreign factual findings into
US legal and legislative responses, but the conversion was
downstream of the foreign-authority factual establishment.

The Air France 447 alternative is technically correct but
teaches a different lesson — single-jurisdiction
accident-investigation independence — and lacks the
jurisdictional-plurality dimension that is the load-bearing
transferable lesson here. The Boeing case is selected for
ch-5 because the laundering attempt was structured around
the foreign-provenance of the operators ("Lion Air had been
EU-blacklisted"; "the Ethiopian first officer was
inexperienced"), and the foreign-authority interception was
the structural counter to that structuring. The chapter must
acknowledge the counter-argument's strength — that US
institutions ultimately did the prosecutorial and legislative
work — and frame the foreign-authority interception as the
factual-establishment phase that the US-institutional phase
depended on, not as a free-standing reversal that closed the
case.

Pattern match with Bhopal (ch-5 primary anchor):
Bhopal and Boeing share the partial-scapegoat structure: a
named individual or named group in the chain (Warren Anderson;
the Lion Air and Ethiopian crews) is publicly blamed, and the
attribution is deliberately stopped at that level to prevent
responsibility from climbing to the upstream design and
governance choices (UCC's parent-corporation design and cost-
cutting authority; Boeing's MCAS architecture and the FAA's
ODA framework). In both cases the laundering rested on
geographic distance — the disaster is "over there" (India for
Bhopal; Indonesia and Ethiopia for Boeing) and the
sophisticated upstream actor is "over here" (US for both
Anderson/UCC and Boeing/FAA). The counter-case pattern in both
is the persistence of the host-state institutions in refusing
to let the climb stop at the visible actor:

(i) Bhopal: the Indian Supreme Court's 1991 review judgment
    (AIR 1992 SC 248) reinstated criminal proceedings against
    UCC and Anderson and rejected the US-side framing that the
    1989 civil settlement extinguished criminal liability. The
    2010 CJM Bhopal convictions of seven UCIL officials further
    established that the disaster was not a one-employee
    sabotage event.

(ii) Boeing: the KNKT (October 2019) and ECAA (December 2022)
     final reports established that the crashes were caused by
     MCAS design and Boeing's certification documentation
     choices, not by crew procedural failure. The foreign-
     authority findings compelled the US institutional response.

The cases differ at the speed of interception (Bhopal took
seven years to the 1991 SC review; Boeing took five months to
the ECAA preliminary), at the institutional infrastructure
(no industrial-accident equivalent of ICAO Annex 13; aviation
has a stronger international regime), and at the climb-of-
attribution destination (Bhopal climb stopped at UCC and seven
UCIL officials; Boeing climb stopped at the corporation and
proceeded to a procedural-shell DPA-then-NPA disposition).
What is shared, and what the chapter installs as beat-9
lesson, is that the partial-scapegoat layer can be pierced
when an institutional actor with sufficient independence
refuses to defer to the manufacturer-friendly framing — even
if the layer above the pierced layer turns out to have its
own alibi.
