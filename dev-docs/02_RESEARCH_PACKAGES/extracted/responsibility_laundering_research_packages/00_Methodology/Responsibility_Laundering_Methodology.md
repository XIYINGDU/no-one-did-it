# Responsibility Laundering Research Methodology

## Core definition
Responsibility laundering is the process by which an actor, institution, or system preserves control, benefit, or strategic discretion while moving blame, liability, moral burden, evidentiary risk, or public outrage onto a substitute: a person, class, contractor, proxy, bureaucracy, machine, dataset, model, legal entity, or procedure.

## Four case categories

1. **Pure scapegoat** — a largely innocent or unrelated person/group is blamed to close a crisis.
2. **Partial scapegoat** — the visible actor is directly involved or culpable at one level, but blame stops too low and protects higher control.
3. **System/object alibi** — a machine, model, file, market, procedure, benchmark, database, or legal entity is made to carry responsibility.
4. **Cost-bearing goat** — a person/group is not necessarily accused of wrongdoing, but is forced to absorb harm while responsibility is moved elsewhere.

## Main diagnostic
Do not start with the visible actor. Start with the responsibility vector:

- Who wanted the outcome?
- Who designed the system?
- Who selected the agent, proxy, contractor, model, metric, or procedure?
- Who controlled the records?
- Who had authority to stop the action or prevent recurrence?
- Who benefited?
- Who bore the cost?
- Who can compensate the victims?

**Working rule:** responsibility belongs where control, benefit, knowledge, and preventability overlap.

## Evidence grades

- **A:** court decision, official inquiry, inspector general, GAO, UN/ICC body, primary legal record, company admission, or primary document.
- **B:** major NGO investigation, reputable investigative journalism, credible technical/forensic analysis, well-sourced scholarly paper.
- **C:** active, contested, incomplete, under appeal, or politically disputed. Treat as a lead, not a final claim.

## Writing discipline

- Do not turn every bad outcome into responsibility laundering. The test is whether responsibility was moved away from control, benefit, knowledge, or preventability.
- Do not claim legal guilt unless a reliable legal source establishes it.
- Do not confuse distributed responsibility with vanished responsibility. Some systems genuinely involve many actors; the task is to reconstruct the chain.
- Always include the best counterargument.
- Be especially careful with active wars, live litigation, and current AI competition.
